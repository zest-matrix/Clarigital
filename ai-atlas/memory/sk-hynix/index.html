SK hynix: The Complete Guide

The company that got to HBM first and built a business on it. What SK hynix makes, the NVIDIA agreements in both companies’ own words, the Nasdaq listing nobody expected, and where its own sources contradict each other. Three reading levels, every number sourced.

1.At a glance

SimpleStart here

SK hynix is the world's second-largest memory maker and the company that developed HBM, the stacked memory every AI accelerator now needs 1. It makes DRAM, the fast working memory, and NAND flash, the storage that survives a power cut 2.

What it makes"Memory semiconductor: DRAM, NAND Flash, MCP (Multi-Chip Package), etc." 2
How bigKRW 79.3 trillion revenue in Q2 2026, an all-time quarterly high 3
The AI positionHBM4 in mass shipment since Q2 2026, with a multi-year NVIDIA partnership 3 4
Can you buy direct?Not the chips. SK hynix publishes no memory chip prices 5
DeeperThe detail

DRAM is the bulk of it. SK hynix's own prospectus states that "Our DRAM products accounted for 77.3% of our total sales in the first quarter of 2026 and 77.1% in 2025 and our NAND flash memory products accounted for 22.0% of our total sales in the first quarter of 2026 and 21.3% in 2025" 6. In July 2026 the company listed in the United States, raising gross proceeds of US$26.507 billion 6.

ExpertFor specialists

The Q2 2026 figures are extraordinary enough to look like errors, and are not: revenue of KRW 79.3187 trillion, operating profit of KRW 60.5426 trillion "with an operating margin of 76%", and net profit of KRW 93.9226 trillion "with a net margin of 118%" 3, the same numbers filed with the SEC as provisional, unaudited K-IFRS figures 7. Net profit exceeding revenue is real and reported; neither the release nor the filing explains what produced it 3 7, and this page does not guess.

The July 2026 US listing was substantial: 177,900,000 American Depositary Shares at US$149.00 each, where "each ADS represents one-tenth of a share", for gross proceeds of US$26.507 billion, settling 14 July 2026 6. The size was not chosen freely — the prospectus explains it "was determined taking into account the requirement under the Monopoly Regulation and Fair Trade Act that SK square Co., Ltd. ExpertFor specialists

Two disclosures from the prospectus are worth more than the headline numbers. Customer concentration: "Our two largest customers represented 14.8% and 12.4%, respectively, of our total revenue in the first quarter of 2026 and our largest customer represented 23.9% of our total revenue in 2025" 6 — and the prospectus does not name them. And the listing created a second stream of SK hynix disclosure: the company now files with the US SEC as well as in Korea, including the Form 6-K carrying its quarterly figures 7 6.

3.The story

SimpleStart here
  • February 1983 "Established Hyundai Electronics Co., Ltd" 1.
  • 1985 "Started mass production system of 256K DRAM" 1.
  • 1999 "Took over large stockholder's share of LG Semiconductor" 1.
  • 2001 "Changed the Company name to 'Hynix Semiconductor Inc.'" 1.
  • 2013 "Developed the World's First TSV-based HBM" 1.
  • 2021 "Closed the first deal of the acquisition of Intel NAND business" 1.
  • 2026 Nasdaq listing; HBM4 mass shipments begin 6 3.
DeeperThe detail

The company is older than its name. It began as Hyundai Electronics in 1983, absorbed LG Semiconductor in 1999, became Hynix in 2001, and only later joined SK Group 1. Its HBM lineage runs the whole way through: HBM2 "released in 2018", HBM2E in 2020 where "SK hynix was also the first memory vendor to begin mass producing HBM2E", and HBM3 developed October 2021 into mass production in June 2022 10.

ExpertFor specialists

SK hynix's own sources disagree about its most important date. The history page records "December 2013 — Developed the World's First TSV-based HBM" 1; the company's own HBM history article says SK hynix "jointly developed the world's first TSV (Through Silicon Via) HBM product with AMD in 2014" 10; and its 41st-anniversary piece dates the start of development to 2009 and says "four years later" the first-generation HBM arrived, pointing back at 2013 11. The honest reading is that development completed in 2013 and the first product was co-developed with AMD — a partner the history page's sole-first framing leaves out 1 10.

DeeperThe detail

SK hynix showed its full current range at its own DTF 2026 conference in Seoul: a "256GB 3DS RDIMM" it calls "industry's largest capacity", a 128GB MRDIMM for HPC, a 64GB RDIMM on the 1c node, a 192GB SOCAMM2 built on LPDDR5X, and a second-generation CMM-DDR5 CXL memory module 14. Note that MRDIMM capacity is quoted as 96GB at MWC in March 2026 and 128GB at DTF in August 2026 15 14 — these are different points in a moving line, not a contradiction, and this page dates each.

ExpertFor specialists

The mobile and automotive side is broader than the AI coverage suggests. The MWC 2026 lineup includes LPDDR6, UFS 4.1 at 1TB, uMCP 4.1 combining "16GB LPDDR5X + 512GB UFS", ZUFS 4.1, plus automotive LPDDR6 on the 1c node, automotive LPDDR5/5X, Auto UFS 3.1, Auto eMMC 5.1 and the PA101/PA201 automotive SSDs 15. LPDDR6 is listed as a product in SK hynix's own material, and for the automotive part the process is stated: automotive LPDDR6 and Auto/Robotics LPDDR5/5X are "both built on the 10-nanometer-class sixth-generation (1c) process technology" 15. What this check did not find in any SK hynix release is a speed figure or a shipping date for LPDDR6, so neither is stated here.

5.How it’s made

SimpleStart here

SK hynix runs its own fabs and names its DRAM processes by generation of the 10nm class.

  1. DRAM: the current leading node is the "1c node, 6th generation of 10nm process" 17.
  2. HBM4: built on "the 1bnm process, or the fifth-generation of the 10-nanometer technology", with Advanced MR-MUF packaging 18.
  3. NAND: the current generation is "321-high 4D NAND Flash", in mass production since November 2024 19.
DeeperThe detail

SK hynix's node names are unusually well defined in its own words: the "1c node, 6th generation of 10nm process" 17, and HBM4 built on "the 1bnm process, or the fifth-generation of the 10-nanometer technology, to minimize the risk" 18. Between them those two releases fix the whole 1a→1b→1c ladder as fourth, fifth and sixth generation 10nm. The company states where it intends 1c to go: "applying the 1c technology equipped with the best performance and cost competitiveness to our major next-generation products including HBM, LPDDR6, and GDDR7" 17.

ExpertFor specialists

On NAND the official vocabulary differs from common usage. SK hynix's term is "321-high 4D NAND Flash", triple-level-cell, 1Tb 19 — not "321-layer", although its own Q2 2026 results page does use "321-layer products" 3. The enabling technique is what it calls "Three Plugs" process technology, which allows stacking over 300 layers, and the generation delivers a 59% productivity gain over its predecessor 19. Packaging is the other half of the story: SK hynix credits Advanced MR-MUF with "10% higher heat dissipation performance compared to the previous generation" and better warpage control on its 12-layer HBM3E 13.

SK hynix's SEC prospectus gives the cleanest single statement of where it makes things:

"We own and operate wafer fabrication facilities ('fabs') in Icheon and Cheongju, Korea and Wuxi and Dalian, China. We also own and operate assembly and testing facilities for back-end processing of our products in Icheon and Cheongju, Korea and Chongqing, China" 6.

ExpertFor specialists

The build-out is enormous and dated. M15X in Cheongju took KRW 5.3 trillion for construction against "more than 20 trillion won" long-term, for next-generation DRAM including HBM 9. In August 2026 SK hynix committed a further KRW 54 trillion: KRW 35.2 trillion for Yongin Y2, for "high-bandwidth memory (HBM) and other next-generation DRAM products", breaking ground July 2027 with cleanroom in June 2029; and KRW 19.1 trillion for Cheongju M17 for NAND, breaking ground February 2027 23. Indiana is "over $4 billion" for HBM advanced packaging, with cleanroom by October 2028 and mass production in H2 2029, alongside a Purdue University research testbed 22 24.

Two cautions. The Indiana figures moved: the July 2026 prospectus expected operations in H2 2028 6, where the August 2026 groundbreaking release says mass production H2 2029 22 — this page uses the later, more specific official figure and dates it. And China carries a named regulatory risk: "On August 29, 2025, the BIS announced that our designation as a VEU would be revoked effective December 31, 2025", replaced by "an annual approval mechanism", where "Failure to obtain the required licenses in a timely manner may materially impact our manufacturing operations in China" 6.

7.How the memory works

SimpleStart here

DRAM holds each bit as a charge that leaks away, so it is rewritten constantly and forgets everything when the power goes. NAND traps charge in a cell that keeps it without power, which is why files survive a reboot. SK hynix makes both 2.

HBM is DRAM rearranged: dies stacked vertically and wired straight through, placed beside the processor instead of across the board 10.

DeeperThe detail

The wiring is what makes HBM different, and SK hynix's own numbers show it. Its HBM4 has "2,048 I/O terminals", double the previous generation's, which is how bandwidth doubles without raising the speed of each individual connection to something unmanageable 18. The stacking technique matters as much as the die: SK hynix's Advanced MR-MUF is what holds a 12-layer stack flat and gets heat out of it 13.

ExpertFor specialists

SK hynix's HBM4 deliberately exceeds the standard it implements. JEDEC's JESD270-4 sets 8 Gb/s per pin across a 2,048-bit interface, 32 channels per stack, up to 2 TB/s and a 64GB maximum 25; SK hynix's part runs at "over 10Gbps", which it states explicitly as being above the "JEDEC standard operating speed(8Gbps)", with power efficiency improved "more than 40%" and up to 69% better AI service performance 18. Note how the company frames the achievement: Justin Kim's quote is "We are unveiling the establishment of the world's first mass production system of HBM4" 18 — a claim about readiness to produce, not about having shipped first.

DeeperThe detail

Two of these deserve their context. The 1c DDR5's "more than 9%" power gain sounds small until SK hynix converts it: the company says it can "help data centers reduce the electricity cost by as much as 30%" 17. And GDDR7's headline is a range, not a number — 32 Gbps operating speed, where "the speed can grow up to 40Gbps depending on the circumstances", with thermal resistance cut 74% 26.

ExpertFor specialists

SK hynix's HBM4E figures are a straight generational delta on HBM4, stated against its own previous part rather than a competitor's: 48GB at up to 16 Gbps, power efficiency improved over 20%, and "improved heat resistance by 17 percent, compared to the preceding HBM4" 12. Notably, the HBM4E release makes no "industry first" claim at all 12, where the HBM4 and HBM3E releases both do 18 13 — a small but deliberate change in how the company is describing its own lead.

9.Official pricing

SimpleStart here

SK hynix publishes no price for any memory chip. DRAM, NAND and HBM are sold on negotiated contracts, not from a list. The only SK hynix prices in public are for finished consumer drives 5.

DeeperThe detail

Its consumer SSD site lists the Gold P31 at $309.99 for 2TB, $166.99 for 1TB and $79.99 for 500GB, an M.2 2280 PCIe 3.0 drive using SK hynix's own 128-layer 3D TLC, rated up to 3,500 MB/s read and 3,200 MB/s write with a five-year warranty 5. There is no checkout — the site offers a store locator only 5. These are launch prices on an older PCIe 3.0 product and should be read as such, not as a current market rate.

ExpertFor specialists

What SK hynix does publish, in place of prices, is the demand picture. Its own 2026 outlook post describes an "HBM-led memory supercycle" and republishes third-party forecasts — a $54.6 billion HBM market in 2026 from BofA, and custom-ASIC HBM demand growth from Goldman Sachs 27. Those are analyst numbers carried on an SK hynix page, not SK hynix forecasts, and this page attributes them to the houses that made them 27.

10.The HBM position

SimpleStart here

HBM is the whole story at SK hynix. The company developed the first one in 2013 1, and as of this check its HBM4 began mass shipments in the second quarter of 2026 3.

DeeperThe detail

The sequence in SK hynix's own words: in September 2025 it announced it had "completed development and finished preparation of HBM4 ... mass production for the world's first time" 18; in July 2026 it reported that "The company began mass shipments of HBM4 in the second quarter", describing it as "achieving customer-required operating speeds while delivering industry-leading power efficiency and cost competitiveness" 3. HBM4E samples went to customers in June 2026, with mass production promised only "in a timely manner" 12.

The NVIDIA relationship is confirmed by both companies. In June 2026 they announced a multi-year technology partnership covering "NVIDIA Vera Rubin AI supercomputers, Vera CPUs, RTX Spark-powered PCs, Jetson Thor robotics", with SK Group chairman Chey Tae-won saying "SK hynix and NVIDIA have been building toward this for years" 4.

ExpertFor specialists

In July 2026 the relationship widened: "SK Group and NVIDIA today announced plans for a $500-billion-plus comprehensive partnership to establish AI infrastructure", within which "SK hynix is also entering into a long-term AI memory partnership with NVIDIA" that "allows NVIDIA to secure a stable supply of next-generation AI memory" 28 29. Read that figure precisely: the $500 billion is the SK Group–NVIDIA AI-infrastructure envelope, not the value of an SK hynix memory contract. No memory contract value is disclosed by either company 28.

Equally, SK hynix has never stated its share of NVIDIA's HBM4. Its own market-outlook page carries a 70% figure but attributes it to UBS: "UBS predicts that SK hynix will achieve approximately a 70% market share in the HBM4 market for NVIDIA's next-generation Rubin platform in 2026" 27. That is an analyst forecast republished by SK hynix, not an SK hynix claim, and this page does not convert it into one.

11.Who buys it and why

SimpleStart here

SK hynix sells to the companies that build servers, phones, PCs, cars and AI systems. Its best-known customer relationship is with NVIDIA, confirmed by both companies 4 28.

DeeperThe detail

Revenue is concentrated. SK hynix's prospectus states its "largest customer represented 23.9% of our total revenue in 2025", with the two largest at 14.8% and 12.4% in Q1 2026 6 — roughly a quarter of the company's income from one buyer in 2025. The prospectus does not name them 6, so while NVIDIA is a confirmed partner 4, no official source found here identifies it as the largest customer, and this page does not assert that.

ExpertFor specialists

The July 2026 SK Group–NVIDIA announcement points at a customer relationship becoming an infrastructure one: it includes an SK Telecom "2-gigawatt-scale AI Cloud in Korea" built on NVIDIA systems and "powered by SK hynix HBM4", with a first AI factory planned for 2027 28. On the server side, SK hynix showed a "256GB 3DS RDIMM, which achieves the industry's largest capacity" at its own 2026 conference 14, and its framing of the strategy is broad: Jungdal Choi described the 321-high NAND as putting SK hynix "on track to advancing to the Full Stack AI Memory Provider" 19.

12.Hidden in plain sight

SimpleStart here

SK hynix listed on Nasdaq in July 2026, raising gross proceeds of US$26.507 billion 6 — one of the largest listings ever by a non-US company. Any reference work still describing SK hynix as listed only in Korea is out of date, and most were written before July 2026.

DeeperThe detail

The second thing hidden in plain sight is in the history page. SK hynix records "December 2013 — Developed the World's First TSV-based HBM" as a solo achievement 1, while its own longer HBM article says the first product was "jointly developed ... with AMD in 2014" 10. The company that is now the reference point for AI memory got there with a GPU partner, and its official timeline does not mention it.

ExpertFor specialists

A third concerns the Intel NAND deal, and it is a gap in announcement rather than in substance. SK hynix marked the first closing in December 2021 with its own release, calling it "a paradigm shifting moment for SK hynix's NAND flash business to enter the global top tier level" and predicting Solidigm would be "the world's next big semiconductor company" 30. For the final closing, this check found no SK hynix release at all: the primary record is Intel's SEC filing, which states "On March 27, 2025, the second closing of the transaction occurred. The consideration received by Intel at the second closing of the transaction was approximately $1.9 billion, net of certain adjustments" 31. The deal was contracted at US$9 billion total — US$7 billion at first closing plus US$2 billion at final 21 — so the actual total came in slightly under the headline figure, and the only primary record of that is the counterparty's filing 31.

13.Weak spots

SimpleStart here
  • China exposure with a named risk. Its VEU designation was revoked effective 31 December 2025, replaced by annual licence approvals 6.
  • Customer concentration. One customer was 23.9% of 2025 revenue 6.
  • Its own sources disagree on when and with whom it made the first HBM 1 10.
DeeperThe detail

The China risk is stated by SK hynix itself in the plainest terms available: "In lieu of the VEU framework, the BIS implemented an annual approval mechanism under which we are required to submit yearly plans detailing our anticipated needs for U.S.-origin equipment", and "Failure to obtain the required licenses in a timely manner may materially impact our manufacturing operations in China" 6. SK hynix operates two fabs and an assembly site in China 6, and publishes no product breakdown for any of them 8.

ExpertFor specialists

The disclosure gaps are narrower than Samsung's but real. SK hynix publishes no employee count in any source checked here; no exact SK square shareholding beyond the 20% statutory floor 6; no explanation of how Q2 2026 net profit exceeded revenue 3 7; and no LPDDR6 release giving specifications or timing despite listing the product 15. There is also no SK hynix announcement of the Solidigm final closing 31. Each is a case where the widely-quoted figure comes from somewhere other than the company.

14.India angle

SimpleStart here

SK hynix has no fab and no announced R&D centre in India. Its official global network lists exactly one Indian entity: "SK hynix Semiconductor India Pvt. Ltd.", at "Unit No. 3D, Uppals Plaza M6, 3rd Floor, Plot No. 6 District Centre, Jasola, New Delhi, INDIA (110025)" 8 — an office, not a plant.

DeeperThe detail

This is a clean negative and worth stating as one. No SK hynix source found in this check announces manufacturing, assembly, test or research investment in India 8. Where a company has published nothing, this page says so rather than reaching for a press report to fill the space.

ExpertFor specialists

For contrast, SK hynix's actual overseas manufacturing expansion is in the United States, not India: the West Lafayette, Indiana advanced packaging plant, over $4 billion, cleanroom by October 2028, mass production H2 2029, with around 7,000 direct and indirect jobs and a Purdue University research partnership 22 24. Ambassador Kang Kyung-wha described it at the groundbreaking as "the establishment of the first AI memory production facility and R&D center in the United States" 22.

15.What’s next

SimpleStart here

Only what SK hynix has announced:

  • HBM4E: samples shipped; mass production "in a timely manner", no date given 12.
  • Yongin Y2: KRW 35.2 trillion, groundbreaking July 2027, cleanroom June 2029 23.
  • Cheongju M17: KRW 19.1 trillion for NAND, groundbreaking February 2027 23.
  • Indiana: cleanroom October 2028, mass production H2 2029 22.
DeeperThe detail

SK hynix explains the KRW 54 trillion commitment as timing rather than ambition: "This investment is a decision made to seize opportunities in line with the market's growth speed. By proactively securing production capabilities, we aim to establish ourselves as a key partner in AI infrastructure, contributing to the stability of the global AI semiconductor supply chain" 23. The quote is given by "an SK hynix official" and is not attributed to a named person in the release 23.

ExpertFor specialists

On NAND the near-term plan is stated numerically: 321-high "products already represent the largest share of total production, and the company plans to expand this to approximately 50% of domestic production capacity by the end of the year" 3. The open questions are the ones the company has not answered: no HBM4E production date 12, no disclosed memory contract value inside the SK Group–NVIDIA partnership 28, and an annual US licence approval to obtain each year for the Chinese fabs 6.
