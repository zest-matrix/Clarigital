SanDisk: The Complete Guide

A company with a famous name, spun out of Western Digital in 2025 and legally new. It owns no wafer fab, buys half of a Japanese joint venture’s output, and is trying to build a flash answer to HBM. Three reading levels, every number sourced.

SanDisk 16 sections · 3 levels 35 linked sources Checked September 2026
How to read this page. Every maker in our Memory section uses the same 16 sections in the same order, the same shape as our AI Chips brand guides. Each section starts Simple, then goes Deeper, then Expert: stop wherever you have what you need. The small numbers are sources: click one to open the original document. Where a company has not published something, we say so rather than estimate.

1.At a glance

SimpleStart here

SanDisk makes NAND flash memory and the drives and cards built from it 1. The name is old and famous; the company is not — this SanDisk was created in 2025 when Western Digital split itself in two, sending the flash business one way and the hard-drive business the other 2.

What it makesNAND flash, SSDs, memory cards and USB drives 1
How big"Fiscal year 2026 revenue was $20.25 billion, up 175% year-over-year" 3
Who makes itNobody at SanDisk — "Substantially all of our flash-based memory is obtained from our joint ventures with Kioxia" 4
The AI betHigh Bandwidth Flash, being standardised with SK hynix 5
DeeperThe detail

The fiscal calendar matters for reading any of these numbers: "The Company's fiscal year ends on the Friday nearest to June 30 and typically consists of 52 weeks", and fiscal 2026 was a 53-week year ending 3 July 2026 6 1. On that basis FY2025 revenue was $7.355 billion 7 and FY2026 was $20.25 billion 3 — close to a tripling in one year, on the same memory upcycle running through every company in this section.

ExpertFor specialists

The fourth quarter alone shows the slope: "Fiscal fourth quarter revenue was $8.97 billion, up 51% sequentially", with "GAAP net income reported at $6.90 billion ($43.97 diluted net income per share)" and a gross margin of 84.6% 3. The shift in what SanDisk sells is as striking as the total — it renamed its end markets between years, and on the new basis FY2026 Datacenter revenue was $5,153 million against $960 million in FY2025, which SanDisk describes as "Datacenter up 437%" 3. Alongside the results it announced "an additional $14 billion buyback program, bringing total remaining authorization to $15.5 billion" 3.

DeeperThe detail

SanDisk joined the S&P 500 in November 2025, effective "prior to the opening of trading on Friday, November 28" 8 — nine months after listing, and a step up from the S&P SmallCap 600 it had entered at the spin. Its workforce is concentrated where the manufacturing is: FY2025 gave the split as "73% in Asia Pacific, 19% in the Americas, and 8% in Europe, the Middle East, and Africa" 2.

ExpertFor specialists

One legal detail shapes how the rest of this page reads. The S-1 states that "Sandisk Corporation was incorporated in Delaware for the purpose of holding WDC's Flash Business in connection with the separation" 9. This is a new registrant, with its own SEC identity, distinct from the SanDisk Corporation that Western Digital bought in 2016 10. Its filings do not claim the older company's founding date; the FY2025 annual report goes no further than "We have a rich heritage of innovation and operational excellence" 2. See The story, below, for what is and is not documented about that heritage.

3.The story

SimpleStart here
  • 1988 The original SanDisk Corporation is "incorporated in the State of Delaware in June 1988" 10.
  • 2016 Western Digital completes its acquisition of that company, 12 May 11.
  • 2023 Western Digital announces it will split into two companies, 30 October 12.
  • 2025 The separation takes legal effect on 21 February; the listing is announced on 24 February 13 14.
  • 2025 Joins the S&P 500 in November 8.
  • 2026 First HBF specification published through the Open Compute Project 15.
DeeperThe detail

Western Digital's stated reason for splitting was that the two businesses had stopped belonging together: its "Board of Directors has unanimously approved a plan to separate its HDD and Flash businesses", to be done in a tax-free manner, citing market-specific focus, distinct capital structures and strategic optionality 12. The mechanics: shareholders received "one share of our common stock for every three shares of WDC common stock held" 9, and Western Digital kept a stake — "WDC retained 28,827,787 shares of our common stock representing its 19.9% retained interest" 9. It has since been unwinding that position, exchanging SanDisk shares for its own stock and retaining 1,038,681 SanDisk shares after a May 2026 settlement 16.

ExpertFor specialists

Two dates circulate for the separation and both are right, which is worth stating rather than choosing between. The legal distribution was 21 February 2025 — the annual report says "As of February 21, 2025, we separated from WDC" 2, and Western Digital's 8-K times it to "February 21, 2025 at 11:59 p.m. Pacific time" against a record date of 12 February 13. The public completion and Nasdaq listing were announced on 24 February, which is the date SanDisk's own FAQ uses: "On February 24th, 2025, Sandisk officially completed its separation from Western Digital as an independent, publicly traded company" 17 18.

On the pre-2016 heritage, this page is deliberately careful. The 1988 incorporation is documented in the old company's own filings 10, which also record that it had "successfully developed and commercialized 11 generations of NAND" 10. But no current SanDisk page found in this check names the founders or claims the 1988 founding as its own, so this page does not attribute either to today's company.

DeeperThe detail

The brands are genuinely confusing, and the confusion is real rather than sloppy reporting. SanDisk's own FAQ sets out that its flash products are sold under "Western Digital®, WD®, WD_BLACK®, SanDisk®, SanDisk Professional®, and SANDISK® brands", while Western Digital's hard drives use "Western Digital®, WD®, WD_BLACK®, SanDisk Professional®, and G-Technology® brands" 17. Both companies use WD_BLACK and SanDisk Professional — split by product type, not by company. WD-branded flash drives are "manufactured by Sandisk Technologies Inc. or its affiliates and distributed under license from Western Digital" 17.

SanDisk is now unwinding that. From January 2026 it is retiring the borrowed names: "SANDISK Optimus™" replaces WD Blue®, and "SANDISK Optimus™ GX" and "GX PRO" replace WD_BLACK™, with retail rollout in the first half of 2026 24.

ExpertFor specialists

On the flash itself, SanDisk and Kioxia ship the same silicon under different names, because they jointly develop and jointly own the output. BiCS10 is SanDisk's label for the generation Kioxia calls its tenth: "332 memory layers", die density "greater than 29Gb/mm²", "Up to 4.8Gb/s NAND interface speed, a 33 percent improvement", and bit density improved "by 59 percent" over the eighth generation 19. The QLC variant reaches ">37 Gb/mm²" 20. Both are built with "CMOS directly Bonded to Array (CBA) technology", where logic and memory arrays are fabricated on separate wafers and then bonded 19.

One node in the range is a hybrid worth noting: SanDisk describes a BiCS9 QLC part combining "a proven BiCS8 array with a BiCS10-based CMOS wafer" 26 — possible precisely because CBA separates the two wafers, so the generations can be mixed.

5.How it’s made

SimpleStart here

SanDisk does not manufacture its own flash. Its 10-K states it plainly: "Substantially all of our flash-based memory is obtained from our joint ventures with Kioxia" 4.

  1. The wafers are made at Kioxia's plants in Japan, through Flash Ventures 1.
  2. SanDisk is entitled to about half that output 4.
  3. It then does its own assembly and test, mostly in Penang, Malaysia 4.
DeeperThe detail

Flash Ventures is three companies, not one: "We and Kioxia currently operate three business ventures, Flash Partners Ltd., Flash Alliance Ltd. and Flash Forward Ltd., (collectively, 'Flash Ventures')", in each of which "We hold a 49.9% ownership position" 4. The output and the pricing are both defined: "We and Kioxia are entitled to purchase a share of Flash Ventures' output, which generally equals 50% each", at a price that "is cost plus a small markup" 4 1.

That arrangement was extended in January 2026 from its original expiry of 31 December 2029 to 31 December 2034, with SanDisk paying "Kioxia USD 1.165 billion for manufacturing services and continued availability of supply", in cash payments "made in installments over the years 2026 to 2029" 27. Goeckeler's framing: "The Kioxia-Sandisk JV continues to be a thriving collaboration across NAND R&D and manufacturing that has consistently delivered the highest performing, lowest cost NAND technology to both companies" 27.

ExpertFor specialists

The manufacturing base grew by one fab between SanDisk's first two annual reports, which is a clean way to see the joint venture expanding. FY2025 described "seven flash-based manufacturing facilities in Japan, six of which are located in Yokkaichi, Japan and one of which is located in Kitakami, Japan", and said an eighth would begin operations in calendar 2025 4; FY2026 describes "eight flash-based manufacturing facilities in Japan, six of which are located in Yokkaichi, Japan and two of which are located in Kitakami, Japan" 1. The eighth is Kitakami Fab2, which began operation in September 2025 to produce 218-layer BiCS8 with CBA, with "meaningful output expected to begin in the first half of 2026" 21.

SanDisk's own facilities are back-end only. Its 10-K describes assembly and test as comprising "in-house assembly and test facilities located in Penang, Malaysia, facilities operated by other contract manufacturers, and the assembly and test facility owned and operated by SDSS" 1 — and even that last one is mostly someone else's, being "a venture that is owned 20% by Sandisk and 80% by JCET Management Co., Ltd." 1.

7.How the memory works

SimpleStart here

NAND flash stores each bit as trapped charge in a cell that holds it without power. Stacking those cells vertically is how capacity keeps growing — BiCS10 is 332 layers tall 19.

SanDisk's flash is built with CBA: the control logic and the memory array are made on two separate wafers and then bonded together, so each can be optimised on its own terms 19.

DeeperThe detail

Cells can hold more than one bit by storing several distinguishable charge levels. TLC holds three bits per cell and is the mainstream product; QLC holds four, trading endurance and speed for capacity 20. SanDisk's enterprise UltraQLC platform is built specifically around that trade, using the "BiCS8 2Tb QLC die" to reach 128TB and 256TB drives 22.

ExpertFor specialists

The most interesting thing SanDisk is building is not a denser drive but a different place for flash to sit. High Bandwidth Flash puts NAND in the position HBM occupies — stacked beside the processor — on the argument that AI inference needs capacity more than it needs DRAM's write endurance. SanDisk's own technical write-up describes "512GB total capacity per 16-die stack", matching "HBM4's physical footprint, power profile, and stack height", built on "CBA technology (CMOS directly Bonded to Array)" with "ultra-low warpage designs" 30. Its headline claim is bandwidth parity at far greater capacity: HBF is designed to deliver "comparable bandwidth to High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) while delivering up to 8-16x the capacity of HBM at a similar cost" 25. See The HBM position for where that stands.

DeeperThe detail

BiCS10's power figures are given separately for the two directions, which is more precise than the usual single number: input power down 10%, output power down 34% against BiCS8 19. The consumer NAS drives are a rare case where SanDisk publishes endurance as well as speed — the NAS 600 SATA drive is rated at "up to 2,500 TBW (4TB model)" — the figure is for the largest capacity, not the range 31.

ExpertFor specialists

The HBF specification is the most detailed public document SanDisk is party to, and it is worth reading as a standard rather than a press release. Published through the Open Compute Project as the "High Bandwidth Flash (HBF) High-Level Base Die Specification", v0.7.0, it defines a maximum of 16 NAND dies per cube for "512 GiB" total, and three performance grades — "0.384 TB/s" at 8 GT/s, "1.536 TB/s" at 16 GT/s and "3.072 TB/s" at 32 GT/s, all on a x64 interface, over "Universal Chiplet Interconnect Express (UCIe) architecture" 32. It describes HBF as "a non-coherent memory-centric flash device" addressing "the memory wall problem", delivering "8x to 16x of the capacity of HBM, while delivering the same read bandwidth" 32. SK hynix's own account of the same specification gives the range as "approximately 0.4TB/s to 3.0TB/s" and positions HBF as "a new memory layer between HBM and SSDs" 33.

A practical note for anyone trying to check current retail prices: sandisk.com does sell direct, but its product pages render prices in the browser rather than in the page source, so they cannot be cited from a fetched document. The one internal price SanDisk does disclose is the one it pays. That mix shift is what its Investor Day targets rest on: a FY2028–FY2030 model of "approximately 80 percent" non-GAAP gross margin and "approximately 75 percent" non-GAAP operating margin 26.

10.The HBM position

SimpleStart here

SanDisk makes no DRAM and no HBM. Its answer to the AI memory problem is High Bandwidth Flash (HBF) — flash arranged like HBM, stacked beside the processor, trading DRAM's speed for far more capacity 25.

DeeperThe detail

The project began as an agreement with a DRAM maker, which is the interesting part: in August 2025 SanDisk and SK hynix "signed a Memorandum of Understanding to establish specifications for High Bandwidth Flash technology", committing to "standardize the specification, define technology requirements and explore the creation of a technology ecosystem for High Bandwidth Flash" 25. SanDisk set out a timeline at the same time: first HBF memory samples in the second half of calendar 2026, and initial AI-inference devices with HBF in early 2027 25.

It has moved on schedule so far. A Technical Advisory Board was formed in July 2025, led by David Patterson, "Pardee Professor of Computer Science, Emeritus at the University of California at Berkeley and a Google distinguished engineer" 34. In February 2026 the two companies established "a dedicated workstream under the Open Compute Project (OCP)" 5, and in August 2026 they released the first technical specification through OCP, "just six months after the consortium began work in February", with "Google and Tenstorrent joined as consortium members during this standardization process" 15.

ExpertFor specialists

What the specification actually covers is broader than a memory part: "system interface, electrical and other technical guidelines for designing systems that interact with and use HBF technology, including basic performance expectations, the xPU-HBF host interface, reliability and packaging guidance for an HBF die stack, and a software user guide for read and write operations", explicitly enabling HBF "to coexist with High Bandwidth Memory" 15. That last phrase is the strategic claim — HBF is pitched as an additional tier beside HBM, not a replacement for it, which is also how SK hynix describes it: "a new memory layer between HBM and SSDs" 33.

The evidence SanDisk offers is a simulation rather than a measurement, and the distinction matters: its technical blog states that "HBF's simulated performance on a Llama 3.1 405B parameter model for reading pretrained weights is within a 2.2% delta of a hypothetically unlimited-capacity HBM" 30. Both hedges are Sandisk's own — simulated, and against a hypothetical part rather than a real one. As of this check the specification exists and samples were targeted for the second half of 2026 25; no shipping HBF product is announced. Note also that HBF is a SanDisk and SK hynix effort — Kioxia, SanDisk's manufacturing partner in everything else, is not a party to it 5.

11.Who buys it and why

SimpleStart here

SanDisk reports its sales in three end markets, which it renamed in 2026: Datacenter, Edge and Consumer 3. Datacenter is the one growing fastest by a wide margin.

DeeperThe detail

The FY2026 figures show the rebalancing: Datacenter $5,153 million against $960 million the previous year, Edge $12,160 million against $4,127 million, and Consumer $2,935 million against $2,268 million 3. Goeckeler called the third quarter "a fundamental inflection point for Sandisk — where our technology leadership is enabling a deliberate shift in our mix toward the highest-value end markets, led by Datacenter" 35.

ExpertFor specialists

SanDisk is also changing how it sells, not just what. At its 2026 Investor Day it reported that eight customers had signed what it calls New Business Model agreements, covering roughly half of FY2027 bits and about two-thirds of FY2028 bits 26 — long-term commitments that trade spot upside for visibility, the same move Kioxia and Micron have each described in their own terms. Its stated market view is that enterprise datacentre flash demand grows "to 1.2 zettabytes by 2030" 26. That is SanDisk's own projection, presented to investors, and this page reports it as such.

12.Hidden in plain sight

SimpleStart here

SanDisk does not own a single wafer fab. A company with $20.25 billion of annual revenue 3 gets "substantially all" of its product from factories belonging to another company, under a joint venture in which it holds the minority stake 4.

DeeperThe detail

The numbers make the dependency precise: 49.9% of each Flash Ventures entity, about 50% of the output, at "cost plus a small markup" 4. SanDisk's own risk language is blunt — "Substantially all of our flash-based memory is obtained from our joint ventures with Kioxia" 4. It is a structure that has lasted decades and was just extended to 2034 27, so it is clearly stable; but it means SanDisk's capacity is not something SanDisk can unilaterally decide to expand.

ExpertFor specialists

The second thing hidden in plain sight is the name. Today's Sandisk Corporation is a Delaware company "incorporated…for the purpose of holding WDC's Flash Business in connection with the separation" 9, with a different SEC registrant identity from the SanDisk that existed until 2016 10. Meanwhile the products carry a tangle of borrowed brands: WD_BLACK flash drives are SanDisk's, made "under license from Western Digital", while WD_BLACK hard drives are Western Digital's 17. SanDisk began untangling this in January 2026 by retiring the WD names from its own NVMe SSD lines in favour of SANDISK Optimus 24. A shopper comparing two WD_BLACK drives in 2025 was, without any indication on the box, choosing between two different companies' products.

13.Weak spots

SimpleStart here
  • No fabs. Capacity depends on a partner's plants and a joint venture it holds 49.9% of 4.
  • One product family. NAND flash only — no DRAM, no HBM 1.
  • One manufacturing country. Every wafer comes from Japan 1.
DeeperThe detail

The concentration is threefold and all of it is disclosed in SanDisk's own filings: substantially all supply from one partner 4, eight fabs all in Japan 1, and a single memory technology. Expansion requires agreement with Kioxia and, per the August 2026 announcement, government support as well — the $31 billion programme is "contingent upon government support" 29.

ExpertFor specialists

There is also a timing risk specific to its biggest idea. HBF's value depends on an ecosystem — processors that can address it, software that can use it, and a standard that others adopt. SanDisk has done the standards work properly, through OCP and with SK hynix, Google and Tenstorrent involved 15, but as of this check the specification is at v0.7.0 32 and no shipping product exists. The stated milestones, samples in H2 2026 and devices in early 2027 25, are the test.

A narrower disclosure point: SanDisk publishes no wafer-capacity or bit-share figure of its own, so its scale can only be inferred from revenue and from the joint venture's fab count 1 29.

14.India angle

SimpleStart here

SanDisk has no manufacturing in India. Its official locations page lists exactly one Indian site, an office in Bengaluru 28.

DeeperThe detail

The address SanDisk publishes is "No.143/1, Amani Bellandur Khane Village Prestige Excelsior, Prestige Tech, Park, Marathalli-Sarjapur Outer Ring Road, Kadubeesanahalli, Varthur Hobli, Bengaluru, Karnataka 560103 India" 28. SanDisk runs an India-localised website, so it clearly sells there 28, but its 10-K breaks out employees only by region — "approximately 11,100 employees across 33 countries" with "73% in Asia Pacific" — without an India figure 1 2.

ExpertFor specialists

This check found no official SanDisk statement of any Indian manufacturing, packaging or assembly-and-test venture, in any press release, 10-K or registration statement 1 9. Where reports of such ventures circulate, none is corroborated by a SanDisk document found here, so none is repeated as fact. Among the memory makers covered in this section, Micron remains the only one with a plant in India.

15.What’s next

SimpleStart here

Only what SanDisk has announced:

  • HBF samples: targeted for the second half of 2026, with AI-inference devices in early 2027 25.
  • $31 billion with Kioxia across the Japanese plants through 2032 29.
  • Flash Ventures runs to 31 December 2034 27.
  • UltraQLC 128TB and 256TB drives launching in the first half of 2026 22.
DeeperThe detail

SanDisk's financial model for FY2028–FY2030, set out at its August 2026 Investor Day, is for revenue growth in the "mid-to-high teens", non-GAAP gross margin of "approximately 80 percent", non-GAAP operating margin of "approximately 75 percent", operating expenses "around five percent", and adjusted free cash flow margin of "approximately 50 percent", while returning "100 percent of excess cash to shareholders after investing in the business" 26. Those are targets, not results, and this page marks them as such.

ExpertFor specialists

The near-term guidance is concrete and dated: Q1 FY2027 revenue of $10.30 billion to $10.80 billion, GAAP gross margin of 83.0% to 84.9%, and non-GAAP diluted EPS of $44.00 to $46.00 3. Whether that held is knowable after this check and was not knowable at it.

The open question is HBF. SanDisk has a published standard, two named consortium partners beyond SK hynix, and a stated sample date 15 25 — but no product and no announced customer. If HBF works, SanDisk becomes a supplier into AI accelerator packages rather than only into storage bays, which would be the largest change in what this company is since the split. If it does not, it remains a flash maker without a fab in a market where its partner decides the capacity.
