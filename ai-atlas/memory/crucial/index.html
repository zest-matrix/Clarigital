Crucial: What Happened to the Brand

For nearly thirty years, Crucial was how Micron sold memory to ordinary people. In December 2025 Micron exited that business to send the supply to AI data centres instead. This page is a record of what Crucial was, precisely what ended, and what owners should know. Three reading levels, every claim sourced.

Crucial 16 sections · 3 levels 24 linked sources Checked September 2026
How to read this page. Every maker in our Memory section uses the same 16 sections in the same order, the same shape as our AI Chips brand guides. Each section starts Simple, then goes Deeper, then Expert: stop wherever you have what you need. The small numbers are sources: click one to open the original document. Where a company has not published something, we say so rather than estimate.

1.At a glance

SimpleStart here

Crucial was Micron’s consumer brand — the only way to buy memory and storage made by one of the world’s three DRAM makers without going through a third party. On 3 December 2025 Micron announced it was leaving that business 1.

What it wasConsumer DRAM modules and SSDs — "our Crucial-branded SSDs and DRAM sold to the consumer market" 2 3
Who made itMicron Technology, which also made the chips inside 2
What ended"the Crucial consumer business, including the sale of Crucial consumer-branded products at key retailers" 1
WhyTo redirect supply to data-centre customers as AI demand surged 1
DeeperThe detail

This page is written in the past tense for a reason, but the reason needs stating carefully, because the obvious version of this story is wrong in two places.

Micron did not announce that it was retiring the Crucial brand. Its release announces exiting "the Crucial consumer business" and the sale of Crucial-branded products at retail 1. It does not say the trademark is retired, and Micron’s annual report for the fiscal year ending weeks before the announcement describes delivering products "through our Micron® and Crucial® brands" 2.

And Micron never said shipments had stopped. It said, in the future tense, that it "will continue Crucial consumer product shipments through the consumer channel until the end of fiscal Q2 (February 2026)" 1 — a commitment to keep shipping until a date, not a report that shipping ended on one. This check found no later Micron statement confirming that shipments actually ceased. See Hidden in plain sight.

ExpertFor specialists

The reason Micron gave is the clearest single statement in this whole section of what the AI build-out did to ordinary buyers. Sumit Sadana, Micron’s executive vice-president and chief business officer: "The AI-driven growth in the data center has led to a surge in demand for memory and storage. Micron has made the difficult decision to exit the Crucial consumer business in order to improve supply and support for our larger, strategic customers in faster-growing segments" 1.

Read that against Micron’s own guide in this section, and against Kingston’s. A chip maker with finite wafers chose its data-centre customers over its retail ones and closed the channel rather than ration it. Every module brand that buys Micron DRAM is on the other side of the same decision.

2.Company card

SimpleStart here
Brand ownerMicron Technology, Inc. 2
Launched"Micron launched Crucial in September 1996, selling random access memory directly to consumers" — local press, not a Micron source 4
RoleMicron’s only consumer-facing brand 4
Exit announced3 December 2025, from Boise, Idaho 1
ShipmentsTo continue "until the end of fiscal Q2 (February 2026)" 1
RevenueNever broken out by Micron in any filing found in this check 2
DeeperThe detail

The founding date is the weakest fact on this page and is labelled accordingly. September 1996 comes from BoiseDev, a local outlet with unusually good Micron access 4, and is consistent with Evertiq’s description of the exit coming "after 29 years" 5 — but this check found no Micron- or Crucial-authored statement of the founding year. Crucial’s own About page exists and could not be retrieved.

Crucial mattered structurally rather than financially. As BoiseDev put it: "Though Micron memory is used in scores of devices from servers to iPhones and more, Crucial is the company’s only consumer-facing brand" 4. Micron sells to Apple and to hyperscalers; Crucial was the one place an individual could buy from it directly.

ExpertFor specialists

Micron has never published a Crucial revenue figure, and the segment structure makes it hard to infer one. In April 2025 Micron announced a reorganisation into four units, with the transition to "be complete early in the company’s fiscal fourth quarter, which begins on May 30, 2025": the Cloud Memory Business Unit, "Focused on memory solutions for large hyperscale cloud customers, and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) for all data center customers"; the Core Data Center Business Unit; the Mobile and Client Business Unit; and the Automotive and Embedded Business Unit, "Focused on memory and storage solutions for the automotive, industrial and consumer segments" 6.

Micron’s annual report settles where Crucial sat, in one sentence: "MCBU sales also included our Crucial-branded SSDs and DRAM sold to the consumer market" 2. MCBU — the Mobile and Client Business Unit — "is focused on memory and storage solutions for the mobile and client segments" 2.

Worth noting because it reads like a contradiction and is not: the Automotive and Embedded unit is also described as covering "the automotive, industrial, and consumer segments" 2 6. Two units mention consumer, and the 10-K resolves which one carried Crucial by naming it. Micron’s fiscal 2026 second-quarter filing confirms the four reportable segments with no separate consumer line in the portion reached by this check 7.

3.The story

SimpleStart here
  • 1996 Micron launches Crucial, selling memory directly to consumers 4.
  • 2022 Micron discontinues Ballistix, Crucial’s gaming memory line 8 9.
  • Apr 2025 Micron reorganises into four AI-oriented business units 6.
  • 3 Dec 2025 Micron announces its exit from the Crucial consumer business 1.
  • Feb 2026 The end of Micron’s fiscal Q2, the date until which it committed to keep shipping 1.
DeeperThe detail

The Ballistix wind-down in February 2022 was the first half of this story and reads differently in hindsight. Micron discontinued the Ballistix, Ballistix Max and Ballistix Max RGB lines, with Teresa Kelley, vice-president and general manager of the Micron Commercial Products Group, explaining: "We remain focused on growing our NVMe and Portable SSD product categories, which both offer storage solutions for PC and console gamers. Additionally, Crucial JEDEC standard DDR5 memory provides mainstream gamers with DDR5-enabled computers with better high-speed performance, data transfers, and bandwidth than previously available with Crucial Ballistix memory" 8, with VideoCardz independently carrying the same statement 9.

At the time that read as a narrowing of focus onto the mainstream Crucial line. Three years later the mainstream line went too.

A Micron release did exist: trade coverage names it as "Micron To End-of-Life (EOL) Crucial Ballistix Product Lines", dated 16 February 2022 10. No Micron-hosted copy of it was reachable in this check, so the wording above survives only through the outlets that carried it 8 9 10. None of that coverage gives an end-of-sales or end-of-support date, and none is stated on this page.

ExpertFor specialists

The strategic logic was stated later and more bluntly than in the announcement. Christopher Moore, a Micron vice-president, told an interviewer in January 2026: "our viewpoint is that we are trying to help consumers around the world. We’re just doing it through different channels. We still have a very sizable business in the client and mobile markets. We are also, of course, servicing our data center customers. And what’s going on right now is that the TAM and data center is growing just absolutely tremendously. And we want to make sure that, as a company, we help fulfill that TAM as well" 11.

The argument is that Micron still reaches consumers, through the PC makers who buy its memory rather than through a shelf. Whether that is the same thing is a matter of opinion; what is not in dispute is the demand pressure behind it, which Micron’s chief executive Sanjay Mehrotra had quantified a month earlier, on the fiscal first-quarter earnings call of 17 December 2025: "The dramatic increase in HBM demand is further challenging the supply environment due to the 3-to-1 trade ratio with DDR5" 12. Three units of ordinary DDR5 capacity given up for every unit of HBM.

Crucial’s own support page still lists the drives it covered, which is the best surviving record of the range 3:

DeeperThe detail

The thing Crucial was best known for was not a product. The Crucial System Scanner read your computer and told you which memory and drives fitted it — a genuinely useful tool at a time when working out whether a module was compatible required reading a motherboard manual.

This check could not establish the Scanner’s fate. Its page is still indexed but could not be retrieved, and no Micron or Crucial statement about retiring it was found. That is a gap in this page, not a finding: it is recorded here as unknown rather than as gone 3.

ExpertFor specialists

Crucial’s position in the market was unusual and worth stating plainly for the record. Every other consumer memory brand documented in this section buys its chips. Crucial did not — it was the chip maker, selling direct. A Crucial module was Micron silicon on a Micron board with a Micron warranty, which is why it was often the reference point buyers compared other brands against.

That is also why the exit is more consequential than a brand closure. It did not move business from one seller to another; it removed a chip maker from the retail channel entirely 1.

5.How a module is built

SimpleStart here

Crucial modules and drives were built by Micron, using Micron’s own DRAM and NAND. There was no separate Crucial factory and no third-party chip supplier — the brand was a route to market, not a manufacturer 2.

DeeperThe detail

Micron’s own description of its structure makes the relationship explicit: the company "delivers a rich portfolio of high-performance DRAM, NAND, and NOR memory and storage products through our Micron® and Crucial® brands" 2. Two brands, one manufacturer.

For a buyer that meant something real: the chips in a Crucial module came from the same fabs as the chips Micron sold to server makers, documented on Micron’s own page in this section. It is the closest thing the consumer market had to buying at source.

ExpertFor specialists

It also meant Crucial had no supply independence of any kind. A module brand that buys from three chip makers, as Kingston does, can shift between them when one allocates elsewhere. A brand owned by one chip maker cannot — and when that chip maker decided its wafers were worth more in a data centre, there was no second source to fall back on. The single-source relationship that made Crucial trustworthy is the same one that ended it 1.

6.Where it’s made: plants and addresses

SimpleStart here

Crucial had no plants of its own. Its products were made in Micron’s facilities, which are documented on Micron’s page in this section — the only maker in this codex that publishes its plant addresses in full 2.

DeeperThe detail

What Crucial did have was a storefront, and its current state is worth recording precisely because it is easy to describe wrongly.

Micron itself still presents the brand as current. Its corporate site carries a "Consumer brand" page headed "Crucial", reading "As Micron’s global consumer brand, Crucial is uniquely able to connect millions of customers to the innovation and technology that Micron has been perfecting for more than four decades", with no discontinuation notice on it 14.

crucial.com has not gone dark. At the time of this check the domain resolved and an origin server answered, but public requests were refused by a bot-protection layer, so this check could not see the storefront. Crucial-branded support content was still being served from Crucial’s content origin, with no discontinuation notice on it 3. So this page does not say the site is gone, and does not say it still sells — neither could be established.

ExpertFor specialists

The support content that is reachable is substantive and current. It describes Storage Executive, which "optimizes and updates your Crucial SSDs on Microsoft Windows computers", lists the drives it supports, notes it is "only available for 64-bit systems", and carries a security-vulnerability notice for the MX100, MX200 and MX300 3. It carries no end-of-support notice of any kind 3.

That matters for owners, and there is a trap in it — see What owners should know.

7.How the memory works

SimpleStart here

Crucial products worked the same way any memory or SSD does; what distinguished them was provenance rather than design. The engineering is covered on Micron’s page in this section.

DeeperThe detail

The one genuinely distinctive piece of engineering attached to the brand was the compatibility tooling. Crucial published a scanner that identified a machine and returned the parts that fitted it, and Micron published Storage Executive for firmware and drive health 3.

Both mattered more than they sound. Memory compatibility is the single most common thing a non-expert gets wrong, and a manufacturer-run scanner that answered the question authoritatively was a real service — it is the same problem the "will it fit" helpers planned elsewhere in this codex exist to solve.

ExpertFor specialists

Crucial shipped JEDEC-standard memory rather than heavily overclocked parts, and Micron said so explicitly when it discontinued Ballistix: "Crucial JEDEC standard DDR5 memory provides mainstream gamers with DDR5-enabled computers with better high-speed performance, data transfers, and bandwidth than previously available with Crucial Ballistix memory" 8.

That is the whole positioning in one sentence. Crucial competed on being the manufacturer’s own standard-compliant part, not on the fastest binned kit — which is why its disappearance leaves a specific gap rather than just one fewer logo.

DeeperThe detail

The sentence most often paraphrased wrongly is the one about what survived, so here it is in full: "Micron will continue to support the sale of Micron-branded enterprise products to commercial channel customers globally" 1.

Three qualifiers, all load-bearing. Micron-branded, not Crucial-branded. Enterprise, not consumer. Commercial channel, not retail. It is not a carve-out that keeps Crucial alive for business buyers, and it is not a general OEM exemption.

ExpertFor specialists

The staffing line is worth quoting because it is the only thing Micron said about the people: "Micron intends to reduce impact on team members due to this business decision through redeployment opportunities into existing open positions within the company" 1. An intention to redeploy, not a commitment to retain. BoiseDev noted at the time that "It is not clear how many people are employed by Crucial in Idaho, and the company didn’t immediately comment" 4, and this check found no later figure.

9.Official pricing

SimpleStart here

Crucial sold at retail, so prices were public in a way no chip maker’s are — but Micron published no price list, and this check could not reach the Crucial storefront to record what the final prices were 3.

DeeperThe detail

What is documented is what happened to memory prices around the exit. Two days after the announcement, an Indian outlet reported that "In the Indian market, units that were once selling for around Rs 6,500 are now reselling for as high as Rs 25,200" 16 — roughly a fourfold rise. That figure is about the memory market generally rather than Crucial parts specifically, and it reflects the shortage that caused the exit rather than the exit itself.

Crucial leaving the shelf did not cause the shortage; the same underlying cause produced both. Micron said as much: AI-driven data-centre demand made consumer supply the thing to give up 1.

ExpertFor specialists

The price story has a second act, and it is the most practically important thing on this page. Because Micron’s warranty commitment carried no terms, the question of how a claim would be settled was left open — and by mid-2026 press reports described it being settled in cash at the original purchase price rather than by replacement. In a market where memory had multiplied in price, that is a materially worse outcome for the owner. See What owners should know for the detail and the caveats.

10.Whose chips are inside

SimpleStart here

Crucial never bought chips from anyone. Every Crucial module and drive used Micron’s own DRAM and NAND — the brand and the fab belonged to the same company 2.

DeeperThe detail

That makes Crucial the inverse of every other brand in this part of the codex. Kingston, Corsair, G.Skill and the rest buy chips from the makers and add boards, binning, testing and a warranty. Crucial added a storefront to a chip maker.

It is also why the exit says something the closure of an independent brand would not. When a module brand fails, its share moves to another module brand. When a chip maker withdraws its own brand, capacity leaves the consumer market altogether 1.

ExpertFor specialists

The mechanism Micron described is the "3-to-1 trade ratio with DDR5" — its chief executive’s own framing of how much ordinary memory capacity is consumed to produce high-bandwidth memory 12. HBM stacks use more silicon per usable bit and more packaging, so every increment of HBM output subtracts a multiple of it from the DDR5 pool that consumer modules come from.

Crucial is what that arithmetic looks like when it reaches a shelf. The brand did not fail commercially and Micron never said it did; it was closed because its wafers were worth more elsewhere 1.

11.Who buys it and why

SimpleStart here

Crucial sold to individuals, through its own website and through retailers — Micron’s release refers to "the sale of Crucial consumer-branded products at key retailers" 1.

DeeperThe detail

Its buyers were the people the rest of this section’s companies never deal with directly: someone adding memory to an ageing laptop, a student building a first PC, a small business replacing a failed drive. Micron’s argument for why losing that channel is acceptable is that it still reaches those people indirectly, through the PC makers it supplies 11.

The counter-argument is upgrades. A PC maker sells you memory once, when you buy the machine; a retail brand sells it to you four years later when the machine is slow. Nothing in Micron’s statements addresses that second market 1 11.

ExpertFor specialists

For business buyers, Micron’s answer is the enterprise line: "Micron will continue to support the sale of Micron-branded enterprise products to commercial channel customers globally" 1. That is a genuine continuation, but into a different channel with different products and different minimum quantities. It does not replace a retail brand.

12.Hidden in plain sight

SimpleStart here

Micron has never publicly confirmed that Crucial shipments actually stopped. It committed to shipping until February 2026 and issued no further statement about it 1 17. Meanwhile its corporate site still carries a page headed "Crucial" describing it, in the present tense, as "Micron’s global consumer brand" 14.

DeeperThe detail

The silence is documented rather than inferred, and it is unusual. Micron’s prepared remarks for its fiscal first quarter of 2026 — delivered on 17 December 2025, two weeks after the announcement, and retrieved in full for this check — do not mention Crucial at all 12. Neither do the prepared remarks for the fiscal second quarter of 18 March 2026, the quarter in which shipments were to end 17, nor those for the third quarter of 24 June 2026 18.

Those are verified absences in complete documents, not failures to retrieve. Management announced the closure of its only consumer brand and then did not refer to it in the prepared remarks of any of the three following earnings calls.

Two honest limits. Prepared remarks are not the analyst question-and-answer session, which this check could not obtain — a question may well have been asked. And Micron’s fiscal second-quarter 10-Q could only be read in part, so this page does not claim Micron’s filings are silent on the exit 7.

ExpertFor specialists

The second thing hidden in plain sight is that the brand was never declared dead. Micron exited a business; it did not announce retiring a trademark. Its most recent annual report still describes selling "through our Micron® and Crucial® brands" and records that "Micron, Crucial, any associated logos, and all other Micron trademarks are the property of Micron" 2; micron.com still runs a page presenting Crucial in the present tense as its global consumer brand 14; Crucial-branded support content was still being served at the time of this check 3; and no Micron statement found here says the name is finished.

Whether Micron intends to keep the mark alive, revive it if the memory cycle turns, or simply let it lapse is not answerable from anything published. This page records what ended — the consumer business and the retail channel 1 — and declines to declare the brand itself dead, because Micron has not.

13.Weak spots

SimpleStart here
  • One supplier, and it was also the owner. No second source existed when Micron reallocated its wafers 1.
  • The warranty commitment has no stated duration and no stated terms 1.
  • Warranty claims are reportedly being settled in cash, not replacements, at prices set years ago 19 20.
  • Micron never quantified the business, so its size is unknowable from outside 2.
DeeperThe detail

The warranty is where a discontinued brand’s weakness becomes a reader’s problem. Micron’s entire published commitment is one clause: the company "will work closely with partners and customers through this transition and will provide continued warranty service and support for Crucial products" 1. No end date, no claims address, no terms. Contemporary coverage read it as reassurance — "The warranty of existing Crucial products will continue to be valid" 21.

By mid-2026 the practice being reported looked different. On a failing 48GB DDR5 kit, Micron was reported to have offered a refund of the original purchase price, US$241.86, when the cheapest comparable 48GB kit then started at US$649.99 — roughly two and a half times the refund; a like-for-like replacement — three Crucial Pro DDR5-6400 modules — followed only after further correspondence with the company 19. A second account described a US$259.99 refund offered on a 128GB kit then costing around US$1,800, with no replacement 20.

Both accounts are trade-press reporting and neither contains a direct quotation from Micron. The outlet in the first case states it approached Micron and published no response 19. This page reports them as what they are — reported cases, not Micron policy — but an owner should know the pattern exists before assuming a like-for-like replacement.

ExpertFor specialists

The structural weakness worth drawing out is one this whole section illustrates. A brand owned by its chip maker looks like the safest possible supply relationship right up to the moment the chip maker has somewhere better to sell. Crucial had no second source because it did not need one, and that is exactly why it had no defence 1.

Micron gave no figure for what it was giving up, and none can be derived: the company has never broken out consumer revenue as a line, its segment structure changed months before the exit, and the fiscal 2026 second-quarter 10-Q could only be read in part in this check 2 6 7. Trade coverage asserts the same non-disclosure 22.

14.India angle

SimpleStart here

Crucial sold in India, through a dedicated Indian storefront. Micron’s exit applied worldwide — the release names no country and carves out none 1.

DeeperThe detail

The clearest India-specific record of the period is a price one: an Indian outlet reported "units that were once selling for around Rs 6,500" reselling "for as high as Rs 25,200" two days after the announcement 16 — a comment on the Indian memory market generally rather than on Crucial stock specifically.

For claims, the only Micron-published India route found in this check is its general returns page — and its wording matters. It tells anyone who bought "from our Micron consumer brand, reseller, authorized distributor or contract manufacturer" to "contact them directly for their return policies", which routes a Crucial buyer back to the shop rather than to Micron. For product bought through an India authorised distributor it names the Kaizen RMA Service Center 23. The word "Crucial" does not appear on the page 23.

ExpertFor specialists

Micron operates an assembly and test plant at Sanand in Gujarat, documented on its own page in this section — the only India manufacturing by any maker in this codex. This check found no statement from Micron or anyone else connecting the Crucial exit to Sanand, and none disclaiming a connection 1 23. Sanand is a packaging and test facility rather than a consumer-brand operation, so no connection would be expected; but that is reasoning, not a sourced fact, and this page asserts neither.

15.What’s next

SimpleStart here

If you own Crucial memory or a Crucial SSD, three things are worth knowing.

  • Firmware still works. Storage Executive, reachable through Crucial’s support pages, "optimizes and updates your Crucial SSDs on Microsoft Windows computers" and carries no end-of-support notice 3.
  • Do not go to micron.com for it. Micron’s own Storage Executive page is a different product 24.
  • The warranty stands, but reported claims have been met with a cash refund at the original purchase price — in one case modules followed after further correspondence, in another they did not 19 20.
DeeperThe detail

The firmware trap is worth spelling out, because both tools share a name. Crucial’s support page describes Storage Executive as covering "Crucial X10 Pro, X9 Pro, X9, X8, X6, P-series, T-series, T500, MX-series, BX-series, M550, and M500 SSDs" 3. Micron’s corporate page describes a different thing: "Storage Executive is a Graphical User Interface (GUI) for managing and supporting Micron SSDs", listing only Micron-branded drives — the 3500, 3400, 2300, 2210, 2100 and 5400 series, a mix of client and data-centre parts — and making no mention of Crucial client SSDs 24.

An owner sent to micron.com for Crucial firmware will not find it. The Crucial-side resource is the one to use 3 24.

ExpertFor specialists

What happens to the name is genuinely open. Micron exited a business without retiring a trademark, still lists both brands in its most recent annual report, still runs a present-tense Crucial page on its corporate site, and has said nothing about Crucial in the prepared remarks of three subsequent earnings calls 2 14 12 17 18. A brand kept alive in a filing and absent from every statement is a brand whose owner has not decided, or has not said.

The wider question the page exists to record is whether the consumer memory channel gets its chip maker back. Micron’s stated reason was allocation, not failure — supply directed "to our larger, strategic customers in faster-growing segments" 1. Allocation decisions reverse when cycles turn. Nothing in anything Micron has published says this one will, and nothing says it will not.
