AMD Instinct MI455X: The Complete Guide

AMD’s newest AI chip, and the one its biggest customers are betting billions of watts on. Simple to expert, every number links to its source.

1.At a glance

SimpleStart here

MI455X is AMD’s newest AI chip, part of the MI400 series, in production from July 2026. It does not ship alone — AMD sells it inside Helios, a complete rack that pairs 72 MI455X chips with 18 of AMD’s own EPYC “Venice” CPUs 12.

Deep diveThe technical detail

MI455X was first previewed at AMD’s Advancing AI 2025 event on 12 June 2025 3, then entered production a little over a year later. AMD’s own July 2026 material treats the chip and the Helios rack as one story rather than two — nearly every performance figure AMD publishes for MI455X is stated at the rack level, not the single-chip level 4.

2.Launch and history

SimpleStart here

Three of the largest names in AI — OpenAI, Meta and Anthropic — have each signed multi-gigawatt deals for chips from this exact family, alongside Oracle and Microsoft. AMD’s next few years of AI-chip revenue rest heavily on this one generation actually shipping on schedule.

Deep diveThe technical detail

All five deals are dated within a single 12-month stretch: Oracle (October 2025), OpenAI (October 2025), Meta (February 2026), Microsoft (July 2026) and Anthropic (July 2026) 56789 — see the Systems section for the figures behind each one. That concentration is itself worth noting: MI455X is not one customer’s chip, it is the chip AMD is betting several of its largest relationships on at once.

3.What’s inside it

SimpleStart here

MI455X uses AMD’s fifth-generation CDNA architecture and carries 256 Work Group Processors, per AMD’s own product page 10.

Deep diveThe technical detail

AMD’s MI400 product page states the architecture directly as “5th Gen AMD CDNA architecture” with 256 Work Group Processors 10. AMD does not publish a transistor count or a specific manufacturing process node for the MI455X GPU die itself on this page or in any other official material this page could find — a figure of 320 billion transistors circulates in secondary technology coverage, but this page does not repeat it without an AMD source confirming it.

5.Where it’s made

SimpleStart here

AMD has not published which foundry or process node makes the MI455X GPU itself. Its companion CPU in the same rack, EPYC “Venice”, is confirmed on TSMC’s 2-nanometre N2 process 11.

Deep diveThe technical detail

This is a genuine and specific gap in AMD’s own public material: AMD’s April 2025 press release states plainly that “Venice”, the CPU, is “the first HPC product in the industry to be taped out and brought up on the TSMC advanced 2nm (N2) process technology” 11 — but that claim is about the CPU by name, not the MI455X GPU that ships alongside it. Comments attributed to AMD’s CEO in earnings-call coverage suggest a 2-nanometre process for the MI450-series GPU too, but this page could not locate a primary AMD product page, press release or conference slide stating that directly for the GPU, so it reports the gap rather than repeating an unconfirmed figure.

7.Official pricing

SimpleStart here

Not published by AMD. MI455X is sold through Helios rack deployments and direct hyperscaler deals rather than listed with a per-chip or per-GPU-hour price.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Given that MI455X is brand new to production as of this page’s last check and its confirmed customers are hyperscalers buying at gigawatt scale rather than renting individual GPU-hours, no cloud provider’s own pricing page yet lists it, and AMD has not published a list price for the chip or the rack.

8.Real-world performance

SimpleStart here

AMD’s own headline claim: Helios delivers 34 times the token throughput of MI355X-based systems at high interactivity, on the DeepSeek-V4-Flash model 4.

Deep diveThe technical detail

The exact wording, from AMD’s own July 2026 Helios launch blog: “34X higher token throughput at high interactivity” compared with MI355X-based systems, plus a separate claim of “18X lower token cost”, both benchmarked specifically on DeepSeek-V4-Flash 4. This is AMD’s own first-party benchmark, tied to one named model and one named operating condition (“high interactivity”) — this page states those qualifiers rather than repeating “34x faster” as an unconditional, general claim.

9.What came before, what came next

SimpleStart here

Came before: the MI350 series, announced June 2025. What AMD says comes next: MI500 in 2027, MI600 in 2028.

Deep diveThe technical detail

AMD’s own July 2026 release states its roadmap in one line: “Next-generation AMD Instinct MI500 Series GPUs are coming in 2027, with next-generation compute, memory and interconnect technologies for leadership performance,” with MI600 series “coming in 2028” 2. AMD’s own June 2025 material had already described MI350-to-MI400 as up to a “4x generation-on-generation AI compute increase,” giving some sense of the pace AMD is claiming between generations 14.

10.Hidden in plain sight

SimpleStart here

AMD barely talks about MI455X as a standalone chip — almost every number it publishes is at the level of the whole Helios rack, not the single GPU, which is itself a statement about how AMD expects this generation to be bought and used.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Helios’s own rack-level figures are striking on their own terms: 31 TB of combined HBM4 memory, 1.7 petabytes per second of combined bandwidth, 260 TB/s of scale-up networking bandwidth (AMD states this uses UALink, the open interconnect standard AMD co-founded, at 21.6 TB/s bidirectional per switch) and 43 TB/s of scale-out networking through AMD’s own Pensando “Vulcano” 800 Gbps network cards 154. At the rack level, AMD states 2.9 exaflops of dense FP4 compute and 1.4 exaflops of FP8 — figures that only make sense once you know they describe 72 chips working together, not one 4.
