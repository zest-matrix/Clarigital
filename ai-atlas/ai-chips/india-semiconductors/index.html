India’s Semiconductor Push

India spent decades importing every chip it used. Since 2021 it has approved twelve chip plants, opened its first ones and handed over its first made-in-India memory modules. What has been approved, what is actually running, what it costs the public purse, and what India still cannot make. Start simple, go as deep as you like.

1.The scheme and the money

SimpleStart here

In December 2021 India's cabinet approved the Semicon India Programme with ₹76,000 crore to build a chip industry from almost nothing 1. The government pays up to half the cost of an approved plant 2. In July 2026 it approved a second, larger phase, Semicon 2.0, worth ₹1,27,500 crore 3.

First phase₹76,000 crore, approved December 2021 1
Government shareUp to 50% of the cost of a plant 2
Second phase₹1,27,500 crore, approved 15 July 2026 3
Plants approved12, about ₹1.64 lakh crore 4
DeeperThe detail

The original scheme offered different support for different kinds of plant: up to 50% for chip and display factories, but only 30% for packaging and compound-semiconductor units 1. In September 2022 the cabinet levelled this up to 50% for everything, paid in step with the company's own spending 2. The India Semiconductor Mission runs the schemes 5.

ExpertFor specialists

Semicon 2.0 has six parts: design, machines and materials, more fabs, packaging, research aimed at nodes more advanced than the 28 to 110 nanometres India starts with, and talent 3. The February 2026 budget had set aside ₹1,000 crore for ISM 2.0 and ₹8,000 crore for the existing programme in 2026-27, before the larger July approval 6 7.

2.The twelve approved plants

SimpleStart here

Twelve plants have been approved. The government groups them as one silicon chip factory, one silicon-carbide factory, one display factory and nine assembly and testing units 4 — though one of those nine, the HCL-Foxconn plant at Jewar, will process 20,000 wafers a month of its own 8.

DeeperThe detail

Four more were approved in August 2025, together ₹4,600 crore: SiCSem in Bhubaneshwar (India's first commercial compound-semiconductor factory, silicon carbide), 3D Glass Solutions in Bhubaneshwar (advanced packaging), ASIP in Andhra Pradesh, and Continental Device India in Mohali (discrete chips) 13. Two more came in May 2026: Crystal Matrix in Dholera (Micro-LED displays) and Suchi Semicon in Surat 14.

ExpertFor specialists

Dholera is the only general-purpose logic fab: 50,000 wafers a month at 28 to 110 nanometres, with a fiscal support agreement signed in March 2025 and more than 20,000 jobs promised 10 15. Tata's Assam plant began construction by August 2024 16. Sub-town addresses are published only for the Odisha units ("Info Valley, Bhubaneshwar") and the Uttar Pradesh unit ("near Jewar airport") 13 8.

3.What is actually being made

SimpleStart here

The first "made in India" chips were shown to the prime minister on 2 September 2025. They came from a pilot line, and 20 chips designed and made by Indian students were shown alongside them 17.

The first commercial plant opened on 28 February 2026: Micron's assembly and test plant at Sanand 9. At the opening, Micron says it "presented its first shipment of made-in-India memory modules to Dell Technologies" for laptops built in India 18.

DeeperThe detail

Micron expects "tens of millions of chips" from Sanand in 2026, "scaling to hundreds of millions in 2027" 18. Kaynes Semicon's plant at Sanand was inaugurated on 31 March 2026, and the HCL-Foxconn plant had its groundbreaking on 21 February 2026 5.

ExpertFor specialists

Government counts of how many plants are producing differ: a release from SEMICON India 2026 says "03 of the 12 semiconductor projects approved under Semicon 1.0" have started commercial production, while a background note from the same month says "five units have commenced commercial production"; neither lists them 19 4. All of them are packaging and testing plants. The Dholera fab is still being built, and no government or company page we could find gives a first-chip date 15.

4.India’s AI computing

SimpleStart here

India rents AI computing rather than making the chips. Under the IndiaAI Mission, the government hired GPUs from Indian cloud companies and resells the time cheaply to startups, researchers and students 20.

The May 2025 release reaches 34,333 by adding 15,916 to 18,417 existing GPUs, slightly fewer than the 18,693 announced in January 20 21
March 2026More than 38,000, with 190 projects approved 22
DeeperThe detail

The government published its prices. The bidding produced ₹115.85 per GPU-hour, or ₹150 for high-end chips, and a 40% government subsidy brought the price to users below ₹100 an hour, against $2.50 to $3.00 internationally, the ministry said in January 2025 20. Later published on-demand rates include ₹153 an hour for one NVIDIA H100 and ₹2,584 an hour for eight B200s 21.

ExpertFor specialists

The first ten providers were Jio Platforms, Tata Communications, Yotta, E2E Networks, CtrlS, NxtGen, CMS Computers, Locuz, Orient Technologies and Vensysco 20. Separately, foreign companies are building AI data centres in India: Google's $15 billion AI hub at Visakhapatnam with AdaniConneX and Airtel 23 24, Microsoft's $17.5 billion over four years 25, and AWS's ₹1,05,600 crore by 2030 26. Yotta says it will run 20,736 NVIDIA Blackwell Ultra GPUs 27.

5.Chip design and talent

SimpleStart here

India has long designed chips for foreign companies. The government says India is home to nearly 20% of the world's chip design workforce 4. Its Design Linked Incentive scheme pays up to half the cost of designing a chip, capped at ₹15 crore 28.

DeeperThe detail

By January 2026 the scheme had supported 95 companies with professional design software and sanctioned 24 chip-design projects, worth about ₹900 crore in total 4; by September 2026 the figure given was 105 startups 28 4 19. Results so far: 16 designs sent to a factory, 6 chips actually made, and more than 140 reusable design blocks 28.

ExpertFor specialists

The Chips to Startup programme, ₹250 crore over five years, covers hundreds of colleges; 56 student-designed chips have been made and delivered, and 211 chips have been taped out by 75 institutions, 149 of them on an older 180 nm process at Mohali 29 30. C-DAC's VEGA family of Indian-designed processors includes THEJAS64, made at Mohali on 180 nm 31; its AUM high-performance processor design, with 64 GB of HBM3 memory, was published as planned for 2024, and we found no confirmation that it has been made 32. AMD said in 2023 that its Bengaluru campus "will serve as the company's largest design center" 33.

6.What India does not have yet

SimpleStart here

What India does not yet have, on the government's own account:

  • No working silicon chip factory. The plants producing today are packaging and testing plants 4.
  • No advanced chipmaking. The only fab under construction stops at 28 nm, decades behind the 2 nm chips used for AI 15 3.
  • No AI memory (HBM). No approved project makes it 7.
  • No AI accelerator. No approved project builds one 7.
DeeperThe detail

The research pillar of Semicon 2.0 exists precisely to reach nodes beyond the 28 to 110 nanometres India is starting with, and a second pillar exists to start making the machines and chemicals that India currently imports 3. Under Semicon 2.0 the first fab is scheduled to be commissioned in 2028 34.

ExpertFor specialists

The nearest thing to an Indian AI chip is Netrasemi's 12 nm AI system-on-chip for cameras and drones, which the ministry describes as India's first 28. That is an edge chip, not a data-centre accelerator. For what those need, see our AI memory guide and NVIDIA guide.

7.How India fits into the world

SimpleStart here

India imported nearly $150 billion of semiconductors between 2016-17 and 2024-25, growing 23% a year; on that path imports would reach $240 billion a year by 2035, the government says 4. That is the case it makes for spending public money on factories 3.

DeeperThe detail

India's own market was about $38 billion in 2023 and is projected at $100 to $110 billion by 2030 7. At SEMICON India 2026 the government put India's future requirement at $200 billion as the world market doubles to $2 trillion 19.

ExpertFor specialists

India's position is different from China's: it is not under US export restrictions and buys freely from NVIDIA and others, but it also has no advanced factory of its own. See our export rules guide for how those restrictions work, and our Huawei Ascend guide for the Chinese comparison.

8.What happens next

SimpleStart here

Announced dates to watch:

  • Semicon 2.0 spending of ₹1,27,500 crore begins 3.
  • More plants: the 2026-27 plan targets one new fab and nine packaging units 7.
  • First fab under the new phase: commissioning scheduled for 2028 34.
  • Dholera fab: under construction; no official first-chip date 15.
DeeperThe detail

The 2026-27 plan also targets 30 more design companies supported, 10 new Indian design blocks and 200 more design professionals 7. The talent pillar counts 315 universities and about 68,000 students already trained on professional design software 3.

ExpertFor specialists

The test is whether approvals become output. Three to five of twelve plants are producing, all in packaging; the fab, the display plant and the compound-semiconductor plants are still to come 4 19. A government target of about 85,000 design engineers was described by the IT secretary in September 2026 (press report of a government official) 35.
