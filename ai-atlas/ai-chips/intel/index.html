Intel AI Chips: The Complete Guide

The only big AI chip company that owns its own chip factories, yet its AI accelerator is made by TSMC. What Intel sells for AI, where its fabs are, the prices it publishes, the US government stake, and the facts buried in its filings. Three reading levels, every number sourced.

1.At a glance

SimpleStart here

Intel is unlike NVIDIA and AMD in one big way: it designs chips and owns the factories that make most of them, in the US, Ireland, Israel and Asia 1. Yet its current AI accelerator, Gaudi 3, is made by TSMC 2. In its latest quarter Intel sold $16.1 billion, up 25% on a year earlier, and its Data Center and AI business sold $6.3 billion, up 59% 3.

What it sellsXeon server processors, Core Ultra PC chips with built-in AI units, Gaudi AI accelerators, and chip-making for other companies 4
Latest AI acceleratorGaudi 3 (2024); Crescent Island, an inference GPU, is next 5 6
Who makes the chipsIntel's own fabs for its processors; TSMC for Gaudi 3 7 2
Biggest AI roleXeon is the main processor inside NVIDIA's DGX B300 AI servers 8
DeeperThe detail

In 2026 Intel's AI story is mostly about processors, not accelerators. Its CEO says the CPU "now serves as the orchestration layer and critical control plane for the entire AI stack" 9, and the finance chief said businesses driven by AI made up about 70% of revenue in the June 2026 quarter 10. Intel's Q2 2026 results release does not mention Gaudi or any data-centre GPU 3.

ExpertFor specialists

Intel's fiscal 2025 revenue was $52.9 billion, flat on the year, with a gross margin of 34.8% (36.7% on Intel's adjusted basis). Data Center and AI sold $16.9 billion with $3.4 billion of operating profit; Intel Foundry reported $17.8 billion of revenue, mostly from Intel itself, and a $10.3 billion operating loss 11. For the third quarter of 2026 Intel guided revenue of $15.8 to $16.8 billion 3.

3.The story

SimpleStart here
  • 1968 Founded by Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore 12.
  • 1971 Launches the 4004, the world's first programmable microprocessor 22.
  • 1998 Introduces the Xeon brand for servers 22.
  • 2019 Buys Habana Labs of Israel, maker of Gaudi, for about $2 billion 23.
  • 2022 Launches Gaudi 2 24.
  • 2024 Launches Gaudi 3 5.
  • 2025 Drops Falcon Shores as a product; Lip-Bu Tan becomes CEO; the US government takes a 9.9% stake 25 13 19.
  • 2026 First chips on its new Intel 18A process ship: Panther Lake PCs (January) and Xeon 6+ (second quarter) 26 3.
DeeperThe detail

Intel bought its way into AI accelerators twice. It agreed to buy Nervana Systems in August 2016 27, then bought Habana Labs, based in Caesarea, Israel, in December 2019 for about $2 billion 23. The Gaudi chips come from Habana. In 2021 Pat Gelsinger's "IDM 2.0" plan opened Intel's factories to outside customers 28, and in 2024 Intel Foundry became a separately reported business 18.

ExpertFor specialists

2025 recapitalised Intel. SoftBank agreed to buy $2 billion of shares at $23 on 18 August 20. On 22 August the US government agreed to buy 433.3 million new shares at $20.47, $8.9 billion in all, paid for with grants Intel had already been awarded 19. Silver Lake bought 51% of Intel's Altera chip unit, closing on 15 September 29 30. On 18 September NVIDIA agreed to buy $5 billion of shares at $23.28 21, a deal that closed in the fourth quarter 31.

Intel's accelerator roadmap has been reset more than once. In January 2025 it said Falcon Shores would be used only as an internal test chip, "without bringing it to market", and that it would focus on Jaguar Shores, a rack-scale system 25. In October 2025 Lip-Bu Tan said Intel planned to launch inference-optimised GPUs every year 38. ExpertFor specialists

Gaudi 3 comes as an OAM module (HL-325L, up to 900 W air-cooled) and a PCIe card (HL-338, up to 600 W) 2. For China, Intel listed cut-down versions, HL-328 and HL-388, at 450 W 40. The next Xeons: Diamond Rapids, with up to 256 cores and 16 memory channels on Intel 18A-P 6, then Coral Rapids, which brings back multi-threading 41.

5.How it’s made

SimpleStart here

Intel makes most of its own processors, but not everything 1:

  1. Design: Intel's own teams; the Gaudi chips come from Habana Labs in Israel 23.
  2. Wafers in Intel's fabs: Panther Lake and Xeon 6+ on Intel 18A in Arizona; Xeon 6 on Intel 3 in Ireland 7 42 43.
  3. Wafers at TSMC: all of Gaudi 3, on TSMC's 5 nm process 2, and some tiles of Intel's PC chips 44 45.
  4. Packaging: Intel stacks and joins chip pieces with its Foveros technology in New Mexico 46.
  5. Assembly and test: Intel's own sites in Malaysia, Vietnam and China 47 1.
DeeperThe detail

Intel 18A is Intel's newest process. It combines a new transistor design, RibbonFET, with PowerVia, which delivers power from the back of the chip; Intel claims up to 15% better performance per watt and 30% better density than Intel 3 7. Volume production began in Oregon, with Arizona ramping later in 2025 48. Xeon 6+ mixes processes in one package: 12 compute tiles on 18A, three base tiles on Intel 3 and two input/output tiles on Intel 7 49.

In March 2025 Intel was still buying about 30% of its wafers from outside, mostly TSMC, and the finance chief said getting that to zero quickly was "no longer the strategy" 50. In April 2026 Lip-Bu Tan called TSMC "a very important partner" and described a "multi-foundry approach" 51.

ExpertFor specialists

Yields on 18A (the share of good chips per wafer) were a worry: in January 2026 Tan said they were "still below where I want them to be" 41. By July 2026 Intel said yields were running ahead of expectations and 18A output was about 25% above target 10. Intel was first to put ASML's newest High-NA EUV machines to work in volume: the first was assembled in Oregon in April 2024 52, and in July 2026 some Intel 18A layers for Panther Lake were running on them 53 3. The next process, Intel 14A, is due for risk production for Intel's own products in the second half of 2027 and high volume in 2028 10.

ExpertFor specialists

In August 2025 the unpaid grants were turned into shares 19. Intel says its US spending on tools and buildings from 2021 to 2026 is "approaching $100 billion" 10. In July 2026 its New Mexico fab manager said some customers are coming to Intel first for advanced packaging 65.

7.How it works

SimpleStart here

Gaudi is built differently from a GPU. It has a few very large matrix engines for the heavy maths plus 64 smaller programmable cores 2. Its other trick is networking: each Gaudi 3 has 24 ports of standard 200 Gb Ethernet on the chip itself, so chips link over ordinary Ethernet instead of a proprietary connection 2. Xeon processors do AI maths too, using a built-in unit called AMX in every core 66.

DeeperThe detail

Gaudi 3 is two compute dies joined in one package 67, with 96 MB of on-chip memory and 128 GB of HBM2e memory running at 3.7 TB/s 2. Intel's rack design holds up to 64 Gaudi 3 chips with 8.2 TB of memory 33. Crescent Island takes a different route: instead of costly HBM it uses LPDDR5X, the memory found in laptops, which lets it carry 160 GB, or up to 480 GB in partner versions, on an air-cooled 350 W card 6 68.

ExpertFor specialists

AMX on Xeon 6 performs up to 2,048 INT8 operations or 1,024 BF16 operations per cycle per core 66. Gaudi 3 has 8 matrix engines and 64 tensor processor cores 2. Crescent Island has 32 Xe3P cores with 256 matrix (XMX) engines and 32 MB of shared cache, and supports number formats from FP4 to FP64 6 68; Intel has not published its compute figures 69.

10.The software lock-in

SimpleStart here

Intel's strongest position in AI is the server processor. Its Xeon 6 is the main CPU inside NVIDIA's DGX B300 8, and was chosen for NVIDIA's DGX Rubin NVL8 systems 9. Its other advantage is owning factories in the US and Europe 1. For software, Gaudi runs PyTorch through Intel's Gaudi software (version 1.24.1 supports PyTorch 2.11) 79, and the popular vLLM serving tool has a Gaudi plug-in 80.

DeeperThe detail

Intel is also partnering with the market leader. Under its September 2025 deal, Intel will build custom x86 processors for NVIDIA's AI platforms, linked by NVIDIA's NVLink, and PC chips that include NVIDIA RTX graphics chiplets 21. In April 2026 Intel announced a multi-year collaboration with SambaNova on AI inference systems combining SambaNova's chips with Xeon 6 9. For AI on PCs, Intel's free OpenVINO toolkit reached version 2026.4 in September 2026 81.

ExpertFor specialists

Software has been Gaudi's weak point. When Intel missed its Gaudi revenue target in 2024, Pat Gelsinger cited "software ease of use" among the reasons 82. In January 2025 Michelle Johnston Holthaus said "it's not enough to just deliver the silicon"; Intel needs to deliver "a complete rack scale solution" 83. In February 2025 Intel stopped maintaining its open-source SynapseAI Core driver code 84.

11.Who buys it and why

SimpleStart here

At the Gaudi 3 launch in April 2024 Intel named customers and partners including NAVER, Bosch, IBM, Ola/Krutrim, NielsenIQ, Seekr, IFF, CtrlS Group, Bharti Airtel, Landing AI, Roboflow and Infosys 5. IBM Cloud became the first global cloud to offer Gaudi 3, generally available from 31 March 2025 85.

DeeperThe detail
  • Stability AI (2023): anchor customer for an AI supercomputer with about 4,000 Gaudi 2 chips 86.
  • Inflection AI (2024): its enterprise product runs on Gaudi 3 87.
  • Dell and Supermicro: sell servers with eight Gaudi 3 chips 88 89.
  • NVIDIA: uses Xeon 6 as the host processor in DGX systems 8 9.
  • Chip-making customers: Microsoft (a chip design on 18A, 2024) 90, Amazon Web Services (an AI fabric chip on 18A) 91, the US Department of Defense 92, and Fortinet (2026) 3. Reports that Tesla and Apple will use Intel's factories come from Tesla's CEO and a newspaper, not from Intel 93 94.
ExpertFor specialists

Intel depends on a few PC makers: its three largest customers took 43% of revenue in 2025 (19%, 12% and 12%); the 10-K data names them as Dell, Lenovo and HP 95. We found no Gaudi revenue figure in Intel's filings or results releases; the only public number is the 2024 target it missed 96 82.

12.Hidden in plain sight

SimpleStart here

Public facts buried in Intel's filings and releases. Each links to the exact document.

  1. Its fastest growth in 15 years came with an $11 billion loss. In the June 2026 quarter revenue grew 25%, which the CEO called the strongest growth in more than fifteen years, but Intel reported a loss of $2.16 a share 3 17, mainly from a $12.5 billion non-cash charge in the quarter ($13.6 billion over six months): shares it owes the US government rose in value as Intel's stock climbed 60 97.
  2. The government can take another 5% if Intel lets go of its factories. It holds a five-year warrant at $20 a share that can be used only if Intel stops owning at least 51% of its foundry business 19 98.
  3. 158.74 million shares sit in escrow, released to the government as Secure Enclave money arrives 98; about 143 million were still unreleased in June 2026 60.
  4. The government must vote with Intel's board, except in limited cases, and has no board seat 98.
  5. Intel may stop developing its next process without an outside customer. Its annual report says that without "a significant external foundry customer for Intel 14A" it may pause or discontinue 14A and shift manufacturing to third parties, "particularly TSMC" 4.
DeeperThe detail
  1. Intel's own AI accelerator is made by its foundry rival: Gaudi 3 is built on TSMC's 5 nm process 2.
  2. Three PC makers bought 43% of everything Intel sold in 2025 95.
  3. About 23,800 fewer employees in one year: 108,900 to 85,100 in 2025, partly because Altera and the memory business left 11.
  4. Intel Foundry lost $10.3 billion in 2025 on $17.8 billion of revenue, most of it from Intel itself 11.
  5. Spending plans flipped within six months: in January 2026 Intel guided capital spending "flat to down slightly"; in July it said "more than $20 billion" 41 10.
ExpertFor specialists
  1. Gaudi 3 costs the same per hour as NVIDIA's H100 SXM in India: ₹153 each on the IndiaAI portal, before GST 78.
  2. China got its own Gaudi 3: the HL-328 and HL-388, limited to 450 W 40.
  3. No mention of accelerators: the June 2026 results release and call remarks talk about CPUs, custom chips and packaging, not Gaudi or a data-centre GPU 3 10.
  4. First to use ASML's newest machines in volume: some Panther Lake layers now run on High-NA EUV 53.
  5. The government stake replaced the grant strings: the August 2025 deal removed the earlier CHIPS Act clawback and profit-sharing terms 19.

13.Weak spots

SimpleStart here

Where Intel has fallen short, in its own words:

  • Missed its AI target: "We will not achieve our target of $500 million in revenue for Gaudi in 2024" 82.
  • A cancelled chip: Falcon Shores became an internal test chip only 25.
  • Factory losses: Intel Foundry lost $10.3 billion in 2025 11.
  • Delays: the Ohio fab slipped to 2030 to 2031 58.
  • The government as shareholder is listed among the risks in its annual report 4.
DeeperThe detail

Market share is hard to pin down: the only figure we found, from TechInsights, put Intel at 22% of a $17.7 billion data-centre AI chip market in 2023, but that counts processors used for AI, not Gaudi alone; NVIDIA had 65% and AMD 11% 99. Intel's own accelerator roadmap now rests on Crescent Island, which had no named customer as of August 2026 69.

ExpertFor specialists

Export rules hit Gaudi. In April 2025 Intel told Chinese customers that AI chips above set memory and connection speeds need a US licence, which covers Gaudi 100. In December 2025 the US allowed NVIDIA H200 sales to approved Chinese buyers for a 25% fee and said the policy would also apply to AMD and Intel 101. Intel's 14A plan also depends on winning an outside customer 4.

14.India angle

SimpleStart here

India is one of Intel's biggest engineering bases. In June 2022 Intel had nine design and engineering centres in India, eight in Bengaluru and one in Hyderabad, described as its largest design operations outside the US, with about 14,000 employees 102.

DeeperThe detail

In December 2025 Tata and Intel signed a memorandum of understanding in Mumbai to explore making and packaging Intel products for local markets at Tata Electronics' upcoming fab and assembly plants, and to scale AI PCs for India 103. It is non-binding: the plants are Tata's Dholera fab in Gujarat and its assembly site in Assam, and no Intel investment was disclosed 104.

ExpertFor specialists

Gaudi 2 and Gaudi 3 are in the IndiaAI Mission's shared compute pool, which listed 34,333 GPUs in May 2025 105; one Gaudi 2 card lists at ₹57.60 an hour and one Gaudi 3 at ₹153 78. Indian names among Intel's 2024 Gaudi partners include Ola/Krutrim, CtrlS, Bharti Airtel and Infosys 5. We found no newer official figure for Intel's India headcount than 2022.

15.What’s next

SimpleStart here

Only what Intel has announced:

  • Crescent Island: customer samples in the second half of 2026, as Intel said when announcing it 36; press reports put the launch in 2027 37.
  • Jaguar Shores: the rack-scale AI system that replaced Falcon Shores 25.
  • Nova Lake PC chips: end of 2026 41.
  • Diamond Rapids and Coral Rapids: the next Xeons 6 41.
  • Intel 14A: risk production in the second half of 2027, high volume in 2028 10.
DeeperThe detail

Intel said in January 2026 that 14A customers would make "firm supplier decisions starting in the second half of this year" 41, and in July that the next 14A design kit (version 0.9) is due in October 2026 10. It plans capital spending of more than $20 billion in 2026 and "significantly above" that in 2027 10.

ExpertFor specialists

Two tests to watch. First, whether 14A wins a confirmed outside customer, since Intel's own annual report says the node depends on it 4; the Tesla and Apple reports are not confirmed by Intel 93 94. Second, whether Crescent Island, Intel's next data-centre AI chip, finds buyers 6 69.
