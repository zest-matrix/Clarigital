Intel Gaudi 2: The Complete Guide

The chip that tripled Gaudi’s memory and quadrupled its rental customers’ choices — still rented today, in India and through independent clouds. Simple to expert, every number links to its source.

1.At a glance

SimpleStart here

Gaudi 2, launched at Intel Vision on 10 May 2022, tripled the memory of the first Gaudi chip — from 32 GB to 96 GB — and Intel’s own launch materials claimed “2x throughput performance over Nvidia A100 for training” 1.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Intel backed that claim with public numbers fast: MLPerf training results published 29 June 2022, just over a month after launch, showed Gaudi 2 with a “36% reduction in time-to-train as compared to Nvidia’s [time-to-train] for A100-80GB” on ResNet-50 2. Intel’s Sandra Rivera, then an executive vice president, called the speed of that submission notable in itself — “just one month after launch” 2.

2.Launch and history

SimpleStart here

Gaudi 2 is the generation that made Habana’s chips look like a real alternative to NVIDIA’s GPUs rather than a curiosity: Intel’s launch materials led with a head-to-head comparison against NVIDIA’s A100, not just a price argument 1.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Habana Labs, bought by Intel for about $2 billion in December 2019, designed Gaudi 2 the same way it designed the original chip — from its Caesarea, Israel team 3. Stability AI later used Gaudi 2 as the anchor for an AI supercomputer sized at about 4,000 chips, announced at Intel Innovation in September 2023 4.

3.What’s inside it

SimpleStart here

Twenty-four Tensor Processor Cores, up from eight on the first Gaudi chip, plus double the on-board SRAM (48 MB) and 24 integrated 100 Gigabit RoCE ports for chip-to-chip networking 1.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Intel’s fact sheet gives a server-level networking figure too: “2.4 terabits of networking throughput in the 8-card Gaudi server” 1. Core count would grow again to 64 on Gaudi 3 5 (see that page on this site), but the jump from 8 cores on the first Gaudi chip 6 to 24 here was the larger relative increase.

5.Where it’s made

SimpleStart here

Designed by Habana Labs in Caesarea, Israel 3. Intel’s fact sheet names the process node (7 nm) but not the specific foundry 1.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Gaudi 3, the next generation, is explicitly named by Intel’s own white paper as made by TSMC (see that page on this site) — no equivalent foundry name was found published for Gaudi 2, treated here as genuinely unreported.

6.Which systems use it

SimpleStart here

Sold mainly through system builders and cloud partners rather than one flagship cloud (compare the original Gaudi’s near-exclusive AWS launch, on that page on this site). Rented through Denvr Dataworks and listed on India’s government-run IndiaAI Mission compute pool 7 8.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Stability AI’s roughly 4,000-chip Gaudi 2 supercomputer, announced September 2023, is the largest named single Gaudi 2 deployment this site found sourced 4. The IndiaAI Mission’s shared compute pool, which includes Gaudi 2, listed 34,333 GPUs in total as of May 2025 9.

7.Official pricing

SimpleStart here

Intel published a list price for a kit of eight Gaudi 2 chips on a baseboard: $65,000, offered to system makers as of June 2024 — about $8,125 per chip by our arithmetic 10.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Rental prices vary by provider. Denvr Dataworks charges $1.25 per chip-hour, reserved, with 8 chips per machine 7. On India’s IndiaAI Mission compute portal, one Gaudi 2 card (96 GB) lists at ₹57.60 an hour and a full 8-card server at ₹460.80, before GST 8.

8.Real-world performance

SimpleStart here

Intel’s own launch claim: Gaudi 2 “demonstrates 2x throughput performance over Nvidia A100 for training” 1.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Intel followed up with an independently-checkable benchmark, not just a marketing multiplier. Its MLPerf Training 2.0 submission, published 29 June 2022, reported a 36% cut in time-to-train versus an A100-80GB on ResNet-50, and a 45% cut versus an A100-40GB configuration on both ResNet-50 and BERT 2. Generation over generation, Intel reported Gaudi 2 reaching “3x speed-up in training throughput for ResNet-50 and 4.7x for BERT” compared with the first Gaudi chip 2. MLPerf is a third-party industry benchmark, though the specific figures above are Intel’s own summary of its submitted results, not an outside reviewer’s independent test.

9.What came before, what came next

SimpleStart here
Came before: the first Gaudi chip, covered on its own page on this site. Came after: Gaudi 3, covered on its own page on this site.
Deep diveThe technical detail

Gaudi 2 shipped about seven months after the first Gaudi chip reached AWS general availability — a faster generational turnaround than the roughly two years between Intel’s Habana acquisition and that first chip’s own AWS launch (see that page on this site) 1. Gaudi 3 followed almost two years later, in April 2024 11.

10.Hidden in plain sight

SimpleStart here

Intel still hadn’t put its own name on the chip when this one launched: the fact sheet is titled “Habana Gaudi2 Launch Fact Sheet,” not “Intel Gaudi 2” — that rebrand only arrived with the next generation, Gaudi 3, in April 2024 1 11.

Deep diveThe technical detail

The 2x-over-A100 claim compares Gaudi 2 to NVIDIA’s A100, which by Gaudi 2’s 2022 launch was already two years old (2020) and would itself be followed by NVIDIA’s H100 later that same year — the comparison chip, not the newest one available at launch 1.
