Apple M6: The Complete Guide

Apple’s first chip built on a 2-nanometre process — and, so far, the only Mac chip in Apple’s newest generation with no Pro, Max or Ultra sibling yet. Simple to expert, every number links to its source.

1.At a glance

SimpleStart here

M6 is Apple’s newest Mac chip, and the first one Apple has built on a 2-nanometre manufacturing process. It was announced 25 August 2026 and started shipping 22 September 2026, in one product: the redesigned Mac mini 123.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Apple’s own words: M6 is “Apple’s first state-of-the-art 2-nanometer chip” 1. Unusually, M6 launched at the base tier while the Pro, Max and Ultra tiers that same week still ran the older M5 generation — Mac mini shipped with M6 or M5 Pro, and Mac Studio shipped with M5 Max or M5 Ultra, not M6 versions of either 24. As of this page’s check date, no M6 Pro, M6 Max or M6 Ultra has been announced.

2.Launch and history

SimpleStart here

M6 only ships in one machine so far, and it is not the machine you might expect. Apple skipped putting the base M5 chip in the Mac mini entirely — the Mac mini’s last chip before M6 was M4, so Apple compares the new Mac mini against both M4 (its direct predecessor in that specific machine) and M5 (its direct predecessor as a chip generation).

Deep diveThe technical detail

This creates two genuinely different comparison sets in Apple’s own marketing material, and this page keeps them separate rather than blending them: the Mac mini release compares M6 to M4 (and, for context, to the original M1) since M5 never reached that machine 2; the M6 launch release itself, alongside M5 Ultra, compares M6 to M5 directly as chip generations 1. A reader comparing “M6 vs M5” and “new Mac mini vs old Mac mini” is reading two different sets of Apple’s own numbers.

3.What’s inside it

SimpleStart here

12-core CPU (2 super cores, 4 performance cores, 6 efficiency cores), 12-core GPU with a Neural Accelerator built into every core, and a dual 16-core Neural Engine 15.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Apple’s own quote from Sri Santhanam, its vice president of Silicon Engineering Group: “Built using the cutting-edge 2 nm process, M6 combines a new CPU complex, two additional CPU and GPU cores, a Dual 16-core Neural Engine, and more unified memory bandwidth to power through workloads with amazing energy efficiency” 1. Apple describes no multi-die construction for M6 in any material found, where it does for M5 Ultra — which “uses UltraFusion to connect two dual-die M5 Max chips to form the quad-die architecture” 4. Apple states only that M6 packs “greater transistor density,” with no specific transistor count, and gives no absolute Neural Engine TOPS figure — both “Not published by the company.”

5.Where it’s made

SimpleStart here

Apple states only “2-nanometer process technology” and, as with every M-series chip, names no foundry.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Apple’s own release never uses the word “TSMC” or “foundry” — verified directly against the launch text 1. The foundry attribution comes from analyst coverage: TrendForce headlines its report “Apple Unveils M6, Its First 2nm Chip Built by TSMC” 6, consistent with TSMC’s own public N2 process roadmap. This page states the 2nm process as Apple’s own claim and the TSMC attribution as third-party analyst reporting, not as something Apple itself has confirmed.

6.Which systems use it

SimpleStart here

As of this page’s check date, M6 ships in exactly one product: the redesigned Mac mini, available since 22 September 2026 23.

Deep diveThe technical detail

No Apple Newsroom release for an M6-equipped MacBook, iMac, iPad or Vision Pro was found. The same week’s Mac Studio refresh used M5 Max and M5 Ultra, not M6, and the same week’s higher Mac mini configuration used M5 Pro, not a higher M6 tier 42 — M6 sits only at the entry-level Mac mini today.

8.Real-world performance

SimpleStart here

Apple’s own device-level claim, M6 Mac mini vs. the previous M4 Mac mini: “up to 4x faster AI performance” and “40 percent faster CPU performance” 2. Chip-to-chip, M6 vs. M5: “up to 1.2x faster multithreaded performance” and “a nearly 30 percent increase in peak GPU compute for AI” 1.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Apple publishes two separate comparison sets, and this page keeps them apart: against the Mac mini’s own direct predecessor, M4, Apple claims “up to 13.5x faster LLM prompt processing in LM Studio when compared to Mac mini with M1, and up to 4.8x faster than M4,” plus “up to 2x faster gaming performance with ray tracing in Cyberpunk 2077: Ultimate Edition when compared to Mac mini with M4” 2. Against M5 as a chip generation, Apple claims “world’s fastest single-threaded performance” and a “10 percent increase [in memory bandwidth] over M5” 1. All of these are Apple’s own published comparisons, not independent benchmarks.

9.What came before, what came next

SimpleStart here

Came before: the M5 family (M5, M5 Pro, M5 Max, M5 Ultra), which finished rolling out the same week as M6’s announcement. Came after: nothing yet — no M6 Pro, Max, Ultra, or M7 of any kind has been announced as of this page’s check date.

Deep diveThe technical detail

M6’s launch is unusual in M-series history: it is the first time Apple has introduced a new base-tier chip generation (M6) in the same announcement window as completing the previous generation’s top tier (M5 Ultra, also announced 25 August 2026) 1. The base, Pro, Max and Ultra tiers of one M-series “generation” are, for the first time in this family’s history covered on this site, split across two different chip generations at once — M6 at the bottom, M5-generation chips everywhere above it.

10.Hidden in plain sight

SimpleStart here

The newest Apple silicon chip you can buy right now, at the entry-level Mac mini price, is built on a more advanced manufacturing process than the far more expensive M5 Ultra chip inside the flagship Mac Studio.

Deep diveThe technical detail

M6 uses Apple’s first 2-nanometre process 1. M5 Ultra, announced the same day and priced from $5,499 in Mac Studio, sits on an older node — though Apple does not say which: its M5 launch material gives “third-generation 3-nanometer” for the base M5 only 7, and the Mac Studio release states no process node for M5 Max or M5 Ultra at all 4. So the cheapest new Mac carries Apple’s newest process while its most expensive one does not, which is the reverse of the usual order — M4, by contrast, reached the top of the line late, arriving first in the entry iPad Pro in May 2024 and only later in M4 Pro and M4 Max.
