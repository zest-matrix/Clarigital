Session 9 of 12 — Money Explorer

Money Explorer · Session 9 of 12⏱ 25 min · 👦 Parent present

🎯 Saving Up Is Hard

Why waiting is difficult, and how a helper can make it easier.

🎯 Today's goal: Your child sees that saving is mostly about making a future thing feel real today, and designs something to help.

🔥 Warm Up — 2 minutes

Warm Up

Offer a deal: one biscuit now, or three biscuits after dinner. Whatever they choose, ask them why.

“Everyone finds waiting hard. Today we work out how to make it easier.”

🤖 The Activity — 15 minutes

Main Activity

Pick something imaginary they want that costs 500 pretend rupees. They get 50 a week.

Draw ten boxes. Colour one in for each week. Ask how it feels looking at ten empty boxes.

Now change one thing at a time and ask whether it helps: seeing a picture of the thing · a bar that fills up · someone cheering at the halfway point · being able to spend it any time, or not.

🧩 The Twist — 5 minutes

The Twist

Ask the sharp question: “Should the helper make it hard to spend the money early?”

Let them argue both ways. A helper that makes it impossible is bossy. One that makes it too easy is useless. Real apps make this choice for millions of people, and somebody decided it.

🎯

Saver Badge

Designed something that makes waiting easier

Parent Notes — tap to expand

▼
What they learned

That saving behaviour is shaped by design choices, not willpower alone — and that those choices are made by somebody, on purpose.

Questions to ask
  • Which change helped the most, and why?
  • Should an app be allowed to stop you spending your own money?
  • Who should decide — you, your parent, or the app?
What to watch for

Children who chose the biscuit now are not impatient — many have learned that promised future things do not always arrive. Worth noticing rather than correcting.

Safety in context

Keep the target imaginary. Tying this to a real thing they want turns a design exercise into a negotiation about buying it.
