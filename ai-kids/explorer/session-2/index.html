Session 2 of 16 — AI Explorer

Explorer · Session 2 of 16⏱ 25 min · 👨‍👩‍👧 Parent present

🎮 AI and Your Hobbies

Use AI to go deeper on something you already love.

Stuck at any point? Ask an AI about this page →
🎯 Today's goal: Your child discovers that knowing a topic well makes them a better AI user — they can direct it and catch its mistakes.

🔥 Warm Up — 2 minutes

Warm Up

What is the one thing you know more about than most adults? Sport, game, animal, music, craft — anything.

"We explore this topic with AI. And because you know it well — you are the expert, not the AI."

🤖 The Activity — 15 minutes

Main Activity

Your child is the expert. AI is the research assistant. Child evaluates every answer.

📋 Type this exactly
Tell me everything you know about [chosen topic]. Give me facts, history, and surprising details most people don't know.

Child rates each fact: ✅ correct · ❌ wrong · 🤔 not sure. Keep score on paper.

📋 Type this exactly
Tell me about [a very specific part of the topic the child knows well — a player's stats, a game mechanic, a technical detail].

This is where AI often gets niche details wrong. Full expert mode.

📋 Type this exactly
I already know a lot about [topic]. What is something related that most enthusiasts never explore but probably should?

AI expanding from what you know into adjacent areas — one of its genuine strengths.

🧩 The Twist — 5 minutes

Find the Flaw
📋 Type this exactly
Make up a completely fictional fact about [topic] that sounds totally believable — something that could fool an expert. Then tell me it is made up.

Ask: if AI had not told you — would you have believed it? This builds healthy scepticism even on topics they know well.

🛡️ Safety Moment
“AI makes mistakes — always check”

Today proved this directly. The habit: every time AI states a specific fact, ask how would I check that? On topics they know well — they are the checker.

👨‍👩‍👧 Parent: read this aloud. Ask your child to repeat it back.

Parent Notes — tap to expand

▼
What they learned

Domain expertise as an evaluation tool. Children who realise they know more than AI in their areas gain genuine confidence.

Questions to ask
  • How many facts did AI get wrong about your topic?
  • Did AI tell you anything genuinely new?
  • If someone without your knowledge read this answer — what could go wrong?
What to watch for

Watch for frustration if AI gets many things wrong. Reframe: that means they know more than AI does.

Safety in context

Low safety risk — topic is child-chosen. Ensure no personal details crept in.
