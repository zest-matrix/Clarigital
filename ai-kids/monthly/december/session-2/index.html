✉️ Writing Cards that Matter

Write messages that will actually be kept.

Stuck at any point? Ask an AI about this page →
🎯 Today's goal: Your child learns to write genuinely meaningful personal messages — using AI as an editor, not an author.

🔥 Warm Up

Warm Up

Think of the best card or message you ever received. What made it matter?

"Today you learn to write messages like that."

🤖 The Activity

Main Activity
📋 Type this exactly
What makes a written card or message genuinely meaningful — the kind that people keep for years? Give me the principles, not templates. What are the mistakes that make messages feel generic?
📋 Type this exactly
I want to write a message for [describe occasion and relationship — no real names]. Draft 3 completely different versions: one warm and emotional, one funny and light, one that focuses on a specific shared memory. Show me how different they can be.
📋 Type this exactly
Take the version I liked best and improve it. Make it more specific, more personal, more like something only I could write — not something that could have been sent by anyone to anyone.

🧩 The Twist

Go Deeper
📋 Type this exactly
Can AI write a card that would make someone cry — in a good way? Try it. Then explain: if AI can produce something that emotional, what does that tell us about where genuine emotion in writing actually comes from?
🛡️ Safety Moment
“AI is a tool, not a friend”

A card AI writes is not from you. A card you write — even imperfectly — is. The specific memory, the specific observation about who that person is, the specific words you choose because they sound like you: those are what make a message a gift.

Parent Notes

▼
What they learned

Personal communication as a form of care.

Questions to ask
  • What made your final message feel personal?
  • What would make it even more specific?
  • Would you rather receive a perfect AI-written card or an imperfect hand-written one?
What to watch for

The best outcome is that the child actually sends the message they wrote.

Safety in context
