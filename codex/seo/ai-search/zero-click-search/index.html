Zero-Click Search

A zero-click search is one where the user gets what they needed on the results page. The share has been rising for years and AI answers have accelerated it.

What Causes Zero-Click

Google has steadily added surfaces that answer directly: featured snippets, knowledge panels, weather and currency widgets, sports scores, business hours, People Also Ask, and now AI Overviews.

Each is a rational product decision — the user wanted a fact and got it faster. Collectively they remove clicks from publishers who supplied the underlying information.

The effect is uneven. Queries whose answer is a single fact are heavily affected. Queries requiring judgement, comparison, a tool, or a transaction are much less so.

Which Queries Lose Clicks

High exposure: definitions, conversions and calculations, simple facts, opening hours, contact details, and anything a widget can answer.

Low exposure: purchase queries, queries needing a tool or calculator, comparison of options with personal criteria, queries requiring downloads or templates, and anything where the user needs to interact rather than read.

The strategic implication follows directly. Content built entirely around simple factual queries is structurally exposed. Content that requires the user to reach the page is not.

Adapting the Strategy

Shift the content mix. Move effort toward content whose value cannot be summarised — tools, calculators, interactive comparisons, templates, original data, and genuinely opinionated analysis.

Accept snippet capture where the brand value is worth it. Being the cited source on a high-volume informational query has awareness value even without the click. Treat it as a brand impression.

Target deeper in the journey. The query "what is X" may not generate a click. "Best X for Y situation" still does.

Build direct relationships. Email, community and returning visitors are unaffected by what the results page does. This is the most durable response.

Measuring in a Zero-Click World

Clicks alone increasingly understate search value. Track impressions as a visibility metric in its own right, monitor branded search volume as a proxy for awareness generated by uncited exposure, and segment performance by query intent so that informational decline does not mask commercial health.

A falling click-through rate with stable impressions on informational queries is an expected pattern, not necessarily a problem to fix.

What Not to Do

Deliberately withholding the answer to force a click does not work. Users bounce, engagement signals worsen, and the snippet gets awarded to a competitor who answered properly.

Blocking snippet display via nosnippet or max-snippet is available and almost always counterproductive — it removes the visibility without recovering the click.

Measuring a Channel That Stopped Sending Clicks

The reporting problem is structural: impressions rise, clicks do not, and the standard metrics say the channel is failing when it may be working differently.

What to change in how you read the data:

Stop using click-through rate as a health measure on informational queries. A falling CTR on a query whose answer is now shown in the results is expected, not a defect, and optimising the title to recover it is usually wasted effort.

Segment queries by type. Informational, navigational and transactional behave completely differently under zero-click, and a blended CTR conceals that transactional performance may be entirely unchanged.

Watch branded search volume and direct traffic as the indirect signal that visibility without clicks is still doing something.

Track position and impressions on the queries that matter commercially, and accept impressions as a partial outcome on the rest.

And separate what you can influence from what you cannot. A query answered completely in the results is not winnable, and a strategy that keeps spending on it is spending against the platform rather than the competition.

Where the Clicks Still Are

Zero-click is uneven rather than universal, and the response is to move effort toward the queries that still convert into visits.

Queries with no short answer — comparisons, decisions with trade-offs, anything where the useful response is a judgement rather than a fact.

Queries needing a tool: calculators, configurators, checkers. The answer surface can describe the calculation; it cannot do it with the user's numbers.

Transactional intent, where the user has to arrive somewhere to complete the action.

Queries where currency matters and the visible answer may be stale — pricing, availability, regulation, anything dated.

And anything requiring trust in a source, where a professional reader wants to see who says it before acting on it.

The strategic move is not to fight for clicks on answered questions. It is to shift the content mix toward questions that cannot be answered in a box, and to accept presence without a visit as a legitimate outcome on the rest — while being honest that a business model funded by pageviews on informational content is under genuine pressure, and no optimisation resolves that.
