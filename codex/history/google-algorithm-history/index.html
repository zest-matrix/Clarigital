Google Algorithm History · Major Updates 2003–2024

Google makes thousands of changes to Search every year — it reports more than 4,000 improvements in 2023 alone (Google) — but a few have reshaped how sites rank. This history covers the major ones, using Google’s own announcements and its ranking systems guide, and marks the names coined by the SEO industry.

Panda: February 2011

On 24 February 2011 Google announced a change “designed to reduce rankings for low-quality sites”, saying it “noticeably impacts 11.8% of our queries” (Google, Feb 2011). The announcement did not name it; it became known as Panda, and Google’s ranking systems guide says the Panda system “evolved and became part of our core ranking systems in 2015” (Google ranking systems guide).

Google said the change aimed to give better rankings to high-quality sites with original content. Google’s current guidance on helpful content keeps the same spirit as a list of self-assessment questions, for example “Does the content provide original information, reporting, research, or analysis?” (Google Search Central).

Penguin: April 2012

On 24 April 2012 Google launched a change targeting webspam, from “keyword stuffing” to “link schemes that attempt to propel sites higher in rankings”, which “affects about 3.1% of queries in English” (Google, Apr 2012). Google’s ranking systems guide lists it as the Penguin system, “designed to combat link spam”.

In September 2016 Google announced “Penguin is now part of our core algorithm” (Google Search Central, Sep 2016); the ranking systems guide says it “was integrated into our core ranking systems in 2016” (Google ranking systems guide).

Hummingbird: August 2013

Hummingbird was not a penalty but a change to how Google’s whole ranking system works. Google describes it as “a major improvement to our overall ranking systems made in August 2013” (Google ranking systems guide). Google’s 15th-birthday post of 26 September 2013, which does not name Hummingbird, described search features for questions without a simple answer and for asking “questions aloud with voice search” (Google, Sep 2013).

In this guide’s reading, the shift built on the Knowledge Graph, launched in May 2012 to understand “things, not strings” (Google, May 2012): Google moved further from matching individual keywords towards understanding what a whole query means.

Mobile-Friendly Ranking: April 2015

In February 2015 Google gave advance notice that mobile-friendliness would become a ranking signal that “will affect mobile searches in all languages worldwide” (Google, Feb 2015); it began rolling out on 21 April 2015 (Google, Apr 2015). The SEO industry nicknamed it “Mobilegeddon”. Google’s April post described it as a change to rankings on mobile phones.

In November 2016 Google announced mobile-first indexing (Google Search Central, Nov 2016), under which Google would primarily use the mobile version of a page for indexing and ranking, and in May 2019 it announced mobile-first indexing by default for new domains (Google Search Central, May 2019).

RankBrain: 2015

Google says it launched RankBrain in 2015 and that “it was the first deep learning system deployed in Search”, helping it understand how words relate to concepts (Google, Feb 2022). That matters because, as Google noted in 2019, “15 percent of those queries are ones we haven’t seen before” (Google, Oct 2019).

The August 2018 Core Update (“Medic”)

The SEO industry nicknamed a broad core update of August 2018 “Medic”; the name is not Google’s. In the industry’s interpretation, the update drew attention to E-A-T (Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness), a concept from Google’s Search Quality Rater Guidelines.

Google explained in 2019 that raters “are trained to understand if content has what we call strong E-A-T”, but “Rater data is not used directly in our ranking algorithms” (Google Webmaster Central Blog, Aug 2019). In December 2022 Google added Experience, making E-A-T into E-E-A-T (Google Search Central, Dec 2022).

BERT: October 2019

On 25 October 2019 Google said BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers) was helping Search understand language, “representing the biggest leap forward in the past five years” (Google, Oct 2019). BERT models “process words in relation to all the other words in a sentence, rather than one-by-one in order”.

Google’s example was a query about whether you can get medicine for someone at a pharmacy: “With the BERT model, we can better understand that ‘for someone’ is an important part of this query.” At launch it applied to “one in 10 searches in the U.S. in English”, and Google also used a BERT model to improve featured snippets (same post). By 2022 Google said BERT “plays a critical role in almost every English query” (Google, Feb 2022).

Broad Core Updates

Google has announced significant ranking changes since at least 2011 (the changes later known as Panda and Penguin, and the 2015 mobile-friendly update). Today it gives notice of major core updates on its Search Status Dashboard; core updates they “don’t target specific sites or individual web pages”, and Google advises site owners to “Avoid doing ‘quick fix’ changes” (Google Search Central). As it put it in 2019, “There’s nothing wrong with pages that may perform less well in a core update” (Google Webmaster Central Blog, Aug 2019). In this guide’s reading, other content may simply have been judged more relevant.

The Helpful Content System: 2022–2024

Google’s helpful content update began rolling out on 25 August 2022 (Google Search Status Dashboard). The ranking systems guide says the system was “designed to better ensure people see original, helpful content written by people, for people”, “rather than content made primarily to gain search engine traffic” (Google ranking systems guide). In February 2023 Google explained “how AI-generated content fits into our long-standing approach to show helpful content” (Google Search Central, Feb 2023).

In September 2023 Google “Released the September 2023 helpful content update, which improves our classifier” (Google Search Status Dashboard, Sep 2023). In March 2024 the helpful content system “evolved and became part of our core ranking systems” (Google ranking systems guide), and Google said the March 2024 core update and its earlier efforts “will collectively reduce low-quality, unoriginal content in search results by 40%”; after the rollout it reported “45% less low-quality, unoriginal content” (Google, Mar 2024, updated Apr 2024).

Earlier versions contained errors — including the month of Hummingbird, an invented BERT example, the claim that the helpful content system joined Google’s core systems in 2023, and a misquoted March 2024 figure — and many unsourced claims, which have been corrected or removed.
