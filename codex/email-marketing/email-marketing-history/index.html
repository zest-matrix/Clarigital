Email Marketing · Session 9, Guide 1

Email Marketing History · 1971 to AI Personalisation 2026

Email is the oldest digital marketing channel and still among the highest-ROI. From Ray Tomlinson's first network message in 1971 through the spam crisis of the early 2000s, the CAN-SPAM Act, the mobile revolution, marketing automation, and today's AI-driven personalisation — this guide documents every major milestone that shaped the email marketing we practise today.

What You Will Learn

  • How spam destroyed early email marketing's effectiveness and trust

1971–1990: Origins of Email

Ray Tomlinson sent the first networked email in 1971 using ARPANET — the precursor to the internet — sending a message between two computers in the same room using the @ symbol to separate the user name from the host machine. His choice of @ established the convention that persists in every email address today.

The first email sent for marketing purposes is generally attributed to Gary Thuerk, a Digital Equipment Corporation marketing manager who sent an unsolicited promotional email to 400 ARPANET users in 1978 — advertising DEC computers. The message generated significant complaints from recipients unused to commercial messages in a network they considered academic and professional. Thuerk has since been called "the father of spam" — though his email was more accurately the first bulk commercial email, predating the modern spam phenomenon by two decades.

YearMilestone
1971Ray Tomlinson sends first networked email via ARPANET; establishes @ convention
1978First bulk commercial email sent by Gary Thuerk at DEC; 400 recipients
1982SMTP (Simple Mail Transfer Protocol) standardised — RFC 821 published
1988MIME (Multipurpose Internet Mail Extensions) developed — enables non-text content in emails

1990–2003: The Commercial Email Era

The commercialisation of the internet in the early 1990s brought email to the mainstream. Hotmail (1996) and Yahoo Mail (1997) made free web-based email accessible to millions of non-technical users. By the late 1990s, marketing departments at major corporations were building email lists and sending HTML-formatted promotional emails to hundreds of thousands of subscribers.

YearMilestone
1991Internet opened to commercial use; email marketing becomes viable for businesses
1996Hotmail launches — free web email; rapid adoption among consumers
1996First HTML email marketing campaigns — images and formatting in commercial email
1998First email service providers (ESPs) emerge as dedicated infrastructure for bulk email sending
1999Melissa virus spreads via email — first major malware distributed through email
2000Dotcom boom drives aggressive email acquisition; list renting and purchasing widespread

2000–2005: The Spam Crisis

By 2001, unsolicited commercial email — spam — represented over 50% of all email traffic. The dotcom crash had left thousands of failed companies with large email lists and no ethical constraints on how to monetise them. Spam volumes accelerated throughout 2002–2004, with some estimates placing spam at 80–85% of global email traffic by 2004.

The spam crisis had two lasting consequences for legitimate email marketers: inbox placement became difficult even for permission-based senders (ISPs deployed aggressive filtering that caught legitimate email alongside spam), and users' trust in commercial email collapsed — causing open rates across the industry to fall sharply.

2003–2010: The Regulation Era

The US CAN-SPAM Act (Controlling the Assault of Non-Solicited Pornography And Marketing Act) was signed into law on December 16, 2003, establishing the first federal standards for commercial email in the United States. CAN-SPAM set requirements for commercial email: accurate header information, clear identification as an advertisement, physical postal address, and a functional opt-out mechanism honoured within 10 business days.

YearMilestone
2003US CAN-SPAM Act signed into law; sets baseline commercial email requirements
2003Gmail launches (April 2004 technically, widely considered 2003 development) — Google's entry changes webmail expectations
2004SPF (Sender Policy Framework) published as email authentication standard
2007DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail) published — cryptographic email authentication
2008iPhone App Store launches — beginning of mobile email reading
2009Litmus founded — professional email testing across clients becomes possible

2010–2018: The Mobile Revolution

The introduction of smartphones — particularly the iPhone (2007) and Android (2008) — fundamentally changed how people read email. By 2013, mobile surpassed desktop as the dominant email reading environment for the first time. Email designs built for 600px-wide desktop clients were unreadable on mobile screens — forcing the industry to develop responsive email design.

YearMilestone
2012Responsive email design becomes standard practice — media queries for email
2013Mobile email opens surpass desktop for the first time (Litmus data)
2015Google introduces email tabs (Primary, Social, Promotions) — changes inbox placement strategy
2016Apple Mail Privacy Protection precursor — privacy-first email environment begins
2018GDPR enacted — transforms opt-in requirements and data handling for EU email marketing

2015–2022: The Automation Era

Marketing automation platforms — Mailchimp, Klaviyo, HubSpot, ActiveCampaign, Salesforce Marketing Cloud — matured during this period, making sophisticated trigger-based email sequences accessible to businesses of all sizes. Behavioural triggers (abandoned cart, browse abandonment, post-purchase), lifecycle automation (welcome series, win-back sequences), and predictive send-time optimisation became standard capabilities.

YearMilestone
2016Apple Mail Privacy Protection — open rate tracking reliability begins to degrade
2018GDPR enforcement begins — explicit consent requirement for EU subscribers
2019DMARC adoption accelerates — major mailbox providers enforce DMARC policies
2021Apple Mail Privacy Protection (MPP) launched with iOS 15 — opens tracking severely impacted
2021Google BIMI support — brand logos in Gmail for authenticated senders

2022–2026: The AI Era

AI and machine learning have progressively entered email marketing at multiple levels: generative AI for subject line and body copy creation; predictive analytics for churn risk and purchase probability; dynamic content personalisation at the individual level; and send-time optimisation that determines the optimal send moment per subscriber based on historical engagement patterns.

YearMilestone
2022Gmail and Yahoo tighten bulk sender requirements; DMARC becomes effectively mandatory
2023Generative AI email copy tools become mainstream; ChatGPT integration in ESPs
2024Google and Yahoo enforce bulk sender requirements — SPF, DKIM, DMARC, one-click unsubscribe mandatory for 5,000+ daily senders
2025AI-driven personalisation at per-subscriber level — subject, content, product recommendations, send time all individually optimised
2026Interactive AMP emails expand; email as a two-way channel rather than one-way broadcast
