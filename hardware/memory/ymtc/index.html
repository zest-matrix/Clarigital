YMTC: The Complete Guide

The only maker in this section where most of what is known comes from governments and courts rather than from the company. What YMTC actually publishes, what the US filings document, and what is only estimate. Three reading levels, every number sourced and labelled.

1.At a glance

SimpleStart here

YMTC is China's best-known maker of NAND flash memory. In its own words it was "established in Wuhan, China in July 2016" and is "an IDM focusing on the design and manufacturing of 3D NAND flash memory" 1 — IDM meaning it both designs and manufactures, rather than using someone else's fab.

What it makes3D NAND flash, and Zhitai-brand consumer SSDs 1 2
Signature technologyXtacking, its own bonded-wafer architecture, from 2018 3
US statusOn the Entity List since 16 December 2022 4
How much it publishesVery little — no financials, no capacity, no fab count 1
DeeperThe detail

This page is built differently from the others in this section, because YMTC is. Samsung, SK hynix, Micron, Kioxia and SanDisk all publish audited accounts on their own sites, name their plants and describe their capacity. YMTC's own site carries none of that — a company description, a technology explanation, product specifications and consumer SSD pages, and almost nothing else 1 3 5. There is no leadership page, no financial statement, no fab list, and no comment on its own regulatory position. Its August 2026 stock-exchange filing is the first document that should contain those things, and it is discussed below 6.

So most of what can be said with confidence about YMTC comes from two other places: US government documents, which are detailed and precise, and US court dockets, where YMTC is currently suing the Commerce Department, the Bureau of Industry and Security, the Department of Defense and Micron 4 7 8 9. This page separates those sources from company statements throughout, and says plainly where a widely-repeated figure has no official basis at all.

ExpertFor specialists

That may be changing. In May 2026 a holding company, 长江存储控股股份有限公司, entered IPO counselling with CITIC Securities, filed with the China Securities Regulatory Commission's Hubei bureau 10, and on 21 August 2026 the Shanghai Stock Exchange accepted a STAR Market listing application 6. A Chinese IPO prospectus would, for the first time, put audited financials, ownership, capacity and risk disclosures into a public filing. This page does not quote figures from that prospectus, because this check could not open the document itself — the figures circulating are media reports of it, and some look anomalous enough to warrant reading the original before repeating them.

DeeperThe detail

YMTC publishes no ownership information and no board or leadership page. The most authoritative public statement of who controls it is therefore American: the US Department of Defense, designating YMTC under Section 1260H, states that "YMTC is indirectly owned by SASAC and is indirectly affiliated with MIIT and SASTIND" 11 — SASAC being the Chinese state's asset-supervision commission, MIIT the industry ministry, and SASTIND the defence-industry administration.

The IPO process has surfaced one shareholding figure through an official Chinese channel: a Yangtze Daily report on the first-phase counselling filing, carried on the Wuhan municipal government's portal, gives 湖北长晟发展有限责任公司 at 26.5442%, wholly controlled by the Wuhan Optics Valley Management Committee 12. Other shareholding percentages circulating in Chinese financial media are not repeated here.

ExpertFor specialists

YMTC's executives are known only from press releases in which they are quoted, and the list is thin and dated: Simon Yang as CEO in 2018 13, Cheng Weihua as Co-CTO in 2019 14, Thomas Chen as Executive Vice President in 2022 15, and Dr. Zongliang Huo as Chief Scientist and Titus Lo as SVP in 2025 16. There is no current officers list on ymtc.com, and this page does not name a chairman, because no company or regulatory source found in this check does.

The English-language newsroom is itself a data point: it holds nine items spanning 2018 to 2025, with a three-year gap between July 2022 and August 2025 17. The Chinese newsroom is considerably more active but is almost entirely consumer-SSD marketing 18.

3.The story

SimpleStart here
  • July 2016 Founded in Wuhan 1.
  • 2018 Xtacking introduced, and debuted at the Flash Memory Summit 13 17.
  • Sep 2019 64-layer 256Gb TLC enters mass production 14.
  • 2020 128-layer QLC and TLC announced — the last layer count YMTC has published 19.
  • Oct 2022 Added to the US Unverified List 20.
  • Dec 2022 Added to the US Entity List, effective 16 December 4.
  • 2023–2026 Sues Micron, then the Commerce Department and the Pentagon 9 7 8.
  • Aug 2026 STAR Market IPO application accepted 6.
DeeperThe detail

The arc is unusually compressed. YMTC went from founding to 64-layer mass production in three years 1 14, and to 128-layer products in four 19. It then hit the US export-control regime at exactly the point where it was becoming competitive, and the technical story goes quiet in public at the same moment the legal one begins.

ExpertFor specialists

The two threads are related in a way the dates make visible. The October 2022 US rule defines "advanced-node integrated circuits" to include "NOT AND (NAND) memory integrated circuits with 128 layers or more" 21 — a threshold YMTC had publicly crossed in April 2020 19. Two months after that rule, YMTC was on the Entity List 4. It has published no layer count since 22 5. This page does not assert that the second fact is caused by the first; it notes that YMTC published layer counts routinely up to 2020 and has not published one since, and leaves the reader to weigh that against the regulatory threshold being set at the number it had reached.

Notice what the product table does not have: a layer count in any row. YMTC identifies its generations by part number and Xtacking version — X3 with Xtacking 3.0, X4 with Xtacking 4.0 22 5 — and by density, interface speed and plane count. The X3-9070 launch release says it "achieves an I/O speed of up to 2400MT/s, ONFI 5.0 compliant" and offers "1Tb storage capacity in an ultra-compact mono-die footprint" with a six-plane design — and never states how many layers the die has 15.

For the generation step YMTC quantifies density instead: the X4's bit density is described as "increased by 36% compared to the previous X3 generation" 5.

The consumer line is where YMTC publishes most. The Zhitai TiPlus7100 page carries a full specification table: 512GB to 4TB, M.2 2280, "PCIe™ Gen4 x4, NVMe™ 1.4" (NVMe 2.0 at 4TB), sequential read 7000MB/s, sequential write from 3600 to 6000MB/s, 4K random read 800K to 900K IOPS, MTBF of 150万小时, TBW from 300 to 2400, a five-year warranty, and NAND described as 长江存储新一代TLC闪存颗粒 on 晶栈®Xtacking® 3.0 2. That is a more complete specification than several Western makers publish for equivalent drives — the reticence is about the company and the fabs, not the products.

5.How it’s made

SimpleStart here

Xtacking is YMTC's own architecture and the thing it explains most fully. Instead of building the control circuitry beside or beneath the memory array, YMTC builds the two on separate wafers and bonds them together 3.

  1. One wafer gets the memory cell array; another gets the CMOS control logic 3.
  2. The two are then bonded: "Memory cells and peripheral circuits are bonded by billions of metal VIAs on a die the size of a fingernail" 3.
  3. The logic ends up above the array rather than taking up space next to it 3.
DeeperThe detail

YMTC's stated reason is area and time. "In the conventional 3D NAND architecture, the periphery circuits take up ~20-30% of the die area, lowering NAND bit density", where Xtacking puts them above the array instead; YMTC credits the approach with "reducing product development time by at least three months and shortening manufacturing cycle time by 20%" 3. YMTC positions it against two named predecessors: "Before the launch of Xtacking® architecture, 3D NAND architectures in the market were divided into traditional side-by-side structure and CnA (CMOS next to Array) architecture" 3.

The original 2018 pitch was about speed: "With our Xtacking® technology, it is possible for NAND I/O speed to reach up to 3.0Gbps, similar to I/O speed of DRAM DDR4", said Simon Yang, then CEO 13.

ExpertFor specialists

Versions are numbered and dated by YMTC itself: Xtacking 2.0 debuted at IC China in September 2019 17, 3.0 carries the X3 generation 15, and 4.0 won a "Most Innovative Technology Award in the 3D NAND Flash Memory category at the Future of Memory and Storage (FMS) 2025", where YMTC also described a "Super Contact" (SCT) architecture 16.

A deliberate omission on this page: Xtacking is conceptually close to the bonded-wafer approaches Kioxia and SanDisk call CBA, and readers will notice the resemblance. But YMTC's own material names only "side-by-side" and "CnA" as the prior alternatives and never mentions CBA, Kioxia or any competitor 3, and no official source found here compares them. So this page describes each company's architecture in its own words and does not assert that they are the same technique.

6.Where it’s made: fabs and addresses

SimpleStart here

YMTC manufactures in Wuhan, Hubei province. Beyond that, it publishes almost nothing about its own factories.

Its About page gives a founding city and R&D locations, and no fab count, no capacity figure, no wafer-start number and no investment total 1. There is no facilities or operations page on ymtc.com.

DeeperThe detail

The fullest official list of YMTC's addresses is, again, American. The Federal Register notice adding it to the Entity List records corporate addresses across Wuhan, Shenzhen, Shanghai and Hong Kong, and separately lists Yangtze Memory Technologies (Japan) Inc. at "New Tokyo Building 2F, 3-3-1 Marunouchi, Chiyoda-ku, Tokyo 100-0005, Japan", together with a third listed entity, Hefei Core Storage Electronic Ltd. 4.

ExpertFor specialists

What is publicly documented about YMTC's expansion comes through the IPO process and provincial channels rather than from the company. A Yangtze Daily article on the counselling filing, reprinted on the Wuhan municipal portal, refers to 232-layer mass production and a 294-layer process breakthrough 12 — figures YMTC itself has never published 5. This page cites that as a government-hosted newspaper report, which is what it is: better than anonymous analysis, and not a company or regulatory disclosure.

Everything else circulating about YMTC's capacity — a third Wuhan fab, a 2027 start, wafers-per-month targets, global bit-share rankings — comes from press and analyst estimates that this check could not tie to any primary source. None of it appears on this page as fact. If the STAR Market prospectus becomes readable, capacity and site detail is exactly what it would settle 6.

7.How the memory works

SimpleStart here

NAND flash stores each bit as trapped charge in a cell that keeps it without power. Stacking those cells vertically is how modern flash gets its capacity, and bonding the control logic on from a second wafer — YMTC's Xtacking — is how the die area is kept down 3.

DeeperThe detail

YMTC ships both TLC, three bits per cell, in its X4-9070 and X3-9070 parts, and QLC, four bits per cell, in the X4-6080 at 2Tb 5 16 22. The trade is the standard one: more bits per cell means more capacity from the same silicon and less endurance.

ExpertFor specialists

The engineering claim Xtacking rests on is that separating the two wafers lets each be optimised independently — the logic on a process suited to logic, the array on one suited to memory — and that this is what buys the development-time and cycle-time gains YMTC quotes 3. It is the same underlying insight that Kioxia and SanDisk describe for CBA in their own words, and it is why bonded-wafer approaches have become the mainstream direction in NAND. YMTC got there early: Xtacking debuted in 2018 13, several years before the bonded generations its competitors now ship.

Two of YMTC's own numbers make the generational progression measurable without layer counts: interface speed has gone 1.6Gbps at 128-layer 19, to 2,400MT/s at X3 15, to 3,600MT/s at X4 5; and the X4 carries a stated 36% bit-density gain over X3 5. That is enough to track the trajectory even though the physical layer count is withheld.

ExpertFor specialists

The "232-layer" figure attached to the X3-9070 across the industry is not a YMTC claim. The launch release and the product page both describe the part as Gen4 on Xtacking 3.0 and state no layer count anywhere 15 22. The 232 figure originates in teardown analysis and press reporting. The closest thing to an official Chinese reference is the Wuhan government portal's counselling report mentioning 232-layer mass production 12 — a newspaper article on a government site, not a disclosure. This page reports the number's provenance rather than the number, because on a page about a company this opaque, where a figure comes from is the more useful fact.

9.Official pricing

SimpleStart here

YMTC publishes no prices — not for NAND chips, and not for its own consumer SSDs. Its Chinese SSD page carries full specifications and, in place of a price, a note directing buyers to its JD and Tmall flagship stores 2.

DeeperThe detail

The page's own wording is: 网站升级维护，如需购买请移步至"致态(ZhiTai)长江存储京东自营旗舰店"或"致态天猫官方旗舰店" 2 — site under maintenance, please go to the Zhitai JD self-operated flagship store or the Zhitai Tmall official flagship store. Those are company-operated sales channels and do carry live prices, but a price read there is a dated retail observation from a shop, not a published manufacturer price, so none is quoted here.

ExpertFor specialists

Nothing resembling contract or OEM NAND pricing is published by YMTC anywhere 1 5. In that respect it is no different from Samsung, SK hynix, Micron or Kioxia, none of which price chips publicly either — the memory industry sells on negotiated contracts throughout. The difference has been that the others publish audited revenue, so the aggregate is visible even when the unit price is not. YMTC published neither on its own site; its August 2026 IPO filing should change the revenue half of that, once the prospectus can be read 6.

10.The HBM position

SimpleStart here

YMTC makes no DRAM and no HBM. It makes NAND flash only 1. China's DRAM maker is a different company, CXMT, covered on its own page in this section.

DeeperThe detail

Because HBM is built from DRAM, a NAND-only maker has no route into it without entering an entirely different business — the same structural position Kioxia and SanDisk are in. The difference is that both of those have publicly stated AI-memory strategies: Kioxia with fast flash and CXL modules, SanDisk with High Bandwidth Flash standardised through the Open Compute Project. This check found no YMTC statement of an AI memory strategy at all, and no YMTC involvement in HBF or any comparable initiative 17 18.

ExpertFor specialists

There is a regulatory dimension here that the other makers do not face. US export controls define "advanced-node integrated circuits" to include NAND at "128 layers or more" 21, and Entity List placement means a licence requirement "For all items subject to the EAR" with a "Presumption of denial" 4. The practical effect is on what equipment YMTC can buy, which bears directly on how far it can push the density and process sophistication that an AI-oriented flash product would need. Whether YMTC is developing anything in this direction is not something this page can answer from any source found: the company has not said, and no filing or court document addresses it.

11.Who buys it and why

SimpleStart here

YMTC names no customers. Its own channels sell Zhitai-branded consumer SSDs in China 2; beyond that, who buys its NAND is not something the company discloses 1.

DeeperThe detail

The one customer relationship documented anywhere on the record is a negative one, and it is documented by the United States Senate rather than by either party. A July 2026 letter from seven senators to Apple's chief executive states: "Apple confronted a version of this very choice in 2022, when it evaluated YMTC NAND for the iPhone and, after congressional scrutiny, thought better of it" 23. The same letter urges Apple to "abandon any effort to incorporate memory from CXMT, Yangtze Memory Technologies Co. (YMTC), or any other Chinese state-backed supplier into any of its products" 23.

Neither Apple nor YMTC has confirmed any of this. This check found no statement from either company about a supply relationship, its evaluation, or its ending 17. The Senate's account is the only institutional one on the record, and this page attributes it to the Senate rather than treating it as established fact about Apple's purchasing.

ExpertFor specialists

There is also a legislated future restriction on one class of buyer. The Senate letter records that "Section 5949 of the FY 2023 NDAA barred federal agencies from acquiring products that use CXMT and YMTC semiconductors beginning in December 2027" 23 — a procurement bar that reaches not just YMTC's direct sales but any product containing its chips. For a memory maker, whose output typically reaches end users inside someone else's device, that is a materially wider restriction than it first appears.

12.Hidden in plain sight

SimpleStart here

YMTC stopped publishing layer counts. It announced 64-layer parts in 2019 and 128-layer parts in 2020, both with the layer count in the headline 14 19. Every generation since — X3, X4 — has been announced without one 15 5.

DeeperThe detail

The 2020 announcement is specific in a way nothing since has been: "128-layer" stated outright, with the X2-6070 at 1.33Tb QLC and the X2-9060 at 512Gb TLC, and a count of "more than 366 billion effective charge-trap memory cells" 19. Compare the X3-9070 launch two years later, which gives interface speed, density, plane count and ONFI compliance — and no layer figure at all 15.

The context is that in October 2022 the United States wrote a layer count into law. That rule took effect on 13 October 2022, as its own Federal Register comment notice records 24, and the definition it introduced survives in current regulation: 15 CFR 772.1 defines advanced-node integrated circuits to include "NOT AND (NAND) memory integrated circuits with 128 layers or more" 21.

ExpertFor specialists

The second thing hidden in plain sight is who is doing the disclosing. On every other page in this section, the company is the primary source and governments are supplementary. Here it is inverted: YMTC's registered address, its corporate family, its ownership, its Japanese subsidiary and its regulatory status are all documented by the US Federal Register and the Department of Defense 4 11, while YMTC's own site carries none of it 1.

And YMTC is now using the US courts to contest exactly those documents — suing the Commerce Department and BIS over the Entity List 7, the Department of Defense over the 1260H designation 8, and Commerce again under the Freedom of Information Act 25. A company that publishes almost nothing about itself is litigating in an open docket system that publishes a great deal. Those filings may end up being the most detailed public account of YMTC that exists.

13.Weak spots

SimpleStart here
  • Cut off from US technology — Entity List licensing carries a "Presumption of denial" 4.
  • A US federal procurement ban on products containing its chips from December 2027 23.
  • Published almost nothing about itself until its 2026 IPO filing, which this check could not open 1 6.
DeeperThe detail

The Entity List restriction is unusually broad. The Federal Register entry sets the licence requirement as "For all items subject to the EAR" with a review policy of "Presumption of denial" 4, and BIS explains that such listing imposes "additional license requirements on, and limit[s] the availability of, most license exceptions" 26. For a business whose competitiveness depends on buying the newest lithography, deposition and etch equipment, that is the central constraint on everything else in this page.

The stated reason was not a technology-transfer finding but a diversion risk: the agencies "determined to add Yangtze Memory Technologies Co., Ltd.…for posing a significant risk of becoming involved in activities contrary to the national security or foreign policy interests of the United States", based on "information indicating that these companies present a risk of diversion to parties on the Entity List, to include Huawei Technologies Co., Ltd., and Hangzhou Hikvision Digital Technology Co., Ltd." 4. BIS drew the distinction explicitly when it announced the action, noting YMTC was "being added to the Entity List for reasons not related to the prevention of an end-use check" — that is, not for the inspection failure that had put it on the Unverified List two months earlier 27.

ExpertFor specialists

YMTC's public position is that this determination was wrongly made, and it is pursuing that in three separate actions: against Commerce, BIS, the End-User Review Committee and the Commerce Secretary over the listing itself 7; against the Department of Defense over the 1260H military-company designation 8; and a Freedom of Information Act suit against Commerce and BIS, presumably seeking the record behind the decision 25. It is separately in patent litigation with Micron, which it initiated in November 2023 and which remains stayed pending inter partes review 9 28; Micron's own declaratory action against YMTC was dismissed in March 2026 29.

The disclosure weakness compounds all of this. Where Micron's constraints can be read in a 10-K and Kioxia's in an annual securities report, YMTC's can only be read in the documents of the government restricting it — which is a poor basis for any reader trying to judge the company on its merits, and is a limitation of this page as much as of the company.

14.India angle

SimpleStart here

Nothing. This check found no connection between YMTC and India of any kind — no plant, no office, no joint venture, no supply agreement, and no India Semiconductor Mission involvement 1.

DeeperThe detail

No YMTC page mentions India 1 17. Targeted searches for YMTC or 长江存储 against India, Gujarat and the India Semiconductor Mission returned nothing connecting them. This is an absence found by searching rather than a formal exclusion, and it is stated as such.

ExpertFor specialists

The absence is unsurprising in context. India's semiconductor incentive programme has been built in close alignment with the United States and its partners, and YMTC sits on both the US Entity List 4 and the Department of Defense's 1260H list 11. Of the memory makers covered in this section so far, Micron alone has manufacturing in India.

15.What’s next

SimpleStart here

What is actually on the record:

  • An IPO application accepted by the Shanghai Stock Exchange on 21 August 2026 6.
  • Three lawsuits against the US government, all live or recently active 7 8 25.
  • A federal procurement bar on products containing its chips, from December 2027 23.

YMTC has announced no product roadmap publicly beyond the current X4 generation 5.

DeeperThe detail

The listing is the development most likely to change what can be known about this company. A STAR Market prospectus would put audited financials, shareholding, capacity, site detail and risk disclosures into a document anyone can read — the material every other page in this section is built from, and which does not currently exist for YMTC. The application was accepted in August 2026 6; it had not listed as of this check.

ExpertFor specialists

The litigation is the other open question, and its outcomes are genuinely consequential rather than procedural. A successful challenge to the Entity List placement would restore YMTC's access to US-origin equipment; an unsuccessful one leaves a "Presumption of denial" in place indefinitely 4 7. The 1260H challenge and the December 2027 procurement bar run on their own tracks 8 23.

This page will be more useful when the prospectus is readable, and it says so rather than filling the gap. Of everything covered here, the single document that would most improve it is one YMTC has now filed and this check could not open 6.
