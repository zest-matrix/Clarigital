Corsair Dominator Titanium DDR5: The Complete Guide

1.At a glance

SimpleStart here

Dominator Titanium DDR5 is Corsair’s flagship memory line, announced 30 May 2023 as a scarce, individually numbered launch edition rated up to 8000MT/s 1. It has since grown into a regular, directly priced product line sold in multiple speeds, capacities and colours on Corsair’s own store 2.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Corsair’s launch release describes Dominator Titanium DDR5 running “up to 8,000MT/s,” with a demonstration unit shown at 8,266MT/s, in kits up to 192GB (four 48GB modules), “CAS latencies as low as 30 on some SKUs,” and dual certification “supporting Intel XMP 3.0 when paired with 12th and 13th-Gen Core processors or AMD EXPO for Ryzen 7000 CPUs” 1. That first wave was “Limited to just 500 kits and individually numbered,” available from July 2023 1 — see Hidden in plain sight below for how that compares with the line Corsair sells today.

2.Launch and lineup

SimpleStart here

Corsair introduced Dominator Titanium DDR5 as a premium, scarce launch edition, then expanded it into an ongoing catalogue line sold in Black, White and Grey 12.

Deep diveThe technical detail

The May 2023 release frames Dominator Titanium as a design-forward flagship: “a stylish solid aluminum heatspreader,” Corsair’s DHX cooling technology, swappable “Fin Accessory Kits” that replace the RGB top bars “for an elegant look that harkens back to the original DOMINATOR memory,” and 11 individually addressable RGB LEDs per module 1. No price was named in the release itself; it directs buyers to “refer to the CORSAIR website or contact your local CORSAIR sales or PR representative” for current pricing 1. Corsair’s current store lists the line across three colour options — Titanium RGB White, Gray and Black — with mixed Intel XMP and AMD EXPO certification per SKU, and no source checked for this page describes the numbered First-Edition run as a still-ongoing feature of the current lineup 2.

3.What’s inside the module

SimpleStart here

Dominator Titanium DDR5 uses a solid aluminum heatspreader with Corsair’s DHX cooling technology and a customizable RGB top bar with 11 addressable LEDs, replaceable through swappable accessory kits 1.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Corsair’s launch release names the shell material (“a stylish solid aluminum heatspreader”), the cooling approach (DHX, Corsair’s dual-sided heat-spreader technology), the lighting (11 individually addressable RGB LEDs per module) and a swappable top bar: separately sold “Fin Accessory Kits” that replace the RGB top bars with fin-styled ones “for an elegant look that harkens back to the original DOMINATOR memory” 1. Corsair’s current store page describes the same swap option in different, newer wording — “official CORSAIR accessory kits or 3D print your own,” to “change your memory’s style and cooling capabilities” — adding a 3D-printable option the May 2023 launch release does not mention 2. The specific 64GB/6000MT/s/CL30 SKU checked for this page lists a 57mm module height with the RGB top bar installed and iCUE software compatibility requiring version 4.17 or later for lighting control 3.

4.Full spec table

SimpleStart here

A representative current SKU — 64GB (2x32GB) at 6000MT/s — runs CL30 at 1.40V tested, with a 4800MT/s CL40 JEDEC default before XMP or EXPO is enabled, and both Intel XMP 3.0 and AMD EXPO profiles supported on the same module 3.

5.Where it’s made

SimpleStart here

Corsair assembles and tests Dominator Titanium DDR5 modules; neither the launch release nor the current product or SKU pages name a DRAM die supplier 123.

Deep diveThe technical detail

None of the three Corsair sources checked for this page — the May 2023 launch release, the current Dominator Titanium listing page, or the specific 64GB/6000MT/s SKU page — states which company fabricates the DRAM dies inside Dominator Titanium DDR5 123. Corsair’s public material describes its own role as design, assembly, binning and testing, consistent with what this site has found on every other module-brand source checked so far.

6.What it’s compatible with

SimpleStart here

Corsair’s launch release and the current 64GB/6000MT/s SKU both state dual support for Intel XMP 3.0 and AMD EXPO on the same module 13.

Deep diveThe technical detail

The May 2023 launch release states support “Intel XMP 3.0 when paired with 12th and 13th-Gen Core processors or AMD EXPO for Ryzen 7000 CPUs” — the CPU generations current at launch, not a claim of unlimited forward compatibility 1. Corsair’s current store lists individual colour variants with different certification combinations — for example, the White Titanium RGB kit appears with both Intel and AMD-branded listings, while Gray is EXPO-branded and Black is XMP-branded in the store’s own category listing — so a specific SKU’s exact certification should be checked on that SKU’s own page rather than assumed from the family name alone 2. The 64GB/6000MT/s SKU checked in full for this page states both standards on the one module 3.

7.Official pricing

SimpleStart here

Corsair’s May 2023 launch release named no specific price, directing buyers to “refer to the CORSAIR website or contact your local CORSAIR sales or PR representative” 1. Corsair’s current store lists real prices per SKU — the 64GB/6000MT/s/CL30 kit checked for this page is priced at $1,078.99 3.

Deep diveThe technical detail

This is a genuine change in how the line is sold, not just a gap in Corsair’s messaging. The launch release names no figure and points buyers toward the website or a sales/PR contact instead, which lines up with what it says about availability — “Limited to just 500 kits and individually numbered,” the kind of scarce drop that often launches without a fixed public price 1. The current store listing carries no First-Edition or numbered-unit language for any of its Dominator Titanium SKUs, and the specific 64GB/6000MT/s/CL30 kit checked for this page is priced plainly at $1,078.99 in the page’s own product metadata, marked a “Best Seller” 32. No Corsair source checked for this page states when or how the transition from numbered launch edition to standard catalogue item happened.

8.Rated vs. real-world speed

SimpleStart here

Corsair’s own material states rated speed and latency figures but does not publish independent benchmark results for Dominator Titanium DDR5 13. Corsair’s warranty covers it under the standard limited lifetime memory warranty 4.

Deep diveThe technical detail

This page reports only Corsair’s own published specification claims; it does not independently re-run or cite third-party benchmark-suite results for Dominator Titanium DDR5. Corsair’s warranty policy promises products “free from defects in material and workmanship,” replaced at Corsair’s expense during the covered period, and requires “a photo of your product’s serial number and your purchase receipt” to file a claim 4. For US buyers specifically, the warranty page states that DRAM coverage requires purchase from “Corsair.com or a Corsair-authorized reseller” — buying from an unauthorized retailer voids the warranty 4.

9.Where this family sits in the lineup

SimpleStart here

Sits above: Dominator Platinum RGB, an earlier, still-listed Corsair DDR5 tier one step below Titanium, and Corsair Vengeance DDR5, the brand’s mainstream line 5.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Corsair’s own DDR5 category page filters products by series and lists Dominator Titanium, Dominator Platinum RGB and Vengeance DDR5 (plus Vengeance RGB RS and Vengeance SODIMM) all as currently-sold, separate series 5. That page does not date any of the three lines or state that one replaced another, so this page reports them as three current tiers rather than claiming Titanium replaced Platinum.

10.Hidden in plain sight

SimpleStart here

Dominator Titanium DDR5 launched as an exclusive, numbered 500-unit edition with no fixed public price — it is now a standard, directly priced catalogue line, with no Corsair source stating when that changed 13.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Read side by side, Corsair’s own sources describe two different products in two different sales models without ever announcing the shift between them. The May 2023 launch release is explicit: “Limited to just 500 kits and individually numbered,” available from July 2023, naming no price and pointing buyers to “refer to the CORSAIR website or contact your local CORSAIR sales or PR representative” instead 1. The 64GB/6000MT/s/CL30 SKU checked in full for this page carries none of that language — no numbering, no scarcity claim, no sales-contact requirement — and instead shows a plain retail price of $1,078.99 with a “Best Seller” label in the page’s own metadata 3. The current Dominator Titanium listing page shows three ordinary colour variants for sale with no First-Edition framing anywhere 2. Every source checked for this page is consistent with the numbered edition being real and limited to the original 500 units, and with the line that exists today being a separate, ongoing, regularly priced product under the same name — but no source explains the transition, so this page reports the two states as found rather than asserting a specific date or reason for the change.
