Four cautions before you compare

  1. A maker and a brand are not the same kind of company. Three firms make most of the world’s DRAM. Everything else on the shelf is their silicon on somebody else’s circuit board, and the brand’s real product is sorting and testing.
  2. “No price published” is the normal case, not an omission. Almost every company here publishes none, so this site never guesses one. Where a company does publish a price, it is often on one country’s store only.
  3. Whose chips are inside can change without the part number changing. At least one brand states this outright. A matched kit bought today and a second stick bought next year may not contain the same silicon.
  4. “Lifetime warranty” does not mean the same thing at every brand. At some it is the owner’s lifetime; at one it is the product’s production life, after which you get a substitute. The individual guides quote each company’s own definition.

Two explainers pick up where this table stops. For what the speed on the box actually means, read XMP, EXPO and why your memory runs slower than the box says. For the third column above — whose chips are inside, and whether you can find out — read whose DRAM chips are actually inside your memory stick. And for the last column, where almost every brand offers a lifetime warranty and means something different by it, read what a memory lifetime warranty actually promises.
