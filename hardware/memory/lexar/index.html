Lexar: The Complete Guide

In 2017 Micron shut the Lexar business down and sold the name. What the buyer got was the trademark: no factory, no staff, no equity — and a commitment to a United States security committee that it would not issue firmware or software upgrades for the products those marks had been on. Lexar is also the only brand in this section that names its chip supplier in a certification. Three reading levels, every number sourced.

1.At a glance

SimpleStart here

Lexar is an American memory brand that has been owned by a Chinese company since 2017. It does not make memory chips. It buys them, builds them into modules, cards and drives, and sells them under a name that is thirty years old 1 2.

What it makesMemory cards, USB drives, card readers, portable and internal SSDs — and DDR5 and DDR4 modules 3
How bigLexar-branded sales were RMB 4.741bn in 2025, up 34.53% on the year 4
Whose chipsSK hynix — Lexar names it in a certification and on its ARES RGB 2nd Gen page 1 5
Any HBM?No. Its owner packages and tests flash; neither runs a wafer fab 4
DeeperThe detail

Two things make Lexar different from Kingston and Corsair, the other module brands in this section, and both are about ownership rather than product.

The first is that Lexar is a brand before it is a company. What changed hands in 2017 was the trademark portfolio — not equity, not factories, not staff. The legal opinion filed for its owner's stock-market listing says so in terms, and the key sentence of that document is quoted in the original further down this page 6. Everything Lexar sells today is designed, packaged and shipped by Shenzhen Longsys Electronics, which is listed on the Shenzhen Stock Exchange as 301308 7.

The second is that memory is the small part of Lexar. Its Americas site lists 114 products across seven consumer categories, and only 12 of them are DRAM modules 3 8. Lexar's self-description shifts with context: one press release opens by calling it "a leading global brand of flash memory solutions", while the boilerplate at the foot of that same release drops the word flash — "For more than 25 years, Lexar has been trusted as a leading global brand of memory solutions. Our award-winning lineup includes memory cards, USB Flash Drives, card readers, solid-state drives, and DRAM." 9 DRAM is named, but it is last on the list. Every "world's first" on its own anniversary timeline is a card or a workflow product; not one is a memory module 10.

ExpertFor specialists

The framing that matters most on this page, and the reason it is worth reading past the specifications. When Micron sold Lexar it had already announced it was closing the business: "Micron Technology today announced that it is discontinuing its Lexar® retail removable media storage business", adding in the same post that "Micron is exploring opportunities to sell all or part of the Lexar business" 11. What was eventually sold, three months later, was the name. The buyer's own listing documents describe the transaction as a purchase of "商标资产" — trademark assets — explicitly not equity in Micron or its subsidiaries, and explicitly not the commercial operations 6.

That makes Lexar the second Micron-divested brand documented in this section, alongside Crucial, and the two exits were handled in opposite ways. Micron sold the Lexar name to a competitor and walked away; with Crucial it ended the consumer business and kept the trademark. Reading the two together is the clearest illustration in this codex of what a memory brand actually is: a reputation that can be detached from the factory that earned it.

One consequence of that detachment is documented and genuinely unusual. Because the buyer was Chinese and the seller American, the trademark purchase went through a United States national-security review, and the commitments that came out of it constrain what Lexar's owner may do for the products Micron had already sold. Those commitments are set out in "Hidden in plain sight", below.

DeeperThe detail

The group's two-brand structure is stated plainly in the listing documents: Longsys "拥有行业类存储品牌FORESEE和国际高端消费类存储品牌Lexar（雷克沙）" — it owns the industrial-sector storage brand FORESEE and the international high-end consumer storage brand Lexar 6. FORESEE was created in-house in 2011 15; Lexar was bought in 2017. Industrial customers get the brand Longsys built; consumers get the brand it acquired.

Lexar has its own subsidiary chain inside that group. The definitions section of the 2025 annual report names 深圳雷克沙, 香港雷克沙, 美国雷克沙, 日本雷克沙 and 欧洲雷克沙 — the Shenzhen, Hong Kong, United States, Japan and Europe Lexar entities — alongside Lexar Enterprise, among roughly thirty group companies 4. One of them does corporate work well beyond memory: in 2026 Longsys approved a purchase by Lexar Europe B.V. of the remaining 19% of Zilia Eletrônicos, the group's Brazilian subsidiary, for US$46.08m 16.

A detail worth noticing, because it is the kind of thing a reader checks and then doubts themselves over. Lexar's own United States privacy notice does not name Longsys anywhere. It identifies "Lexar International" at a San Jose address and refers to "all Lexar-affiliated companies" without listing them 13. The ownership is not concealed — Longsys publishes it, and so does Lexar's own timeline 2 1 — but it is not on the document a United States buyer is most likely to open.

ExpertFor specialists

On standards bodies the group and the brand sit in different places, and the distinction is exact rather than pedantic. JEDEC's published member list includes "Shenzhen Longsys Electronics Co. Ltd." and does not include an entry for Lexar 17. The list rendered as a single continuous alphabetical roster, and the entries under L were Lattice Semiconductor, Lenovo, LG Electronics, Lightmatter, Lotes and LX Semicon — so the gap falls where a Lexar entry would sit. The SD Association's member listing names both, as "Lexar International" and "SHENZHEN LONGSYS ELECTRONICS CO., LTD" 18; that listing does not show membership class.

The reading is straightforward and worth stating for readers tracing who sits at which table: the memory-standards seat is held by the owner, under its own name, while the brand holds a seat at the card-standards body where its reputation was built.

A limit of this page rather than a finding. Longsys's annual report is a long Chinese-language PDF and the retrieval of it truncated before the employee-numbers table, the directors-and-officers table and the certified subsidiary schedule 4. So no total group headcount appears on this page, and none should be inferred: what could be read is the research figure, "截至2026年6月30日，公司拥有技术研发人员1294人" — 1,294 technical research and development staff at 30 June 2026, against 1,240 at the end of 2025 15 4. Lexar's own About page gives no headcount at all 1.

3.The story

SimpleStart here

Lexar has been sold twice, and the second sale is the strange one.

  • 1996 "Founded in 1996, Lexar was born out of innovation to create industry-leading data storage and memory solutions worldwide" 1. Its own timeline adds: "Lexar was founded in California, USA, and set the standard for CF Card reading speed" 1.
  • 2006 Micron agrees to buy Lexar Media, Inc., then a public company trading on NASDAQ as LEXR 19. The price is raised before the deal closes 20, and the merger completes in June 2006 21.
  • 2012–2014 A run of card records: "Introduced the world's first 256GB CompactFlash Card & SDXC UHS-I Card", "Introduced the world's first Professional Workflow solution", "Introduced the world's fastest SD UHS-II Card" 10.
  • June 2017 Micron announces it "is discontinuing its Lexar® retail removable media storage business", while "exploring opportunities to sell all or part of the Lexar business" 11.
  • August 2017 "In August 2017, Lexar was acquired by Longsys, a company specializing in storage products and market service R&D" 2. Micron signs the trademark assignment on 28 August 2017 6.
  • Undated Longsys and Micron file a joint voluntary notice with the United States security committee CFIUS, which reviews the purchase; Longsys gives a written Letter of Assurance. The legal opinion gives no date for the review concluding 6.
  • 2023 "Acquired the exclusive 'Lexar Empowered By SK hynix' Gold Label certification" 1.
  • 2026 Thirtieth anniversary, an "AI storage vision", and a global partnership with the Argentina national football team 22.
DeeperThe detail

The Micron acquisition is documented to an unusual level of detail because both companies were listed. The March 2006 announcement set an exchange ratio of 0.5625 Micron shares per Lexar share against roughly 81.6 million Lexar shares, and named the principals: Micron's chairman, chief executive and president Steve Appleton, and Lexar Media's chairman, chief executive and president Eric Stang 19. In June the terms improved — "MICRON TECHNOLOGY, INC., ANNOUNCES WILLINGNESS TO INCREASE EXCHANGE RATIO TO 0.5925 IN ACQUISITION OF LEXAR MEDIA, INC." — with the shareholder meeting adjourned to 16 June 2006 20. Micron's annual report records the completion: "in June 2006, the Company acquired Lexar Media, Inc. ('Lexar'), a designer, developer, manufacturer and marketer of Flash memory products, in a stock-for-stock merger" 21.

What Micron then did with it is also on the record: "As a result of its acquisition of Lexar, the Company began selling in the fourth quarter of 2006 high-performance digital media products and other flash-based storage products through retail and original equipment manufacturing (OEM) channels" 21. Eleven years later it decided those channels were not where it wanted to be, citing "the company's ongoing efforts to focus on its increasing opportunities in higher value markets and channels" 11.

The sequence is the thing to get right, because the two events are usually collapsed into one. Micron did not sell a going concern. On 1 June 2017 it announced it was closing the Lexar retail business, and said in the same announcement that it was looking for a buyer 11. The sale of the marks followed in August 2 6. A reader who remembers "Micron sold Lexar" has the second half without the first.

ExpertFor specialists

The precise description of the transaction comes from the legal opinion filed for Longsys's listing, and it is worth reading in the original because the marketing language is looser. The opinion states: "由于本次交易系购买美光科技的商标资产，不涉及美光科技或其子公司的股权或有关实体在全球其他地区的商业运营，因此不涉及其他国家或地区主管部门的批准，仅需在标的商标注册地办理有关知识产权登记变更手续" — because the transaction was a purchase of Micron's trademark assets, and involved neither equity in Micron or its subsidiaries nor those entities' commercial operations elsewhere in the world, no approval was needed from authorities in any other country, and only intellectual-property registration changes were required in the jurisdictions where the marks were registered 6.

The same document records the housekeeping that followed, which is itself evidence of how thin the transfer was: Micron issued the trademark assignment on 28 August 2017 confirming it had received the consideration; the United States trademark agent Fleckman & McGlynn served transfer papers on local agents in Brazil and South Africa in June and July 2018; and as late as the 2021 filing eight marks had still not completed re-registration in those two countries, with the opinion noting Micron had raised no objection to Longsys's use of the name in the interim 6. Nothing was being moved except paper, and the paper took years.

One figure this page does not give: what Longsys paid. The legal opinion confirms only that consideration was received, without stating an amount, and the retrieval of that PDF truncated 6. Micron's June 2017 announcement carries no price 11, nor does Longsys's own brand page 2. Nor could the 2006 Micron–Lexar total consideration be established: the fiscal 2006 annual report describes the merger but the retrieval returned no aggregate figure and no exchange ratio from it 21. Both prices are unretrieved in this check, not undisclosed — each sits in a long document that could not be read to its end.

Lexar's memory range is small and clearly tiered. Its Americas memory page lists twelve products 8.

DeeperThe detail

The fastest kit Lexar lists is an ARES RGB 2nd Gen part: 48GB (2×24GB) at 8000 MT/s, CL40-48-48-128, 1.35V, part number LD5U24G80C40BR-RGD 5. Below it the same family offers 6400 MT/s CL30-38-38-76 and 6000 MT/s, both at 1.4V 5. The memory page's own speed filter tops out at DDR5-8000, which corroborates that ceiling 8. The first-generation ARES RGB reached 7200 MT/s 27.

Two things are worth knowing before treating that list as complete. First, a thirteenth DRAM product page is live but missing from the category listing — HADES RGB DDR4, which appears in Lexar's product sitemap and has a working page but does not show among the twelve 26 8. Second, Lexar's own warranty table names DDR4 sub-families the Americas catalogue does not show: "ARES OC Series", "HADES OC Series" and "THOR OC Series", all DDR4 — the only OC part in the catalogue is a THOR OC DDR5 28 29. The catalogue and the warranty schedule do not describe the same range.

A CUDIMM — a module with its own clock driver — was announced but is not listed. Lexar's CES 2025 release said: "Lexar will also show ARES DDR5 CUDIMM memory which represents the next generation of desktop memory. It boasts speeds up to 9600MT/s and features a Clock Driver IC (CKD) to avoid electronic noise and jitter that can result in errors, lost data, or system freezes." 30 No capacity, latency or part number was given, and no CUDIMM product page appears in the memory listing or the product sitemap as of this check 8. Announced is not shipped, and this page does not treat it as shipped.

ExpertFor specialists

Two inconsistencies on Lexar's own pages, recorded because a careful reader will hit them and wonder which number to trust. The DDR5 SODIMM page's headline and its specification table disagree: the tagline reads "Power up your laptop with next-gen DDR5 performance at up to 4800 MT/s" while the table on the same page states 5600MT/s, CL46-45-45-90, 1.1V, 262-pin, to JESD79-5A 25. The SK hynix certification wording, by contrast, is consistent: the About page and the 30th-anniversary timeline carry the identical 2023 entry, "Acquired the exclusive 'Lexar Empowered By SK hynix' Gold Label certification" 1 10.

A further caution on the "fastest kit" claim above. The specification of a 6000 MT/s ARES part was reported inconsistently across two retrievals of the same page — once as 32GB (2×16GB) and once as 16GB — and a low-latency DDR5-6000 CL26 variant is sold at retail but did not appear in the specification table of the Americas page in either retrieval 5. The page's global-locale equivalent could not be loaded at all, returning a redirect loop. So the range as described here is the Americas catalogue as it rendered, not a certified list of every part Lexar sells.

On generations, the split is clean and worth stating because it affects what a buyer can switch on: the DDR5 gaming lines claim both Intel XMP 3.0 and AMD EXPO, with the ARES specification table reading "XMP 3.0 / AMD EXPO" 5 and THOR stating "Supports Intel XMP 3.0 and AMD EXPO overclocking" 23; the DDR4 line claims XMP 2.0 only, with HADES RGB reading "The Lexar Hades RGB DDR4 is compatible with latest INTEL XMP 2.0 and AMD Ryzen to optimize and enhance your gaming experience" 26 — note that the second half of that sentence claims compatibility with AMD processors, not with EXPO. The plain DDR5 UDIMM and SODIMM pages claim neither; both give JESD79-5A as their standard and nothing else 24 25.

5.How a module is built

SimpleStart here

Lexar describes three steps, and its own words are more specific on the top tier than on the rest.

  1. Pick the chips. "Lexar® ARES RGB DDR5 Desktop Memory modules use carefully binned memory chips — the highest-performing examples from each production batch." 31 The product page puts it as "ARES RGB 2nd Gen DDR5 uses hand-picked chips, carefully screened for maximum reliability and performance" 5.
  2. Build them onto a Lexar board. Its launch release lists a "customized PCB" among the features, 32, and the product page adds "A 1.88mm-thick heatsink and an exclusive PMIC thermal conductive pad provide superior thermal conductivity" 5.
  3. Test the profiles. "Every Lexar® ARES RGB DDR5 Desktop Memory kit includes multiple XMP profiles that have undergone extensive stability testing." 31
DeeperThe detail

The thermal and board claims differ by tier, and Lexar publishes the numbers: a 1.88mm heatsink on ARES RGB 2nd Gen 5, 1.6mm on THOR OC — "1.6mm thick durable heatsink in solid aluminum for superior heat dissipation" 29 — and on THOR RGB 2nd Gen a "Premium aluminum heatsink with a sandblasted finish fuses razor-sharp aesthetics with uncompromising thermal control, delivering durability and cooling" 23. The DDR4 HADES line gets "a sleek aluminum heat spreader to keep your system cool" 26.

What Lexar does not claim anywhere read for this check is that it owns memory-module test equipment or does its own module assembly. The binning language is about chip selection; the manufacturing capability belongs to the owner, and is described under Longsys's name rather than Lexar's — see the next section.

The testing claim attached to Lexar's memory modules repays a close read, because it is not about memory. The sentence on the DDR5 SODIMM page is: "All Lexar products undergo extensive testing in the Lexar Quality Labs, facilities with thousands of different cameras and digital devices to ensure performance, quality, compatibility, and reliability" 25. The dedicated page for those labs describes the same thing: technicians "test products for compatibility as well as durability by putting them through various vibration, temperature, electric shock, impact, and strength tests", and, "Recognizing the needs of photographers and videographers (be they professional or hobbyist), the labs test on cameras and other devices that date back to the mid-1990s" — with "thousands of devices" making the facility "a 'mini-tech museum'" 33. That is a camera-compatibility laboratory, attached as boilerplate to a DIMM. It is a real facility doing real work; it is simply not evidence about DRAM module validation.

ExpertFor specialists

The genuine manufacturing capability in this group is documented, and it sits under the owner's name and in flash rather than DRAM. The 2025 annual report names a specific site: "超薄 ePOP5x、超薄 ePOP4x 及超小尺寸 eMMC，由元成苏州封测制造基地封装测试，采用创新的研磨切割工艺，实现了产品尺寸上明显小于市场主流产品。" — the ultra-thin ePOP5x and ePOP4x and ultra-small eMMC are packaged and tested at the Yuancheng Suzhou packaging-and-test manufacturing base, a group entity, using a grinding-and-dicing process that makes them markedly smaller than mainstream parts 4. The half-year report adds a system-in-package claim for mSSD products, described as "自主" — in-house 15.

On wafers, Longsys states a supply relationship rather than a supplier: "公司已与上游主要存储晶圆原厂建立了长期、稳固且深度的战略合作，是全球少数与多家晶圆原厂直接签署供应协议的存储器企业之一。" — the company has established long-term, solid and deep strategic cooperation with the major upstream memory wafer fabs, and is one of the few memory companies worldwide to have signed supply agreements directly with multiple wafer fabs 15. Even that stronger claim names a class of counterparty, not a company. The one named chip supplier anywhere in this group's public material is on the Lexar side, and it is dealt with in "Whose chips are inside", below.

A scoping limit on certifications, stated so it is not mistaken for a finding. Longsys's Honors page lists no ISO or IATF certification — what it lists are awards and partner recognitions, including "Red Dot Award: Product Design", "Chinese Patent Excellence Award", and "Best Partner" recognition from Samsung, SanDisk, Silicon Motion and Sony, most of them undated on that page, though a few carry a year — "Committee of IT’s Impact on China - Editors’ Choice Award, 2022" is one 34. Its About page does record an "ISO9001:2000 quality system certification" in 2007 and an "IPR management system certification" in 2017 12 — so the group holds quality certifications; they are simply not on the Honors page. The Zhongshan Storage Industrial Park page gives no certification, no investment figure, no capacity and no headcount — what it does give is "Efficient and low-cost automated testing system developed by Longsys" and "over 1000 devices" 35. None of this is a claim that the group lacks certifications; it is a record of what these pages say.

7.How the memory works

SimpleStart here

Two things about a Lexar DDR5 module are worth understanding, and Lexar explains one of them well.

The chip corrects some of its own errors. Lexar's plain DDR5 page carries the heading "On-die ECC offers improved stability" and explains: "Your system will effortlessly navigate overclocking speeds with on-die error correction code" 24. On-die ECC is part of the DDR5 standard, not a Lexar feature — every DDR5 chip has it — and it protects the chip's internal array, not the data travelling to the processor.

The rated speed is not the starting speed. A DDR5 module boots at a conservative default and reaches its advertised figure only when a profile is switched on in the BIOS. That is what Intel XMP and AMD EXPO are: stored profiles the machine can adopt. Lexar's gaming lines carry both 5 23; its plain and laptop lines carry neither, both listing only the JEDEC standard JESD79-5A 24 25.

DeeperThe detail

The gap between default and rated is visible on Lexar's own specification tables. The plain DDR5 UDIMM runs 5600MT/s at CL46-45-45-90 and 1.1V — that is roughly what JEDEC specifies and what the module does unaided 24. The ARES RGB 2nd Gen flagship runs 8000 MT/s at CL40-48-48-128 and 1.35V 5. The higher number needs more voltage and a profile; it is not what the stick does out of the box.

Lexar's own overclocking article is unusually honest about where the performance comes from, and it is the clearest short statement of what a module brand sells: the ARES modules "use carefully binned memory chips — the highest-performing examples from each production batch", and every kit "includes multiple XMP profiles that have undergone extensive stability testing" 31. Sorting and profile validation — that is the product. The silicon is somebody else's.

The announced-but-unlisted CUDIMM is the next step in the same direction, and Lexar's description of why it exists is accurate: a clock driver on the module "to avoid electronic noise and jitter that can result in errors, lost data, or system freezes", enabling "speeds up to 9600MT/s" 30. At those frequencies the clock signal reaching each chip is the limiting factor, so the module regenerates it locally.

ExpertFor specialists

One place where Lexar's own material invites a wrong inference, worth separating carefully. Its RGB software, Lexar RGB Sync, is described as "a powerful RGB lighting control software" and is stated to be compatible with Asus Aura Sync, Gigabyte RGB Fusion, MSI Mystic Light Sync and Asrock Polychrome RGB 26. That is lighting interoperability. Nothing read for this check indicates that Lexar's software reads module temperature or writes overclocking profiles the way some competitors' does, and this page does not claim it does — nor does it claim Lexar lacks such a tool, which would be an absence argued from silence.

On AMD EXPO there is a distinction between claiming support and appearing on a vendor list, and only the first is established here. Lexar's own DDR5 gaming pages state EXPO support plainly 5 23. AMD's own EXPO page carries a partner carousel naming Kingston, ADATA, Corsair, G.SKILL, Geil and V-Color, and Lexar was not among the names returned 36. Treat that as a single observation of a carousel — exactly the kind of element that renders incompletely — and not as evidence that Lexar is absent from AMD's programme. AMD's separate tested-kit compatibility list could not be read at all in this check, so whether Lexar kits appear on it is simply unresolved.

SimpleStart here

This decode is inference from Lexar's own part-number table, not a published key. No Lexar page read for this check explains its part-number scheme, and none should be assumed to exist or not to exist on that basis. Compare the sibling parts and the pattern holds on two of three: LD5U16G64C30BR-RGD is 16GB modules at 6400 CL30. The third, LD5U16G60C30BR-RGD, is listed as a 16GB (2×8GB) kit, where "16G" must be the kit rather than the module — so even within one table the field is not read the same way twice 5. Treat it as a reading aid, not as Lexar's documentation.

One field Lexar does not publish anywhere read for this check is the one this codex cares about most: the chip maker per part. Corsair publishes a version code on the module sticker that maps to Micron, Samsung or Hynix; Lexar names a supplier at brand level but attaches no equivalent code to a part number. The full treatment of that is in the next section but one.

9.Official pricing

SimpleStart here

Lexar publishes no price on eleven of the twelve memory product pages read for this check, and its product pages send buyers to other shops instead. The one exception is the THOR RGB 2nd Gen page, whose part-number table carries an MSRP column — with a figure implausible enough that this page treats it as an artefact rather than a price, and does not print it. See "Deeper", below.

The ARES RGB 2nd Gen page carries a "Where to buy" section naming Amazon and Micro Center; the THOR RGB 2nd Gen page links to Best Buy 5 23. Neither has a price or an add-to-cart button of its own. The memory listing page shows twelve products with no prices, offering "Read more" and "Compare" instead 8.

That puts Lexar with Kingston rather than with Corsair, which does publish prices with a working cart on its United States store.

DeeperThe detail

One structural detail is worth recording without over-reading it. Lexar's Americas site exposes /my-account/, /cart/ and /checkout/ in its own page sitemap — the standard set for a shop platform. Those three pages were not opened in this check, so the accurate statement is that the sitemap exposes commerce pages, not that Lexar runs a working storefront 37. Alongside that, several product pages carry a price of $0.00 in their page metadata while showing no price in the body 27 26 25 — consistent with a catalogue built on shop software whose price fields were never filled in.

A figure deliberately not printed on this page. One retrieval of the THOR RGB 2nd Gen page reported a part-number table containing an MSRP column with a figure for a 32GB DDR5-6000 CL38 kit that was roughly five times that kit's street price, and no other Lexar page showed an MSRP column at all 23. A second, independent retrieval returned the same MSRP column on the same page, so the column is real; the figure in it remains implausible enough — roughly five times that kit’s street price — that it is far more likely a data-entry or extraction artefact than a recommended price. The number is withheld rather than published and later corrected.

The honest summary of the rendering question, which matters for any pricing claim: raw page source could not be inspected for any Lexar page in this check, so "no price appeared" cannot be fully separated from "the price did not render". The claim is strongest on the ARES RGB 2nd Gen page, where two differently-worded retrievals both returned a complete-looking page and both reported the part-number table as having capacity and part-number columns and no price column 5.

ExpertFor specialists

What Lexar publishes instead of a price is a warranty, and its definition of "lifetime" is written out rather than assumed: "'Limited lifetime warranty' means that Lexar will provide repair, replacement, or refund services free of charge if quality problems arise during the use of the product (shipping costs not included)" 28. There is a regional carve-out: "For products purchased in Germany or regions where the limited lifetime warranty is not applicable, the duration of the limited lifetime warranty shall be 10 years" 28, and the ARES product page states the same in its own words 5.

Coverage is tied to the channel, which is the practical point for anyone buying from a marketplace: the warranty covers product "purchased by the original end-user purchaser from Lexar or a Lexar authorized seller", the customer must obtain "a Return Materials Authorization, or RMA, number within the applicable warranty period" and "have proof of purchase", and the exclusions run to "malfunctions or failures resulting from misuse, abuse, neglect, alteration, problems with electrical power, usage not in accordance with Product instructions, acts of nature or improper installation or repairs made by anyone other than Lexar or a Lexar authorized service provider" 28. One further clause matters more for a storage brand than a memory one: "Lexar does not warrant against loss of any data or images (including data stored on Product returned to Lexar for service)" 28.

An apparent gap, reported as unresolved rather than as a finding. A targeted retrieval of the warranty page reported that DDR5 does not appear in its product tables at all — the memory section lists DDR4 under UDIMM, SODIMM, HADES RGB, HADES OC, ARES RGB, ARES OC and THOR OC series, each at "Lifetime limited warranty" 28 — while every DDR5 product page read states a lifetime limited warranty 5 23 24 25. That is a single retrieval of one page against six others, with no raw source available to arbitrate. The reasonable conclusion is that the warranty schedule has not been updated for DDR5, not that DDR5 is uncovered — but this page states the discrepancy rather than resolving it.

10.Whose chips are inside

SimpleStart here

SK hynix. Lexar is the only brand in this section so far to name its chip supplier inside a certification rather than a blog post or a filing. Its own corporate timeline, for 2023, reads:

"Acquired the exclusive 'Lexar Empowered By SK hynix' Gold Label certification." 1

And its flagship product page says it directly: the ARES RGB 2nd Gen is "Equipped with premium SK hynix DRAM chips to enable higher performance and better stability." 5. Lexar's own launch release for that family lists "Premium SK hynix DRAM chips for higher performance and better stability" among the features 32.

No HBM anywhere: neither Lexar nor its owner runs a wafer fab, and neither sells high-bandwidth memory 4.

DeeperThe detail

Now the qualification, which matters as much as the claim. In this check the SK hynix attribution appeared only on ARES RGB 2nd Gen material — its product page and its launch release 5 32. It was not found in the retrieved content of the first-generation ARES page, the two THOR pages read (THOR RGB 2nd Gen and THOR OC — three further THOR products were not opened), the plain DDR5 UDIMM page, the DDR5 SODIMM page or the HADES DDR4 page. Each of those was read once, so this is a not-found rather than a proven absence: the honest statement is that Lexar names SK hynix on its top tier, and this check found no such naming on the rest.

The certification itself is also narrower than it first reads. "Lexar Empowered By SK hynix Gold Label" asserts a labelled relationship with a named chip maker, with a date 1. It does not say what proportion of Lexar's memory uses SK hynix dies, says nothing about wafers, and names no other supplier. A reader should take it as evidence that the relationship is real and publicised — not as a guarantee about any particular stick.

Set against the other brands in this section, the ranking is clear. Kingston names three suppliers on its own blog. Corsair names three in its annual report and publishes a sticker code that identifies which one is inside a module you already own. Lexar names one, in a certification, on its top tier — a stronger marketing signal than either, and a weaker practical guarantee than Corsair's.

ExpertFor specialists

The question this codex now asks of every module brand is not "does it name the chip maker" but "can a buyer choose". For Lexar the answer is: no method of identifying the ICs in a module was found in this check, and the search was not exhaustive enough to call it an absence. What was checked: the ARES RGB 2nd Gen and first-generation ARES product pages, where part-number tables exist but nothing indicates that a digit encodes an IC vendor, and the overclocking article, which describes binning without giving any buyer-facing verification method 5 27 31. Lexar's support FAQ area was surfaced but not opened, so this item is explicitly not checked to completion. Corsair's equivalent — a sticker version code mapping to Micron, Samsung and Hynix — has no counterpart that this check could find.

On the owner's side, the wafer question is answered only at the level of a class of counterparty. Longsys states that it "已与上游主要存储晶圆原厂建立了长期、稳固且深度的战略合作" — has established long-term, solid and deep strategic cooperation with the major upstream memory wafer fabs 15. That is a real disclosure about supply security and tells a reader nothing about whose silicon is in a given Lexar DIMM. No Longsys statement naming a wafer supplier was located in this check — its own About Longsys page describes research, design and sales of flash and DRAM without naming a single upstream fab 12.

The HBM heading a chip maker would see here has no content for Lexar, and the reason is worth a sentence rather than a shrug. Longsys is a packaging and test company that buys wafers and sells finished storage; its named in-house capability is system-in-package work at the Suzhou base, in flash 4 15. High-bandwidth memory is a stacked-DRAM product made by the three firms that own DRAM fabs. A packager with no fab has no route into it, and Lexar's exposure to the AI memory boom is entirely as a buyer of chips whose price it does not set.

11.Who buys it and why

SimpleStart here

Lexar sells mainly to photographers, videographers and gamers — in that order, judged by its own catalogue.

Lexar's site carries five solution pages by audience — professional, content creator, personal storage, gamer and system builders 37.

DeeperThe detail

The money says the same thing the catalogue does. Lexar-branded sales were RMB 4.741 billion in 2025, up 34.53% year on year 4, and reached RMB 3.966 billion in the first half of 2026 alone, up 84.90% 15. Those are brand-level figures across all of Lexar's categories, not DRAM figures — no separate memory-module revenue line was found in this check.

Set against the group, Lexar is a meaningful but minority part of it. Longsys's total revenue was RMB 22.766 billion in 2025, with net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 1.423 billion 7; in the first half of 2026 revenue reached RMB 24.088 billion — more in six months than in the whole of the previous year — with net profit of RMB 10.577 billion and earnings per share of RMB 25.20 16 15. The storage-products segment ran a 59.08% gross margin in that half 15.

A conversion trap worth naming, because it is easy to get wrong by a factor of ten and the error is not obvious. Chinese filings state the brand-level figures in 亿元 — hundreds of millions of yuan — while the group totals are given in plain yuan to the cent. Lexar's 2025 figure is printed as "47.41亿元", which is RMB 4.741 billion, not 47.41 billion 4. Read carelessly it would exceed the whole group's revenue.

ExpertFor specialists

Those numbers are the memory price surge seen from the buying side, and Longsys says so in its own words: "存储价格进入明显上行通道" — memory prices have entered a pronounced upward channel 15. A packager that buys wafers and sells finished product at retail benefits when prices rise faster than its inventory cost, and a 59.08% segment gross margin, with half-year profit of RMB 10.577bn against RMB 14.77m a year earlier — the filing states the increase as 71,528.66% — are what that looks like on a profit-and-loss account 16 15 7.

It is the same mechanism documented on the Corsair page in this section, running through a different kind of company: Corsair reported memory revenue up and its other component lines down by a third, on the argument that expensive memory stops people building whole computers — see that page for the figures. Lexar's owner does not sell cases and power supplies, so it shows the upside without the offsetting drag. Both are cyclical in the same direction, and neither controls the input price.

One structural note for readers tracing where Lexar sits in the market. Its 2026 anniversary release claims the brand "now operates more than 100,000 sales channels across six continents, serving over 100 million users in more than 70 countries" 22 — the grammatical subject there is Lexar, not Longsys. Against that reach, the retailers Lexar itself names are concentrated: eighteen United States retailers and five Canadian ones on its Where to buy page, with a region selector offering the United States, France, Germany, Spain, Italy, China, Japan and a global option 38.

12.Hidden in plain sight

SimpleStart here

The most surprising fact about Lexar is not in any product page. It is in a Chinese legal filing, and it concerns what its owner promised the United States government.

Because a Chinese company was buying an American brand, the purchase went to CFIUS — the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States. The commitments Longsys gave on 27 July 2018 included that it would not handle any after-sales service for the subject products — the filing specifies this covers service arising both before and after the transaction — that it would not provide firmware or other software upgrades for them, and that it would issue annual audit reports 6.

Read that again with a storage product in mind. The new owner of the name undertook not to issue firmware for the products those marks had been on. The filing summarises the list rather than reproducing it, gives no end date, and does not define which products count as "the subject products".

DeeperThe detail

The filing sets out the whole arrangement. It records that "发行人及美光科技就本次交易向美国CFIUS提交该交易相关的联合自愿申报，CFIUS依据上述法律对本次交易进行了国家安全审查" — the issuer and Micron made a joint voluntary filing to CFIUS, which conducted a national-security review under Section 721 of the Defense Production Act of 1950 6.

It then names the auditors. Longsys "聘请经CFIUS认可的第三方审计机构Schellman & Company,LLC进行年度合规履约审计，聘请经CFIUS认可的第三方审计机构Coalfire Systems, Inc.进行产品合规检查" — it engaged Schellman & Company, LLC for annual compliance audits and Coalfire Systems, Inc. for product compliance inspections, both CFIUS-approved, and "上述机构均已出具相应的审计报告和检查报告，CFIUS未对公司合规情况提出异议" — both issued reports and CFIUS raised no objection 6.

So the thin transfer documented earlier had a thick consequence. Longsys bought marks and no operations 6, and then spent years being audited by two American firms to prove it was keeping away from the products those marks used to be on.

ExpertFor specialists

Three smaller things sit in plain sight and are easy to read past.

One: the standards seat belongs to the owner, not the brand. JEDEC's member list carries "Shenzhen Longsys Electronics Co. Ltd." and no Lexar entry 17; the SD Association's listing carries both 18. For a brand whose reputation was built on cards, that is the right way round — but a reader who assumes the famous name holds the memory-standards seat has it backwards.

Two: the memory testing claim describes a camera lab. The sentence attached to Lexar's DIMMs is about "facilities with thousands of different cameras and digital devices", and the labs page confirms the emphasis, noting the labs "test on cameras and other devices that date back to the mid-1990s" 25 33. It is a genuine facility; it is simply not a claim about module validation, and it is presented as though it were.

Three: Lexar runs a scratch-off anti-counterfeit scheme, and it points at China. On its United Kingdom locale, the verification page tells buyers to "find the anti-counterfeiting label on the package. After scratching off the coating, you can scan the QR code through WeChat or enter the anti-counterfeiting code to query", and, for product arriving without a label, to "consult the dealer where you purchased it or the Lexar after-sales service center (Tel: 4001689872)" 39 — a mainland-China service number, given on a British page. Lexar's separate buyer guidance on counterfeits is real and practical, covering packaging print quality and authorised retailers 40, but it is written about memory cards; no counterfeit guidance specific to DRAM modules was found in this check.

13.Weak spots

SimpleStart here

Lexar's weak spots follow from what it is: a famous name attached to a company that did not build it.

  • The brand was bought, the business was not. The 2017 transaction covered trademark assets, not equity and not operations 6. Continuity of name is not continuity of engineering.
  • Memory is a tenth of the catalogue. 12 of 114 products, and no DRAM entry on the anniversary timeline of firsts 8 3 10.
  • The chip supplier is named on the top tier only. SK hynix appears on ARES RGB 2nd Gen material; this check found no such naming on the other lines 5 32.
  • No way to check what is inside. No buyer-facing IC identification method was found 5 31.
  • No price, and no confirmed shop. Product pages route to Amazon, Micro Center and Best Buy 5 23.
  • Counterfeits are a live issue. Lexar runs a scratch-off verification scheme and publishes a guide to spotting fakes 39 40.
DeeperThe detail

The documentation is inconsistent in ways that a careful buyer will notice: a SODIMM page whose headline and specification table give different speeds 25; a warranty schedule that lists DDR4 families and, on the retrieval made here, no DDR5 at all while every DDR5 page promises lifetime cover 28; a product page and a part number that disagree on latency 27; and a live product page for a family missing from the category listing 26 8. None of these is serious on its own. Together they suggest a catalogue maintained less carefully than the products it describes.

The deeper structural point is about what the brand can promise. The CFIUS commitments are written against "the subject products", a term the readable portion of the filing does not define, and the after-sales clause expressly covers service arising both before and after the transaction 6. This page cannot say from that document whether they still bind anything Lexar ships today. What they do illustrate precisely is the general condition: this is a name whose obligations, capabilities and supply all sit with a different company in a different country from the one on the box. That is not a criticism of the products; it is the fact a reader most needs in order to judge them.

ExpertFor specialists

Three limits of this page, stated so they are not mistaken for findings. First, no Lexar page's raw source could be inspected in this check, so every "no price appeared" and "no vendor named" statement describes what rendered, not what was served. Second, Longsys's annual report truncated before its employee, officer and subsidiary tables, so this page carries no group headcount and no certified subsidiary list, and no inference should be drawn from their absence here 4. Third, the Lexar support FAQ area was surfaced but never opened, which is exactly where an IC-identification article would live if one exists — so the "no way to check what is inside" finding above is scoped to the product and overclocking pages that were read, and nothing more.

One further gap worth naming precisely because it would change the page if filled: no litigation or recall search was run for either Lexar or Longsys in this check. That topic is uncovered rather than clear, and this page makes no claim in either direction.

15.What’s next

SimpleStart here

Three things to watch.

A clocked module, announced and still unlisted. ARES DDR5 CUDIMM at "up to 9600MT/s" was shown at CES in January 2025 30 and has not appeared in the memory catalogue in this check 8. Whether it ships is the clearest near-term test of how seriously Lexar takes the enthusiast tier.

A repositioning around AI. The thirtieth-anniversary release pairs the milestone with a "new AI storage vision" and a global partnership with the Argentina national football team 22 — a brand buying attention at the moment its owner's profits are at a record.

Memory prices. Longsys says plainly that "存储价格进入明显上行通道" — prices have entered a pronounced upward channel 15. That is currently working in its favour.

DeeperThe detail

The financial trajectory is the thing to watch hardest, because it is unusually steep and unusually recent. Group revenue of RMB 22.766bn in 2025 became RMB 24.088bn in the first half of 2026 alone; net profit of RMB 1.423bn became RMB 10.577bn over the same comparison 7 16. A storage-products gross margin of 59.08% in that half 15 is not a normal packaging margin, and it is not one a packager can defend: it exists because chips bought earlier are being sold into a shortage.

The same arithmetic runs backwards when prices fall. A company that buys wafers and sells finished modules gains on the way up and loses on the way down, and the gain is larger the more inventory it was holding. Nothing in the readable portion of these filings suggests Longsys expects the current level to persist, and nothing suggests it does not; the figures simply are what a peak looks like from inside.

On the corporate side, the group has been buying rather than selling: full ownership of its Brazilian subsidiary taken in 2026 through Lexar Europe B.V. for US$46.08m 16. A company that started by buying a name is now using entities carrying that name to consolidate operations elsewhere.

ExpertFor specialists

The question worth holding over Lexar for the next few years is whether a purchased brand can accumulate anything of its own. The evidence so far points both ways, and it is worth being precise about which is which.

Against: every "world's first" on Lexar's anniversary timeline predates or coincides with the Micron era or concerns cards 10; the memory range is twelve products with no record, no first and no DRAM entry on that timeline 8; and its most distinctive memory claim is a certification issued by somebody else 1.

For: the brand has shipped at least one documented world first under the new owner — "Lexar ships the world's first 1TB microSD Express card", announced on 2 April 2025, with a named Lexar executive speaking for it 9 — and brand-level sales grew 34.53% in 2025 and 84.90% in the first half of 2026 4 15. Those are not the numbers of a name being harvested.

The narrower test, for readers of this section specifically, is the one raised under "Whose chips are inside": whether the SK hynix naming spreads from the ARES tier to the rest of the range, and whether any way of identifying the ICs in a purchased module ever appears. A brand that names a supplier on its flagship and nowhere else has made a marketing decision. A brand that lets buyers check has made a commitment.
