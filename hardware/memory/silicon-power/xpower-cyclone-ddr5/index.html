Silicon Power XPOWER Cyclone DDR5: The Complete Guide

1.At a glance

SimpleStart here

XPOWER Cyclone DDR5 is Silicon Power’s first memory line to use a CUDIMM — a module with its own built-in clock driver chip — launched 16 May 2025 alongside a plain-UDIMM sibling that carries the same Cyclone name 1. The launch release headlines the CUDIMM’s speed by name, under its own subheading, at “8200MT/s to 9200MT/s,” but never states any speed figure for the UDIMM anywhere on the page — a reader only learns the UDIMM’s actual range, 6000 to 8000MT/s, lower across the board, from the UDIMM’s own product page 12.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Silicon Power’s launch release announces “two new high-performance DDR5 memory modules under its acclaimed XPOWER gaming series: the XPOWER Cyclone DDR5 RGB CUDIMM and XPOWER Cyclone DDR5 RGB UDIMM” in its opening sentence, then gives the CUDIMM its own named subsection, “CUDIMM: Power Meets Precision,” stating there that “with extreme speeds ranging from 8200MT/s to 9200MT/s, the CUDIMM is purpose-built for gamers and overclockers who demand peak performance” 1. The CUDIMM product page confirms that range exactly: 8200, 8400, 8800 and 9200MT/s 3. The UDIMM gets no equivalent subsection and no speed figure anywhere in the release — its actual range, 6000, 7200 and 8000MT/s, comes only from its own current product page 2. The release’s CKD explanation (below) is the likely reason the two diverge: a CUDIMM’s onboard clock driver is what lets it clear the higher range, and the UDIMM sibling does not have one 132.

2.Launch and lineup

SimpleStart here

Silicon Power launched XPOWER Cyclone DDR5 on 16 May 2025, its first product under the CUDIMM standard, paired at announcement with a slower plain-UDIMM sibling of the same name 1.

Deep diveThe technical detail

The release opens by framing the launch as a double announcement rather than a single product: “Silicon Power (SP) is excited to announce the launch of two new high-performance DDR5 memory modules under its acclaimed XPOWER gaming series: the XPOWER Cyclone DDR5 RGB CUDIMM and XPOWER Cyclone DDR5 RGB UDIMM” 1. It closes by positioning the CUDIMM as the headline: “With the launch of its first-ever CUDIMM and the continued evolution of the XPOWER line, Silicon Power reaffirms its commitment to delivering cutting-edge memory solutions that empower gamers to break new records” 1. The release does not explain why a plain-UDIMM sibling was launched in the same announcement rather than left under the existing XPOWER Zenith DDR5 line, which this site has already built and which also ships as a plain UDIMM at a lower top speed of 6800MT/s on its RGB variant 4.

3.What’s inside the module

SimpleStart here

Both SKUs share the same “sharp lines, symmetrical layers, and a futuristic triangular motif” heat spreader design, a 2mm-thick aluminum build the release says cuts temperatures “by more than 10°C/50°F,” a customizable RGB light bar, on-die ECC and an onboard PMIC 1.

Deep diveThe technical detail

The launch release describes the shared heat spreader in design terms rather than material terms beyond its thickness: “sharp lines, symmetrical layers, and a futuristic triangular motif” on a “robust 2mm-thick aluminum heat spreader” that reduces heat “by more than 10°C/50°F,” plus a “modern light bar” for customizable lighting 1. Both product pages list the same physical dimensions, 133.4 x 43.2 x 8.8mm, and the same single color option, white 32. Neither the release nor either product page names an RGB control application anywhere in the sources checked for this page 132, the same gap this site found on XPOWER Zenith DDR5’s RGB product page 4.

4.Full spec table

SimpleStart here

The CUDIMM SKU runs 8200–9200MT/s at 1.4–1.45V in a single 48GB (24GBx2) kit; the UDIMM SKU runs 6000–8000MT/s at 1.35–1.45V across 16GB, 32GB and 64GB module and kit sizes 32.

The CUDIMM ships in exactly one capacity, a 48GB kit of two 24GB modules — an unusual density for a DDR5 consumer kit, and the only capacity option Silicon Power’s own CUDIMM product page lists 3. The UDIMM sibling, by contrast, spans five capacity configurations across three speed grades 2.

5.Where it’s made

SimpleStart here

Silicon Power assembles and tests both XPOWER Cyclone DDR5 SKUs; none of the three sources checked for this page names a DRAM die supplier 132.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Neither the May 2025 launch release nor either current product page states which company supplies the DRAM dies inside XPOWER Cyclone DDR5, for either the CUDIMM or the UDIMM SKU 132. The release names the clock-driver feature by its function (“built-in Clock Driver (CKD)”) but does not name the chip’s manufacturer either 1. As with Silicon Power’s other product-family pages on this site, none of the company’s own published material here lets a buyer confirm either component’s die source from official sources alone.

6.What it’s compatible with

SimpleStart here

All three sources checked for this page state Intel XMP 3.0 support; the release and the CUDIMM product page also name “Intel Core Ultra desktop processors (Series 2) and Z890 series platforms” as the compatibility target, while the UDIMM product page states XMP 3.0 support without naming a processor generation. None of the three mentions AMD EXPO 132.

Deep diveThe technical detail

The launch release states the pair offers “full support for the latest Intel® Core™ Ultra desktop processors (Series 2) and Intel Z890 series platforms” together with “Intel® XMP 3.0 support” 1. Both the CUDIMM and UDIMM product pages repeat Intel XMP 3.0 support; the CUDIMM page repeats the Core Ultra/Z890 claim specifically, while the UDIMM page states XMP 3.0 compatibility without naming a processor generation 32. No AMD EXPO support, AMD platform, or AMD chipset is named anywhere across the release or either product page 132 — unlike Silicon Power’s own XPOWER Zenith DDR5 RGB line, whose current product page states AMD EXPO support alongside Intel XMP 4. This page reports that difference between two Silicon Power gaming lines rather than assuming either one is more representative of the brand.

7.Official pricing

SimpleStart here

None of the three sources checked for this page — the May 2025 launch release, or either current product page — states a price for XPOWER Cyclone DDR5 132.

Deep diveThe technical detail

This is a consistent gap across every Silicon Power source checked for this page rather than an omission on one page only: the launch release and both product pages describe specifications and features without stating a price anywhere 132. None of the three sources says why, or where a buyer should look instead. The same gap exists on Silicon Power’s own XPOWER Zenith DDR5 line, and on Kingston’s and PNY’s DDR5 lines elsewhere on this site 4.

8.Rated vs. real-world speed

SimpleStart here

Silicon Power’s own material states rated speed, latency and voltage figures but does not publish independent benchmark results for either XPOWER Cyclone DDR5 SKU 1. Both SKUs carry the same Limited Lifetime Warranty as the rest of Silicon Power’s memory line, running “until the end of product life cycle,” a date Silicon Power itself determines 5.

Deep diveThe technical detail

This page reports only Silicon Power’s own published specification claims; it does not independently re-run or cite third-party benchmark-suite results for either XPOWER Cyclone DDR5 SKU. Because the CUDIMM and UDIMM siblings carry materially different speed ceilings, capacities and even a different core technology (an onboard clock driver versus none), a buyer comparing benchmark reviews of “XPOWER Cyclone DDR5” should first confirm which of the two distinct products a given review actually covers — the name alone does not specify which 32. Silicon Power’s warranty page states the life-cycle end date for a Limited Lifetime Warranty product “is determined by SP” — the policy does not publish a schedule or list of discontinued products a buyer could check against, so the warranty’s actual end date for either SKU is not independently verifiable from Silicon Power’s own published material 5.

9.Where this family sits in the lineup

SimpleStart here

Sibling line: XPOWER Zenith DDR5, Silicon Power’s earlier gaming DDR5 family, still sold alongside Cyclone at a lower top speed (6800MT/s on its RGB version) and without a CUDIMM option 4. Within the family: the CUDIMM and UDIMM Cyclone SKUs were announced together on 16 May 2025 but diverge sharply in speed, capacity and clock-driver technology 132.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Silicon Power’s May 2025 release launched the CUDIMM and UDIMM Cyclone SKUs together, under one announcement and one family name, but they are not simply the same module in two finishes the way XPOWER Zenith DDR5’s RGB and non-RGB versions are 1. The CUDIMM carries an onboard clock driver chip that the UDIMM does not, reaches a materially higher speed ceiling (9200MT/s versus 8000MT/s), and ships in exactly one capacity instead of five 32. Neither current product page, nor the launch release, frames the UDIMM as an entry-level or budget option relative to the CUDIMM — this page infers the positioning from the specification gap, because no Silicon Power source checked for this page states it directly 132. XPOWER Zenith DDR5, the brand’s existing gaming DDR5 line, continues to be sold alongside XPOWER Cyclone DDR5 rather than being replaced by it, and tops out lower, at 6800MT/s, with no CUDIMM equivalent 4.

10.Hidden in plain sight

SimpleStart here

Silicon Power’s launch release gives the CUDIMM a named subsection and a headline speed figure, but never states a speed figure for the UDIMM anywhere on the page, even though the release announces both products together and the UDIMM is sold alongside the CUDIMM today 12.

Deep diveThe technical detail

The release’s opening paragraph names both products in one sentence — “the XPOWER Cyclone DDR5 RGB CUDIMM and XPOWER Cyclone DDR5 RGB UDIMM” — and gives no speed figure at all at that point, only “top-tier speeds up to 9200MT/s” attributed to “the company’s first CUDIMM” specifically 1. The release’s only detailed speed claim — “extreme speeds ranging from 8200MT/s to 9200MT/s” — sits inside a subsection headed “CUDIMM: Power Meets Precision,” naming the CUDIMM as its subject twice in the same sentence 1. No comparable subsection or speed figure for the UDIMM exists anywhere in the release — a reader who finishes it knows the CUDIMM’s exact speed range and nothing at all about how fast its announced sibling runs 1. That figure only appears on the UDIMM’s own current product page: 6000, 7200 and 8000MT/s, a materially lower and narrower range than the CUDIMM’s 2. This page reports the release’s own structure plainly — it does not state a wrong figure for either product, it simply never mentions one for the UDIMM at all — rather than treating the gap as a headline/body mismatch the way it would read if the release had misapplied the CUDIMM’s figure to both 132.
