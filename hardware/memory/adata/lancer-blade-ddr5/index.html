XPG LANCER BLADE DDR5: The Complete Guide

1.At a glance

SimpleStart here

LANCER BLADE DDR5 is XPG’s low-profile gaming memory line, launched 13 July 2023 in two versions, LANCER BLADE DDR5 and LANCER BLADE RGB DDR5, at “top speeds of 6000MT/s and 6400MT/s” 1. XPG’s own category listing tags it “Low Profile Heatsink,” and its spec tabs list capacities from 8GB to 32GB 23.

Deep diveThe technical detail

The launch release headline is “XPG LANCER BLADE DDR5 Launches with Low-Profile Heatsink and High Price-Performance,” under the section heading “The Ideal Upgrade in a Small Speedy Package” 1. It describes “its low-profile heatsink design” for “small cases with limited space,” names “two models with top speeds of 6000MT/s and 6400MT/s,” and states capacities of “16GB/32GB” 1. XPG’s current spec tabs give a wider range than the release did: 8GB, 16GB, 24GB and 32GB on the non-RGB version, and the same on RGB 34. The release also introduces two other products in the same announcement: DDR4 counterparts SPECTRIX D35G RGB and GAMMIX D35, and a separate, faster LANCER RGB ROG CERTIFIED DDR5 reaching “6600MT/s” standard and “6800MT/s” in ROG Certified mode 1 — neither is LANCER BLADE and neither is covered further on this page.

2.Launch and lineup

SimpleStart here

XPG introduced LANCER BLADE DDR5 in July 2023 as a shorter, lighter alternative to its existing LANCER RGB DDR5 line, at lower top speeds but in more capacities 15.

Deep diveThe technical detail

The July 2023 release opens by describing XPG as “the e-sports arm of ADATA, the world’s leading memory brand” 1. It names no executive and carries no attributed quotation. Its central technical claim is unusually specific for an XPG release: “By utilizing high-quality carefully selected Hynix chips, these memory modules are low latency and easy to overclock while allowing gamers to enable a hassle-free speed boost with one-click overclocking” 1 — see Hidden in plain sight below for how that compares with every other XPG release checked on this site.

3.What’s inside the module

SimpleStart here

XPG’s product page calls the shape “Compact Low-Profile Heatsink Design,” under the tagline “SLEEK EFFICIENCY, COMPACT POWER” — built to fit “smaller PC cases” without interfering with tower CPU coolers 3.

Deep diveThe technical detail

The product page states: “The LANCER BLADE boasts a low-profile heatsink that fits perfectly in smaller PC cases and avoids interference with tower CPU coolers,” with “sleek geometric lines” for “visual appeal” 3. The non-RGB version measures 133.35 x 33.8 x 7.8mm at 32.9g / 36.2g; the RGB version is noticeably taller and heavier at 133.35 x 40 x 7.86mm and 41.11g / 44.41g 34 — RGB adds about 6mm of height and 8–9g, more than the light bar alone typically costs on other XPG lines. Both versions ship in Black and White 34.

XPG’s spec tabs list LANCER BLADE DDR5 at 4800–6400MT/s tested, in 8GB, 16GB, 24GB and 32GB capacities, with the same figures on both the RGB and non-RGB versions except for height and weight 34.

5.Where it’s made

SimpleStart here

XPG’s launch release names its DRAM chip supplier: “high-quality carefully selected Hynix chips” 1. The current product pages do not repeat this — they say only “High-quality ICs and PCBs” 34.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Every other XPG source checked on this site — the ARMAX DDR5 launch release, both ARMAX spec pages, and LANCER BLADE’s own current product pages — describes components only in general terms: “strictly selected high-quality ICs,” “strict component selection and screening procedures,” “High-quality ICs and PCBs” 34. The July 2023 LANCER BLADE launch release is the one exception found on this site: “By utilizing high-quality carefully selected Hynix chips, these memory modules are low latency and easy to overclock” 1. “Hynix” is almost certainly SK hynix, one of the three companies that manufacture DRAM dies — but the release does not spell out the full corporate name, and no other XPG source checked for this page repeats the claim, so it cannot be confirmed as a standing policy for the line rather than a one-time detail in a single release.

6.What it’s compatible with

SimpleStart here

LANCER BLADE supports both overclocking profiles: XPG’s category listing tags both versions “AMD EXPO/XMP3.0 Overclocking” 2, and the product page repeats Intel XMP 3.0 and AMD EXPO support 3.

Deep diveThe technical detail

The launch release states: “At the same time, The XPG LANCER BLADE series supports Intel XMP 3.0 and AMD EXPO and is compatible with the latest Intel and AMD platforms” 1. The current product page separately states “With support for Intel XMP 3.0, get overclocking easily without the need to hassle with BIOS settings” 3 — that ease-of-use framing is attached to XMP in the source, not to EXPO. XPG publishes no motherboard qualified-vendor list on either LANCER BLADE page; the only compatibility tool linked is XPG’s site-wide “Find Compatible DRAM/SSD” search, not a LANCER BLADE-specific list 3.

7.Official pricing

SimpleStart here

XPG publishes no price for LANCER BLADE DDR5 in any source checked for this page 134.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Neither the July 2023 launch release nor either current product page states a price for any LANCER BLADE SKU 134. This matches every other XPG source checked on this site, and every other memory brand this site has checked: module makers state specifications and leave pricing to resellers.

8.Rated vs. real-world speed

SimpleStart here

XPG publishes rated speeds and latencies but no independent benchmark results for LANCER BLADE DDR5 3. Both versions carry a “Limited lifetime warranty” per XPG’s own spec tabs 34.

Deep diveThe technical detail

This page reports XPG’s own published specification claims and does not re-run or cite third-party benchmark results. Neither spec tab maps its tested CAS latencies to a specific speed grade 34. On warranty, both spec tabs state “Limited lifetime warranty,” with no term in years, no regional cap and no exclusion list stated on either page 34 — the only route to warranty terms is XPG’s site-wide “Product Support” and “RMA” footer links, not anything on the LANCER BLADE pages themselves.

9.Where this family sits in the lineup

SimpleStart here

Sits below LANCER RGB DDR5: XPG’s own category listing places LANCER BLADE alongside LANCER RGB, LANCER and NOVAKEY RGB — seven other DDR5 series, with no tier labels stated between them 2.

Deep diveThe technical detail

LANCER RGB DDR5 reaches higher on every figure that matters: 16/32/48GB capacities against LANCER BLADE’s 8/16/24/32GB, test speeds up to 8000MT/s against LANCER BLADE’s 6400MT/s ceiling, and a taller 43.56mm heatsink at 72.4g against LANCER BLADE’s 33.8mm/40mm and roughly 33–44g 534. XPG’s own product pages put LANCER RGB DDR5 and the other LANCER BLADE variant — the RGB page cross-sells the non-RGB one and vice versa — in front of shoppers under “Popular Choices,” alongside two DDR4 lines (SPECTRIX D35G, GAMMIX D35) 34 — without stating which to choose or how the lines differ, the same pattern this site found on XPG’s ARMAX DDR5 page.

10.Hidden in plain sight

SimpleStart here

Of every XPG source checked on this site, only one names a DRAM chip supplier: LANCER BLADE’s July 2023 launch release says the modules use “high-quality carefully selected Hynix chips” 1. XPG’s own current product pages for the same line do not repeat it 34.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Naming a die supplier at all is the exception on this site, not just for XPG — Kingston, Corsair, PNY, Lexar and Silicon Power’s pages checked here all stop at “strictly selected” or “high-quality” components with no company named. XPG’s own ARMAX DDR5 launch release and product pages follow that same pattern 1. LANCER BLADE’s July 2023 release breaks it, once, with a specific sentence: “By utilizing high-quality carefully selected Hynix chips, these memory modules are low latency and easy to overclock while allowing gamers to enable a hassle-free speed boost with one-click overclocking” 1.

Two things temper how far this claim reaches. First, neither current LANCER BLADE product page repeats “Hynix” anywhere — both describe components only as “High-quality ICs and PCBs” 34, so a shopper reading the product page today would not see it. Second, “Hynix” is not a full corporate name — the release does not write out “SK hynix,” and no other XPG source checked for this page confirms whether that supplier relationship is still current or was specific to modules sold at launch in 2023. This page reports the sentence as written, once, in one release, rather than treating it as a standing fact about every LANCER BLADE module sold since.
