XPG LANCER RGB DDR5: The Complete Guide

XPG’s flagship DDR5 line and the module this site has found cited as a comparison point on every other XPG DDR5 page. Simple to deep dive, every number links to its source.

1.At a glance

SimpleStart here

LANCER RGB DDR5 is XPG’s flagship DDR5 gaming memory line, launched 26 October 2021 as “XPG’s first DDR5 gaming memory” 1. XPG’s current spec tab lists it at up to 8000MT/s in 16GB, 32GB and 48GB capacities 2.

Deep diveThe technical detail

XPG’s own product page opens with the tagline “UP YOUR GAME” 2. The line launched in October 2021 at a single 5200MT/s, 16GB grade, with a 6000MT/s version announced as “coming soon” 1; the spec tab checked for this page shows the ceiling has since risen to 8000MT/s across three capacities (16GB, 32GB, 48GB) and four test speeds (6000, 6400, 7200, 8000MT/s) 2. XPG’s own current site groups this launch generation under a separate, non-RGB sibling too — LANCER DDR5, sold today at up to 6400MT/s 3.

2.Launch and lineup

SimpleStart here

The October 2021 announcement frames LANCER as XPG “ushers in the DDR5 era for gaming memory” 1. It shipped in two versions at launch — plain LANCER DDR5 and LANCER RGB DDR5 — with RGB effects including “static, breathing, and comet” plus a “Music Mode” that syncs lighting to audio 1.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Per the press coverage checked for this page, XPG’s own October 2021 release states: “XPG today announces the XPG LANCER DDR5 memory module” 1. At launch it came in a single 5200MT/s, 16GB single- or dual-channel kit, with “6000MT/s version coming soon” 1. RGB lighting supported “static, breathing, and comet” effects, plus a “Music Mode” syncing lights to songs 1. XPG’s current product page keeps the same “UP YOUR GAME” tagline, under a lighting section headed “RGB Your Way” and a performance section headed “A New Speed Benchmark in Gaming Memory” 2 — five years and a roughly 54% speed increase (5200 to 8000MT/s) after the original launch claim.

3.What’s inside the module

SimpleStart here

XPG’s spec tab lists a 133.35 x 43.56 x 8mm heatsink at 72.4g, in Black or White 2. The module carries a Power Management IC (PMIC) and on-die ECC, both required by the DDR5 standard rather than XPG additions, as this site’s memory glossary explains.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Dimensions and weight are identical across LANCER RGB DDR5 and the non-RGB LANCER DDR5 sibling — both list 133.35 x 43.56 x 8mm at 72.4g 23 — so the RGB version adds lighting without changing the heatsink’s footprint or mass, at least as far as XPG’s own published dimensions show. RGB is described as controllable via “XPG Prime lighting control software” or third-party motherboard RGB software, the same pattern this site has found on other current XPG RGB lines 2. XPG’s own coverage for the October 2021 launch describes PMIC and on-die ECC as standard inclusions rather than differentiators 1.

5.Where it’s made

SimpleStart here

Neither the current product page nor the 2021 launch coverage checked for this page names a DRAM die supplier for LANCER RGB DDR5 21 — the same silence found on every XPG line except LANCER BLADE DDR5’s single 2023 release.

Deep diveThe technical detail

The product page and the press coverage of the October 2021 launch both describe the module’s PMIC and on-die ECC without naming which company fabricates the DRAM dies themselves 21. This is the pattern found across every XPG DDR5 line checked on this site with one exception — LANCER BLADE DDR5’s July 2023 release, which named “Hynix” — and LANCER RGB DDR5 does not repeat that exception; no source checked for this page names a chip supplier for it.

6.What it’s compatible with

SimpleStart here

LANCER RGB DDR5 supports both overclocking profiles: XPG’s spec tab and the 2021 launch coverage both describe Intel XMP 3.0 support, with AMD EXPO added to the current product page 21.

Deep diveThe technical detail

XPG publishes no motherboard qualified-vendor list specific to LANCER RGB DDR5; the only compatibility route on the product page is XPG’s site-wide “Find Compatible DRAM/SSD” search, not a LANCER-specific list 2. RGB lighting is controlled through “XPG Prime lighting control software,” with the current page also describing compatibility with third-party motherboard RGB ecosystems, the same pattern this site has found on other current XPG RGB lines 2. The 2021 launch coverage describes onboard lighting modes — “static, breathing, and comet” — plus a “Music Mode” that syncs lighting to audio, run independently of any software sync 1; the current product page does not repeat these named effects, so whether they persist on today’s SKUs is not stated by the sources checked for this page.

7.Official pricing

SimpleStart here

XPG’s current product page publishes no price for LANCER RGB DDR5 2. Its 2021 launch is the one exception this site has found on any XPG DDR5 line: the press coverage checked for this page states “XPG LANCER RGB DDR5 /$399.99” at launch 1.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Every other XPG DDR5 family page this site has built states the same finding: XPG publishes no price and leaves it to resellers. LANCER RGB DDR5’s current product page follows that pattern 2. But the October 2021 launch coverage checked for this page is the single exception found so far — it states plain MSRPs for both versions: “XPG LANCER DDR5 /$349.99” and “XPG LANCER RGB DDR5 /$399.99,” with availability “on Amazon and Shop XPG” 1. This page treats that as true of the 2021 launch specifically, not as a standing fact about today’s product — no source checked for this page states when or why XPG stopped publishing a price, or whether $399.99 still applies to any SKU sold today.

8.Rated vs. real-world speed

SimpleStart here

XPG publishes rated speeds and latencies but no independent benchmark results for LANCER RGB DDR5 2. Independent press reports a 12,762 MT/s overclocking record set on this line in April 2025 4.

Deep diveThe technical detail

This page reports XPG’s own published specification claims and does not re-run or cite independent benchmark testing of rated speeds. Separately, press coverage checked for this page reports that overclocker “HiCookie” (Gigabyte) set a 12,762 MT/s HWBOT-certified record on 2 April 2025 using “LANCER RGB DDR5 de 8000 MT/s” modules, a Gigabyte Z890 AORUS TACHYON ICE motherboard, an Intel Core Ultra 9 285K, and liquid-nitrogen cooling 4. This is an extreme, LN2-cooled result, not a figure achievable at the module’s rated 8000MT/s under normal cooling, and the article does not quote XPG directly about the record 4.

9.Where this family sits in the lineup

SimpleStart here

XPG’s original and still-fastest mainstream DDR5 line: LANCER RGB DDR5 reaches 8000MT/s, ahead of every other XPG DDR5 family this site has checked, and is the line most often cited as a comparison point on XPG’s other product pages 5.

Deep diveThe technical detail

LANCER RGB DDR5’s 8000MT/s ceiling and 43.56mm heatsink height are the figures this site has used as the upper reference point when building ARMAX DDR5, LANCER BLADE DDR5 and NOVAKEY RGB DDR5 — all three sit below it on speed, and NOVAKEY RGB DDR5 sits above it on heatsink height (45.5mm) while below it on weight (53.23g vs. LANCER RGB’s 72.4g) 2. XPG’s own product page puts LANCER BLADE RGB DDR5, LANCER BLADE DDR5 6, and two DDR4 lines (SPECTRIX D35G, GAMMIX D35) in front of shoppers under “Popular Choices,” without stating which to pick or how the lines differ, the same pattern found on every other XPG family page this site has built 2. XPG’s category listing also carries a single-SKU “LANCER RGB DDR5 GOLDEN HORSE EDITION” 5 — a red-heatsink, 16GB, 6000MT/s variant 7 with no launch story or explanation on the sources checked for this page 7.

10.Hidden in plain sight

SimpleStart here

XPG’s own October 2021 launch coverage is the only place this site has found XPG naming a price for any of its DDR5 memory: $349.99 for LANCER DDR5, $399.99 for LANCER RGB DDR5 1. Every current XPG product page checked, including this one, publishes no price at all 2.

Deep diveThe technical detail

Across every XPG DDR5 family page this site has built — ARMAX, LANCER BLADE, NOVAKEY RGB, and this one — the standing finding has been the same: XPG states specifications and leaves pricing to resellers, publishing no MSRP anywhere on its current site 2. LANCER RGB DDR5’s own October 2021 launch breaks that pattern, the only exception found so far: independent press covering the announcement states plain dollar figures — “XPG LANCER DDR5 /$349.99” and “XPG LANCER RGB DDR5 /$399.99” — attributed to the launch release itself 1. No source checked for this page explains when XPG stopped publishing a price, or whether it was a one-time launch practice XPG later abandoned across its whole DDR5 lineup. This page states the fact as true of the 2021 launch and does not extend it to any other line or any later date.
