3.The story

SimpleStart here

Transcend's own history page cannot be read from here. What follows is what the Taiwan registry, the stock exchange and USPTO's trademark database record.

  • 1989 The Taiwan registry's establishment date for 創見資訊股份有限公司 corresponds to 30 August 1989 4.
  • 1997 A first U.S. "TRANSCEND" trademark application is filed in August, then abandoned the following January without ever registering 9.
  • 2001 Transcend lists on the Taiwan Stock Exchange, stock code 2451, on 3 May 5.
  • 2004–2008 Two separate "STOREJET" applications are filed — the first abandoned in 2005, the second registered in 2008 10 11.
  • 2012–2014 Two marks still live today are registered: "T-TRANSCEND" in 2012 and "JETFLASH" in 2014 6 7.
  • 2015 The 2008-registered "STOREJET" mark is cancelled for a missed Section 8 filing 11.
DeeperThe detail

Read together, Transcend's five checked trademark records are the most eventful of any brand this section has built thin — two abandoned applications, one registered-then-cancelled mark, and two that remain live and actively maintained. The cancellation follows the same pattern this section's G.Skill and ADATA guides both recorded: a registration allowed to lapse years after it was granted, for a missed Section 8 filing rather than any dispute over the mark itself 11. Notably, StoreJet as a product line did not disappear when that 2008 registration was cancelled in 2015 — the name remains in active use, per product listings found in this session's research, even though the specific trademark registration covering "multimedia players" under that name is no longer live.

The earliest activity in this record — a 1997 U.S. filing abandoned in 1998, at a California address rather than the Taipei one every later filing uses — suggests Transcend's U.S. trademark presence took a few years to settle into the pattern its later, successful filings follow 9 10.

ExpertFor specialists

No source read for this guide explains why the 2008-registered "STOREJET" mark specifically (as opposed to any of Transcend's other marks) was allowed to lapse in 2015. This page does not speculate about a company decision behind an absence of renewal filing — it reports the cancellation exactly as TSDR shows it, and notes only that the product name itself continued in market use independent of that one registration's fate 11.

4.The lineup

Search results from this session additionally surface product names not mentioned in that 2009 report at all — DrivePro body cameras and dash cameras, JetDrive Mac-upgrade SSDs, and embedded/industrial flash solutions — indicating the catalogue has broadened considerably since 2009, exactly as would be expected, though no current source read for this guide confirms today's full range directly.

12.Hidden in plain sight

SimpleStart here

Three things a casual buyer would not find without checking Taiwan's stock exchange and USPTO's trademark database directly, both confirmed for this guide.

  • Transcend is a publicly traded company, listed on the Taiwan Stock Exchange main board since 3 May 2001, stock code 2451 — a different, more heavily regulated listing tier than ADATA's Taipei Exchange (TPEx) listing 5.

DeeperThe detail

The TWSE listing is worth reading in its proper context: a main-board listing carries continuous disclosure obligations under Taiwan securities law that a TPEx (over-the-counter) listing like ADATA's does not carry in quite the same form, and the fact that this check could nonetheless reach almost nothing current from Transcend's own investor-relations pages says more about this environment's specific access limits than about what Transcend discloses to the market through Taiwan's own official channels 5.

ExpertFor specialists

Worth stating plainly, since it could otherwise read as more dramatic than it is: a cancelled trademark registration means the specific legal right lapsed, not that the underlying product or brand name disappeared — StoreJet, per this session's own search results, remains an active Transcend product line today, independent of the fate of one 2008 U.S. registration covering it 11.
