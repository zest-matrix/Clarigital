Kingston Technology: The Complete Guide

Two thirds of every memory module sold by a third-party brand carries this name, and almost nobody knows it. Kingston buys its chips from Samsung, SK hynix and Micron, says so plainly, and publishes no accounts and no prices. What it makes and what it adds. Three reading levels, every number sourced.

1.At a glance

SimpleStart here

Kingston Technology makes memory modules, not memory chips. It buys the chips from the companies documented elsewhere in this section, puts them on its own circuit boards, tests them and sells them under its own name 1.

What it makesDDR5 and DDR4 modules, SSDs, USB drives, memory cards and encrypted storage 2 3
How big66% of the third-party DRAM module market in 2024, its 22nd consecutive year at number one 4
Whose chipsMicron, SK hynix and Samsung — Kingston names all three itself 1
Any HBM?No. Kingston defines HBM in its own glossary and sells none 5
DeeperThe detail

Kingston is the first company in this section that does not make memory. That changes what the interesting questions are. Not which node, not which fab — but whose chips are inside, what the brand adds on top of them, and whether it tells you.

Kingston’s answer to the third question is unusually direct. Its own homepage title opens "Kingston Technology - Largest Independent Manufacturer of Memory Products" 6, and its engineering blog states plainly: "We leverage our strong relationships with the world’s leading DRAM suppliers—Micron, SK Hynix, and Samsung—to ensure the DRAM components we use meet our stringent requirements for reliability and performance" 1. Most module brands never name a supplier at all.

ExpertFor specialists

Kingston’s EMEA edition of the same announcement puts the self-description differently again — "world’s largest independent manufacturer of memory products" 7. Its wording varies by page, so this guide quotes each claim to the page it came from rather than treating them as one sentence.

The scale is the part most readers will not expect. Kingston’s own announcement of the 2024 rankings gives it 66% market share of a US$13.3 billion market, with the top five suppliers accounting for 81% of sales and second place on 5% 4. A single private company holds two thirds of a market and its nearest rival holds a twentieth of it.

Every share figure Kingston publishes is attributed to a third-party tracker rather than measured by Kingston, and the company labels them that way 8 4. It publishes no revenue of its own — see The company.

DeeperThe detail

Kingston is one of the largest private companies in the United States and says so through a ranking rather than a number: it announced taking "the #28 spot on Forbes’ list of America’s Top Private Companies for 2025" and being the "top ‘Technology Hardware & Equipment’ company on the list" 13. The revenue figure attached to that ranking — "$14.4 billion in annual revenue, measured as of 2025" — is Forbes’s estimate reported in the local business press, not a Kingston disclosure 16.

Two absences are worth stating precisely. Kingston publishes no leadership page and names no current chief executive or president. John Tu and David Sun appear on its history timeline as the 1987 founders, with no current title attached 12. The executives it quotes in releases are business managers and regional vice-presidents 4 17. And the employee count is not on any About page — it appears in the modern slavery statement, which also records "at least 150 suppliers worldwide" 14.

ExpertFor specialists

The corporate group is larger than the two named entities. Kingston’s modern slavery statement adds Kingston Technology Europe Co LLP and Kingston Digital Europe Co LLP 14; its contacts page lists separate legal entities in Taiwan (Kingston Technology Far East Corp.), China, the United Kingdom, Ireland, Germany, France, Brazil, Mexico and India 11; and its JEDEC membership is held not by either American entity but by the Irish one, Kingston Technology International Ltd. 18 11.

One entity matters more than the others and is easy to miss. Advanced Validation Labs, Inc. appears as a named certificate holder on Kingston’s own ISO 9001 certificate alongside Kingston Technology Company, Inc. and Kingston Digital, Inc. 10, and Kingston lists it among its divisions 19. That matters because AVL is the laboratory Kingston says performs its DRAM qualification — see How a module is built.

3.The story

SimpleStart here
  • 1987 "Engineers John Tu and David Sun starts Kingston Technology in Orange County, California" — the grammar is Kingston’s 12.
  • 1988 "Kingston offers an unheard-of Lifetime Warranty for its DRAM memory products" 12.
  • 1995 Sales "exceed $1.3 billion" 12.
  • 1996 "Softbank® Corporation of Japan acquires eighty percent of Kingston for a total of $1.5 billion" 12.
  • 1999 "John Tu and David Sun buy back the eighty percent of Kingston owned by Softbank for $450 million" 12.
  • 2002 "Kingston forms the gaming division of HyperX" 12.
  • 2021 Sells HyperX’s peripherals business to HP and rebrands its gaming memory as Kingston FURY 20 17.
  • 2024 "Kingston named top third-party DRAM module supplier in the world for the 22nd consecutive year" 12.
DeeperThe detail

The Softbank episode is the most remarkable thing in Kingston’s history and the company records both halves of it on its own timeline without comment. Softbank bought 80% for $1.5 billion in 1996; the founders bought the same 80% back for $450 million in 1999 12. Three years, and the stake returned for under a third of what was paid for it. Kingston states the two figures and draws no conclusion; a reader can do the arithmetic.

The 1988 lifetime warranty is the other founding decision that still shapes the company. It remains in force today across memory modules and flash cards 21, and it is the main thing a module brand can offer that a chip maker does not.

ExpertFor specialists

The HyperX split in 2021 is the episode most often got wrong, so here it is in Kingston’s own words. HP paid "$425M, subject to customary working capital and other adjustments, to acquire HyperX’s gaming peripherals portfolio" — "headsets, keyboards, mice, mouse pads, USB microphones, and console accessories" — and crucially, "Kingston will retain the DRAM, flash, and SSD products for gamers and enthusiasts" 20.

Four months later those retained products acquired a new name. Kingston’s launch release: "Building on the incredible success of the HyperX memory products, Kingston has rebranded its DRAM, flash and SSD gaming line into Kingston FURY", adding "Behind the scenes, it has always been Kingston: Kingston engineering, Kingston manufacturing, Kingston testing and world-renowned Kingston customer service" 17. The continuity is real enough that Kingston’s current RGB software still "supports legacy HyperX RGB enabled memory products" 22. So HyperX memory did not move to HP and did not stop — it was renamed.

Kingston organises itself by route to market as much as by product. ExpertFor specialists

IronKey is the outlier in the portfolio and has its own history. Kingston’s page records that "IronKey Inc. was an internet security and data privacy company established in 1996" which received "a grant by the Department of Homeland Security in 2004 to develop a secure, tamper-resistant USB Flash drive", and describes the present line as offering "XTS-AES 256-bit hardware encryption", FIPS 140-3 Level 3 and FIPS 140-2 Level 3 validation, with "built-in protections against BadUSB and Brute Force attacks" 3. Kingston’s account of how it got there: after its 2016 purchase of IronKey, "‘Kingston IronKey’ was born - and became the largest, best-known maker of hardware-encrypted USB drives in the world" 3. That is a claim about the acquired brand, not about Kingston as a whole.

That line is a genuine differentiator: hardware encryption validated to a federal standard is not something a module assembler can buy from a chip maker, and it is the part of Kingston’s business least exposed to DRAM pricing.

5.How a module is built

SimpleStart here

Kingston does not make memory chips. What it does is buy them, mount them on boards it designs, and test the result — and the testing is what it sells.

  1. Qualify the supplier. "Kingston strictly qualifies our DRAM suppliers" 27.
  2. Test every part. "100-percent production testing of all finished goods. This means testing every cell on every chip on every module" 27.
  3. Stress it. "Kingston utilizes a patented Dynamic Server Burn-In process. Here, the DRAM module is pushed to its limits, deliberately stressing an equivalent of three months of heavy utilization in a 24-hour period" 1.
  4. Check it again. "Over 35 quality control checkpoints are integrated into Kingston’s manufacturing, testing, packaging, warehousing, and shipping processes" 1.
DeeperThe detail

Kingston’s own explanation of why 100% testing is necessary is the clearest statement anywhere of what a module brand is for: "Slight physical variances among component manufacturers make this a necessary step", and its "proprietary production test software checks for speed, proper addressing and runs a variety of patterns to check for weaknesses in the memory chip itself" 27.

Read that alongside the supplier list. Kingston buys from three different chip makers 1, whose parts differ physically, and then tests every finished module so the customer does not have to care which one they got. That is the product.

The stages Kingston describes run from design and prototype testing — specification, compatibility, signal integrity, reliability, guardband and automated test equipment — through DRAM qualification, then 100% production testing, then post-production sample quality assurance 27.

ExpertFor specialists

One structural point that Kingston’s own pages establish but never state together. Kingston says "Kingston uses services provided by AVL for DRAM qualification" and introduces AVL as "a technology services company specializing in the pre-production and post-production testing and validation of memory to industry OEMs" 27 — phrasing that reads like an outside laboratory. Its history page records AVL being "launched as an independent memory testing and validation lab" in 2000 12. Whatever it was then, today it is a Kingston division 19 and a named holder on Kingston’s own ISO 9001 certificate alongside the two main Kingston entities 10. The qualification is in-house. That is not a criticism — vertical integration of test is a reasonable thing to build — but anyone reading AVL as a present-day independent third party has it wrong.

The external validation Kingston does have is platform qualification, and it describes the process in detail: work with Intel begins "a year or more before systems are released, where Kingston has access to Intel reference motherboards and processors to test memory prototypes", and "A passing test results in Kingston’s promotion to the list of Intel platform validated memory products", after which modules are qualified again on Intel’s own systems 28. Kingston notes this extends to "legacy Intel server systems" 28. Its consumer parts are "independently validated on AMD and Intel reference platforms, then tested to support thousands of systems, including major OEMs and motherboard manufacturers" 29.

DeeperThe detail

The aggregate figures are the ones worth remembering. Kingston operates "more than 63 state-of-the-art Surface Mount Technology (SMT) lines that produce more than seventeen million customized modules of DRAM / Flash and SSD products each month", from 18 regional offices, working with a distribution network in "more than 175 locations", and states that "Each module is 100% tested to ensure the highest quality and reliability" 15.

Seventeen million modules a month from a company with 2,800 employees 15 14 is a useful sense of what module assembly actually is: highly automated surface-mount lines, not a labour-intensive business.

ExpertFor specialists

The Fountain Valley certificate is worth reading closely because it contradicts the common description of Kingston as an assembler. Three separate addresses appear — 17600, 17665-A and 17665-B Newhope Street — with three different scopes: "Sales and support of computer memory, enhancement products, and digital media"; "Design, manufacturing, testing, packaging, and support of computer memory, enhancement products, and digital media"; and "Design, sales, testing, and support" 10. Design and packaging, not only assembly and test.

Kingston’s certified sites for environmental and health-and-safety management are Fountain Valley, China, Taiwan, the United Kingdom and Ireland, with Taiwan additionally holding ISO 50001 and CNS 45001 32.

The China site is Kingston’s own claim and a striking one. Its history timeline records that in 2005 "Kingston opens the world’s largest memory module manufacturing facility in Shanghai, China" 12. The same timeline dates the other plants: Taiwan and Dublin both in 1997, when Kingston "establishes the headquarters for Asia-Pacific operations including a manufacturing facility and offices in Taiwan" and opens "a manufacturing/logistics facility…in Dublin, Ireland" 12.

Two limits on the detail. Kingston publishes no per-site headcount, floor area or capacity split — only the aggregate figures above 15. And its current contacts page lists only a Beijing office for China 11, while the China ISO 9001 certificate that would give the manufacturing site’s present address is an image-only file this check could not read 30. So the Shanghai plant is Kingston-stated, but its current address and status could not be confirmed here.

7.How the memory works

SimpleStart here

A memory module is a small circuit board with DRAM chips on it, a power-management chip, a tiny identification chip that tells the computer what the module is, and a connector. The chips come from a chip maker; everything else is the module maker’s work 27.

DeeperThe detail

The part a buyer notices is speed profiles. A module has a safe default speed defined by JEDEC, and faster settings stored on the module that a motherboard can apply. Kingston’s own glossary defines the two systems: XMP as "Intel Extreme Memory Profile, pre-programmed timings on overclock modules", and AMD EXPO as "AMD’s Extended Profiles for Overclocking. Memory modules featuring AMD EXPO profiles feature specific speeds, timings, and voltages optimized for AMD systems" 5.

Its DDR5 parts carry both: Intel XMP 3.0 offering "Advanced pre-optimized timings, speed and voltage for overclocking performance and save new user-customizable profiles utilizing a programmable PMIC", and EXPO certification that "include[s] Intel XMP 3.0 profiles, enabling compatibility with the platform of your choice" 26.

ExpertFor specialists

Kingston is a JEDEC member — independently listed in the association’s directory as Kingston Technology International Ltd. 18 — and describes the role as participatory rather than passive: "As a long-standing JEDEC member, Kingston helps shape industry standards and maintains an unwavering commitment to quality, consistency, and long-term performance" 29.

That membership is what makes the module business possible at all. A module built to a JEDEC standard works in a socket designed to the same standard, whoever made the chips inside it — which is precisely why a company can buy from three competing chip makers and ship one product line.

DeeperThe detail

The 2025 COMPUTEX announcement is the best single snapshot of Kingston’s technical range, and it is notable for how little of it is DRAM: a FURY Renegade G5 client SSD at "up to 14,800/14,000MB/s"; a DC3000ME enterprise drive rated "up to 14,000MB/s" read and "2,800,000 read IOPS" in capacities to 15.36TB; the 8,800MT/s CUDIMM; a FURY Impact DDR5 CAMM2 module "up to 128GB"; and a USB drive 24.

CAMM2 is worth noting — it is the newer laptop module format intended to replace SODIMM, and Kingston shipping one places it at the current edge of module design rather than behind it 24.

ExpertFor specialists

What Kingston does not publish is as consistent as what it does. There is no per-site capacity, no yield figure, no bill-of-materials disclosure, and no statement of which supplier’s chips are in any given part number 15 1. The supplier list is given at company level, not at product level — so a buyer knows Kingston buys from three makers but cannot tell which one is in the box.

That is not evasion so much as how the business works: a module brand qualifies multiple sources precisely so it can switch between them, and the 100% testing regime exists to make the switch invisible 27.

9.Official pricing

SimpleStart here

Kingston publishes no prices. This check found no price, no recommended price and no currency figure on its product pages or its where-to-buy page 26 33.

That is the same posture as the chip makers documented earlier in this section, and it is not what a consumer brand usually does.

DeeperThe detail

The product pages carry a "Buy Now" control and an Apple Pay control, but both are scripted widgets rather than links, and no price appears in the page itself 26. The where-to-buy page lists distributors and retailers and offers no direct storefront 33.

One honest limit on that finding: this check could confirm that no price appears in the served page, but could not open the scripted buy widget to see whether a price appears inside it. So the accurate claim is that Kingston publishes no prices on its product pages, not that no Kingston checkout exists anywhere 26.

ExpertFor specialists

What Kingston sells instead of a price is a warranty, and it is the oldest thing about the company. The lifetime warranty introduced in 1988 12 still covers memory modules across ValueRAM, HyperX, Kingston FURY, Server Premier and retail memory, and flash memory cards 21. Kingston defines the term: "Product lifetime is defined as the normal time expectancy for the use of products in the industry", with a ten-year definition applied in Russia 21.

The warranty also carries the company’s main commercial defence: "To the extent permitted by applicable laws, products not imported or sold through authorized Kingston dealers or distributors are not covered by this warranty. This is to ensure certification of quality" 21. For a brand whose product is trust rather than silicon, tying the warranty to the authorised channel is the enforcement mechanism.

10.Whose chips are inside

SimpleStart here

Kingston makes no memory chips at all. It buys them, and — unusually — it says from whom.

From its own engineering blog: "We leverage our strong relationships with the world’s leading DRAM suppliers—Micron, SK Hynix, and Samsung—to ensure the DRAM components we use meet our stringent requirements for reliability and performance" 1. Kingston’s own capitalisation of "SK Hynix" is preserved here.

DeeperThe detail

All three named suppliers have their own guides in this section. Kingston buys from the three largest DRAM makers in the world and frames the multiple sourcing as deliberate: "Having a strict component qualification process ensures consistency among our suppliers, delivering a higher-quality product to our customers with a diverse supply base" 1.

Diverse supply is a real advantage and a real dependency at once. Kingston cannot make a chip if the three of them will not sell to it, and in a shortage the chip makers allocate to their own priorities first. Micron demonstrated exactly that in December 2025 by shutting down its own consumer brand, Crucial, to redirect supply to data-centre customers — a decision documented on that brand’s page in this section.

The naming appears on an engineering blog post, not in Kingston’s formal reporting. Its responsible minerals reports and supplier code of conduct describe suppliers by category only and name no semiconductor company 34 35 36. Kingston does publish supplier-diligence figures — "at least 150 suppliers worldwide", and for 2025, "6 supplier audits were conducted" and "116 supplier assessments were conducted based upon social criteria" 14 — and reports that "100% of the smelters and refiners reported to be in our supply chain participated in RMAP" 34.

ExpertFor specialists

Kingston sells no HBM. It defines the term in its own glossary — "A newer DRAM memory technology developed by AMD in 2008 to address the increasing demand for high performance, high capacity memory to support GPUs, with lower power requirements" 5 — and no HBM product appears in any Kingston catalogue found in this check 2 23 24.

Its position relative to HBM is the opposite of a supplier’s: Kingston sits downstream of it. Every wafer the chip makers move to HBM is a wafer not making the DDR5 that Kingston buys. Its COMPUTEX 2025 theme was "Kingston Powers Tomorrow: Committed to the AI Future", and the products under that banner are fast client and enterprise SSDs and high-frequency DDR5 24 — AI-adjacent, but on the buying side of the squeeze rather than the selling side.

11.Who buys it and why

SimpleStart here

Kingston sells almost entirely through other people. Its where-to-buy page lists distributors including Ingram Micro, TD Synnex, ASI, D&H and Malabs; industrial distributors including Arrow, Avnet, Digikey, Mouser and Future Electronics; resellers including CDW, Insight, SHI and Connection; and retailers including Amazon, Newegg, Walmart and B&H 33.

DeeperThe detail

The one place Kingston names end customers is server memory, where it must: "Whether you require our Server Premier memory adhering to industry standards for your white-box server, or system-specific memory for servers branded by Cisco, Dell, HPE, or Lenovo, Kingston has you covered" 23. Making a part guaranteed to work in a named OEM’s machine requires naming the machine.

Its pitch to that market is the testing regime rather than the price: "Kingston invests millions in the development and testing of its modules today to reduce your total cost of ownership tomorrow", "100% testing is a cornerstone of Kingston’s commitment to delivering the most reliable server memory", and "All Kingston server memory is backed by a lifetime warranty" 23.

ExpertFor specialists

Beyond the channel, Kingston runs a contract-manufacturing business — "Our experience and resources enable benefits and savings to companies and top tier OEMs" 19 — and describes itself as "Trusted by leading PC manufacturers and global cloud providers" without naming any 4.

That is the pattern throughout: Kingston names the four server OEMs whose branded machines it builds specific parts for 23, and names no PC maker and no cloud provider anywhere in the material checked here 4 19. For a company with two thirds of its market, the customer list is almost entirely private.

12.Hidden in plain sight

SimpleStart here

Kingston holds about two thirds of its market and almost nobody outside the industry knows the name. Its own announcement of the 2024 rankings puts it at 66% share of a US$13.3 billion market, with second place on 5% 4.

DeeperThe detail

That is a degree of dominance rare in any industry, and Kingston has held the top position for 22 consecutive years 12 4. Its DRAM business manager’s comment on the 2024 result: "This milestone not only reaffirms Kingston’s dominant presence in the market but also underscores the company’s resilience and significance within the industry, as we now proudly maintain our top position for the 22nd consecutive year" 4.

The reason it goes unnoticed is structural. Kingston is private, so there are no earnings calls and no filings to report on 13. It names no chief executive, so there is no public figure 9. It publishes no prices and issues almost no news beyond product launches 26 25. A company can be enormous and invisible at the same time if it never has to talk.

ExpertFor specialists

A caution about the share numbers, because they look like a trend and are not one. Kingston’s own market share page reports "Kingston owned 78.12% of the DRAM market share in 2022" 8; its 2024 release reports 66% 4; and the 2025 ranking, which Kingston has not itself announced, is reported at a lower figure again in analyst coverage of TrendForce’s data 37. These come from different framings and different reporting years, and reading them as a single declining series would be misleading without that caveat 8 4.

The second hidden thing is the Softbank arithmetic. Kingston’s timeline records Softbank paying $1.5 billion for 80% in 1996 and the founders buying the same 80% back for $450 million in 1999 12. The company states both figures on the same page and never comments on the difference.

13.Weak spots

SimpleStart here
  • It cannot make a single chip. Every module depends on three suppliers Kingston does not control 1.
  • Private and opaque — no accounts, no revenue, no named chief executive 13 9.
  • Counterfeiting is a live problem, and Kingston publishes material on it 38 39.
  • Its share is measured by other people, and the figures are not comparable year to year 8 4.
DeeperThe detail

The supplier dependency is the structural one, and Kingston states it in the language of strength: "strong relationships with the world’s leading DRAM suppliers" and "a diverse supply base" 1. Both are true. But in a shortage the three makers allocate their own wafers first, and a module brand’s cost base is set by companies it cannot negotiate into building more capacity.

Counterfeiting is the risk Kingston documents most fully, because a brand whose value is trust is worth faking. It states plainly that Kingston is "not immune to counterfeiting", advises that "Purchasing Kingston products from authorized distributors and resellers will help ensure product authenticity", and describes the physical defences — "Phantom Technology" on SD cards and SSDs, and "color shift technology, in which the colors on the product label or imprint will change at different angles" on USB drives 38. It has "partnered with OpSec® to protect the trusted Kingston name" against "trademark abuse, counterfeit and grey market sales" 39, and the warranty excludes products not sold through authorised channels 21.

ExpertFor specialists

Kingston publishes no risk disclosure of any kind — being private, it has no obligation to — so every risk on this page is derived from its own operational and commercial documents rather than quoted from a company statement 13 21.

Two narrower gaps worth naming. Kingston publishes no fiscal year end and no financial data at all, so an outsider cannot measure its profitability, its inventory position or how the 2026 memory shortage has actually affected it 13 16. And its press archive is easy to misread: the press centre shows only the current year by default, with prior years reachable only by explicit URL and separate feeds per language 25 40. Any claim that Kingston has never said something needs checking across years and language feeds, not just the page that loads first.

14.India angle

SimpleStart here

Kingston has a named India office — the strongest India presence of any company documented in this section so far, though it is a representative one rather than a factory.

Its contacts page lists the "Kingston Technology India Liaison Office, India 407, Sunteck Crest Near International Airport, Plot No. 3, Next to Leela Business Park, Marol, Andheri (E) Mumbai, Maharashtra 400059" 11.

DeeperThe detail

The words Kingston chose matter. A liaison office is a specific legal form under India’s foreign-exchange rules, and one that may not carry out commercial or manufacturing activity — it exists to represent the parent, not to trade. So Kingston’s own description tells you the shape of its India presence: marketing and representation, not an Indian trading or manufacturing entity 11.

That still puts it ahead of the chip makers documented earlier, none of which lists an India office at all except Micron, whose Sanand plant does assembly and test. Kingston’s India footprint is commercial rather than industrial, but it is a footprint with an address 11.

ExpertFor specialists

Two limits on what can be said. Kingston distributes announcements into the Indian trade press, but the material found in this check is regional rather than India-specific — carrying a quote from its APAC vice-president rather than an India executive. And an older Kingston India marketing campaign is referenced in search results, but the release body now redirects to the current press index and could not be retrieved 25. So this page states the office and says nothing about India strategy, because nothing India-specific could be sourced 11.

Kingston’s ISO-certified sites are Fountain Valley, Taiwan, China, the United Kingdom and Ireland 30 32. India is not among them, and no Kingston manufacturing or investment in India was found in this check.

15.What’s next

SimpleStart here

Kingston publishes product news and very little else. Its 2026 releases cover encrypted drives, a 30.72TB data-centre SSD, embedded solutions, a system-builder partnership and the Forbes ranking 25; 2025 was a similar list 40.

DeeperThe detail

What is not in either year is striking. Kingston issued no press release in 2025 or 2026 about the memory shortage, DRAM or NAND pricing, or supply allocation — the defining event of its market and the one that determines what it pays for everything it sells 25 40. This check covered the main English feed for both years; Kingston also runs separate per-language feeds, so the claim is scoped to the feeds cited here 25 40.

The company’s only substantive comment on the squeeze came from a business manager on a podcast, reported by the trade press: Cameron Crandall, Kingston’s data-centre SSD business manager, advised buyers "I think the best thing to do if you’re looking at upgrading your system is to do it now and not wait because prices are going to continue to go up", adding "it will be more expensive 30 days from now, and more likely it will be more expensive 30 days after that", and saying prices were rising "like we’ve never seen" in his 29 years at the company 41. The same coverage reports NAND prices up 246% from the first quarter of 2025, with 70% of the rise in the preceding 60 days, and headlines the piece with his line "It’s bad, and it’s getting worse right now" 41.

Those are spoken remarks relayed by a journalist, not a company statement, and this page labels them accordingly 41.

ExpertFor specialists

The question Kingston’s next few years turn on is whether a buyer can hold two thirds of a market through a shortage it did not cause and cannot fix. Its position rests on scale, on 22 years of channel relationships, and on being qualified with all three chip makers at once 1 4. None of those helps if the chip makers simply have less to sell it — and one of them has already withdrawn its own consumer brand rather than serve that channel.

Module-market revenue rose 59% in 2025 on TrendForce’s figures, driven by the same crowding-out that constrains chip supply 37. A market growing that fast on price rather than volume is a good year for a module brand’s revenue and a hard one for its margins, and Kingston publishes nothing that would let an outsider tell which happened 13.
